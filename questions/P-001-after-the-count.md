# Ruling needed: how the prompt engine goes on, now that past records cannot show why work was sent back

Milestone: M1

WHY: The count could not settle the question your stop rule asks (whether the instructions are a meaningful cause of rework), because the records say what was wrong, rarely why. So, as the brief provides, it comes back to you. Going on, waiting or stopping is yours to decide. If you do not decide, nothing moves and no test is designed.

What the count found (checked by an independent Source checker, on a different AI model, that opens every cited record): about 1 plan in 20 passed its check first time, about 1 research memo in 4, and 2 pieces of built work in 5. Of 315 times work was sent back, 125 were classified, many by sampling. 41 of those have no findings recorded at all. Of the other 84, 61 name nothing beyond the fault itself. The 23 that name a cause point to: the check itself 10, missing or wrong material handed over 8, the worker's own slip 2, the instructions 1, how the work was split 1, the researcher's access to sources 1. The records almost never say which AI model did the work, and the briefs workers were given are mostly not recorded, so the count cannot say how much either one matters.

What the outside research suggests: in the researcher's opinion, instructions are "one lever among several, not the main one"; in its estimate, the AI model and the effort and context a session works with matter more on hard tasks, and instructions matter most where a hand-over is vague or leaves out requirements (research Q-002).

Whatever you choose, every agent's brief and AI model will be recorded across the model from now on, as you already ruled.

OPTIONS:
  A. **Go on now.** Choose a few changes to test (the "candidates") from the research and the causes the count did find: instructions, the material handed over, or the checks. Then test each against no change on the same past tasks (the "fair test"). *Gain:* the project keeps moving, and the fair test measures each change's effect directly. *Cost:* the candidates rest on indirect evidence, so they may miss the real causes. The test needs its budget (about 300 runs for three kinds of work, set by you at the Build gate). Two things are still unknown: whether past tasks can be replayed faithfully, and whether any change shows a gain the test can see. If none does, the project closes with "no change helps", as you accepted. *Reversible:* yes.
  B. **Wait for new work to show causes first.** Count causes over the next checked work, for example 30 pieces of each kind, now that briefs are recorded, then choose candidates. *Gain:* the candidates rest on evidence of what causes rework here. *Cost:* the project waits for that much new work, with no date, and then still needs the fair test and its runs. *Reversible:* yes.
  C. **Stop here.** Keep what is done (the library entries, the lessons, the brief-and-model record) and close. *Gain:* no more effort or tokens on this project. *Cost:* whether better instructions, inputs or checks cut rework stays unanswered. *Reversible:* yes; it can be started again.

RECOMMENDATION: A, with low-to-medium confidence. A fair test on the same tasks measures a change's effect whether or not past records show causes, and B's waiting has no end date. What would change it: if you would rather not spend test runs on candidates chosen from indirect evidence, B. If you judge the research's "one lever among several" reason enough to stop, C.
