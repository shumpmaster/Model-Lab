# Ruling needed: how the prompt engine goes on, now that past records cannot show why work was sent back

Milestone: M1

WHY: Your stop rule says the project comes back to you if the count of past work shows the instructions are not a meaningful cause of rework. The count could not show it either way: the records say what was wrong, rarely why. Going on, re-aiming or stopping is yours to decide. Until you rule, the fair test cannot be designed.

What the count found (checked by a Source checker on a different AI model): across the Service Desk, the operating model and Model-Lab, about 1 plan in 20 passed its check first time, about 1 research memo in 4, and 2 pieces of built work in 5. Of 84 sent-back pieces of work whose findings are recorded, 61 name no cause beyond the fault itself. Of the 23 that do: the check itself 10, missing or wrong material handed over 8, the instructions 1, how the work was split 1, the researcher's access to sources 1, and the worker's own slip 2. None names the AI model, and only 1 record of any kind blames a brief, without having seen it.

OPTIONS:
  A. **Go on, choosing what to test from the research and the few causes the records show, and start recording every brief now.** Candidate changes come from the outside research (it found instructions matter most for vague hand-overs and missing requirements) and from the causes the count did find (checks and missing material as well as instructions). The fair test then measures each change directly on past tasks, which does not need past causes. Meanwhile every agent's brief and AI model is recorded, so new work shows causes. *Gain:* the project keeps moving; the test answers whether a change helps, whatever past records lack. *Cost:* candidates are chosen with weaker evidence of what causes rework, so some may target the wrong thing; the test would then show "no difference". *Reversible:* yes.
  B. **Pause the testing until new work shows causes.** Record every brief and AI model first, then count causes over the next pieces of checked work (for example 30 of each kind) before choosing candidates. *Gain:* candidates rest on evidence of what causes rework here. *Cost:* the project waits for that much new work, across all projects, with no date; the brief-and-model record must be in place first. *Reversible:* yes.
  C. **Stop the project here.** Keep what is already done (the library entries, the lessons, and your ruling that every agent's brief and model is recorded, which goes into the operating model anyway) and close. *Gain:* no more effort or tokens on this project. *Cost:* the question whether better instructions cut rework stays unanswered. *Reversible:* yes; it could be started again later.

RECOMMENDATION: A, with medium confidence: the fair test measures a change's effect directly, so it does not depend on past records showing causes, and recording briefs from now on fills the gap for later. What would change it: if you would rather not test anything chosen on indirect evidence, B.
