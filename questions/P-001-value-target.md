# Ruling needed: how we will know the prompt engine worked, and when we stop

Milestone: Discovery-2

WHY: Every project must say from the start what it must improve, by how much, by when, and when it stops (the "Success defined" milestone). Setting this is yours alone: it cannot be delegated, and it never defaults. Until it is set, the fair test of the changes (the milestone "A fair test of the changes is ready") has no measure to aim at, and the brief cannot go to you for the Design gate. If you do not decide, the project waits here.

What you told us you want, in your words: "a combination of all but lower agent usage and less of my time", that is, fewer review rounds and better research, with the core goal "to minimize the gaps that might arise from traditional orchestrasted workflows for vibe coding, strategy, etc…".

**The measure used below.** Work goes to an independent check: a plan to its review, a research memo to its source check, built work to its reviewer. The **first-time pass rate** is the share that passes at the first try; each failed try costs a review round. Today's figures come from the Service Desk's records of work started from conversations (the only records long enough to count), and they cannot show *why* work failed: about 1 in 17 plans passed every check first time, 2 in 12 research memos, and about 15 in 50 pieces of built work. The milestone "The causes of rework are known" recounts these per kind of work before the Design gate; its figures replace these once done.

**How the test works.** The same past tasks are run twice, once with the agents' current instructions and once with a candidate change, and the two results are compared task by task (a "paired" test). With about 50 tasks per kind of work (about 100 runs), such a test catches a real 20-point gain about 7 times in 10, and a 10-point gain about 1 time in 4; a change that does nothing would be wrongly kept about 1 time in 40. Testing all three kinds of work at that strength takes about 300 runs; the 100 runs you allowed cover Discovery and Design, and the budget for the test itself is yours to set at the Build gate. With fewer runs, only larger gains can be seen.

OPTIONS:
  A. **First-time pass rate up by at least 20 points.** (1) *In the test:* a change is kept only if it raises the first-time pass rate of the kind of work it targets by at least 20 points over leaving things as they are. Checked when testing ends, before anything enters the operating model. (2) *In real use:* once kept changes are in the operating model, over the first 30 pieces of each kind of work checked under it, in any project, the first-time pass rate is at least 20 points above the recounted figure for that kind. Checked when 30 pieces of each kind are done. With 30 pieces, a real 20-point gain shows about 7 times in 10, and a false alarm about 1 time in 25. *Miss:* in the test, no change clears 20 points; in real use, a kept change falls short. *Gain:* one figure covers both fewer rounds and better research, and it matches what the test can see. *Cost:* real gains below 20 points are missed and those changes dropped; about 3 in 10 real 20-point gains are missed too. *Reversible:* yes; you can change the target later.
  B. **Rounds per piece of work down by at least one.** The same two checks, but the measure is the typical number of tries before a pass, which must fall by at least one round, in the test and over the first 30 pieces of each kind in real use. *Miss:* no change cuts a round in the test; in real use, a kept change does not. *Gain:* it is the measure in your own words, "fewer review rounds", and it counts every round, not only the first. *Cost:* each task in the test must be run until it passes, so each costs several runs, and fewer tasks fit in the same budget; how well it can detect a one-round fall has not been worked out yet. *Reversible:* yes.
  C. **Choose the measure now, set the number after the recount.** Use the first-time pass rate, with the same two checks, but set the size of the gain once the recount of past work is done, still before the Design gate. *Miss:* as in A, at the number then set. *Gain:* the number rests on the finished recount for each kind of work. *Cost:* the fair test cannot be designed until then, and this question comes back to you a second time. *Reversible:* yes.

**The date, under every option.** The target is checked at two events, not on calendar dates: when testing ends, and when 30 pieces of each kind have been checked in real use. Your earlier ruling set no fixed length for Discovery; it did not rule on this target's date, so choosing an option here also rules that the target has no calendar date. To set a calendar date instead, say so.

**Proposed for the miss and the stop rule, under every option** (proposals; your ruling makes them so):
  - **If no change passes the test:** we record "no change helps", the result you accepted in advance; we keep the record of each agent's brief and AI model (milestone "Every agent's brief and model on record"), put that into the operating model, and close the project.
  - **If a kept change falls short in real use:** it is removed at the operating model's next scheduled review of its rules, not adjusted to pass (the model's rule "Killed, not tuned").
  - **If fewer than 30 pieces of a kind are checked within six months of the change entering the operating model:** that kind is judged on what has been checked, and the result says how uncertain it is.
  - **Stop and come back to you** if the recount of past work shows the instructions are not a meaningful cause of rework for any kind of work: you rule whether to re-aim the project or close it.
  - **Stop and come back to you** if the 100 runs you allowed through Design are used up before the fair test is ready.
  - **Your time:** no cap, as you ruled; each status update shows the tokens used and remaining, as you asked.

RECOMMENDATION: A, with medium confidence. It measures both of your aims with one figure and matches what a test of about 100 runs per kind can see. What would change it: if you care more about the total number of rounds than about passing first time, B fits better; if you would rather not fix a number before the recount, C.
