# Ruling needed: your two approvals on every screened method, and what happens to one a trial cannot prove

Milestone: Discovery-2

WHY: Option B below would go outside a written rule of the operating model, "Killed, not tuned" (a change that misses its pre-set mark is removed, not kept or adjusted), and the experiment rule "No re-running for a better answer"; A and C stay inside them. This replaces part 3 of the value-target question, which you answered with a different design: "every suggestion needs a very tight review with approval from me. Effectively another gate." It sets how that gate works. It is part of the value target, so it is yours alone and never defaults; until it is ruled, the plan cannot say what happens after a trial.

Your words on how it should work: "the new ideas enter with a very plain English summary. Effectively just a very clear clean assessment of what the change would be, how we do it today, why we would potentially do it, and what is the impact & risks - based on the initial assessment. Then if I approve, it’s tested and tried, then the results come in a similar more detailed format à la the gate of which I approve or reject the final implementation to the model".

**Under every option, as you described:**
- **Entry card.** Each method found by the screening comes to you first as a short plain-English card: what the change is, how we do it today, why we might do it, and its impact and risks, from the first assessment (its one confirmed source, its conditions read against ours, and any outside code checked, with no data out, as you ruled). The card also carries the trial's plan, fixed from then on. Nothing is tried unless you approve it.
- **Results card.** After the trial, the results come to you in more detail, in the form of a gate: what was found, what it would change, the risks, the Critic's view, and a recommendation. Nothing is adopted into the operating model or the desk unless you approve it there, as you already ruled for the desk ("I dont want the desk to automatically get updated").
- **Nothing is dropped by the pipeline.** Every result, kept or not, comes to you on its results card.

**What a trial can show, at the 1 in 10 line you chose.** With 30 pieces each way, a method that truly halves how often work needs you clears the line about 7 times in 10 if half of pieces need you today, and about 4 in 10 if 3 in 10 do. With one planned second look (30 more each way, judged together), about 9 and 7 in 10. These are before the checks that work holds up, which turn away a harmless method about 1 time in 7 to 1 in 5. So many useful methods will come back "not shown" (the gain did not clear the line), which is not the same as "does not help".

OPTIONS:
  A. **Your two cards; a second look planned on the entry card.** The entry card's trial plan includes one second look if the first leans the right way. On a "not shown" result you may reject the method, or approve a new, truly different trial on a fresh entry card; you may not adopt it on that result. *Gain:* the most useful methods found (about 9 and 7 in 10), and every result stays inside the model's rules, so a kept method can enter the model. *Cost:* about 3 to 5 trials in 10 run twice as long, with twice the pieces and your touches.
  B. **As A, and you may also adopt on faith.** On a "not shown" result whose work held up, you may approve the method for use in this project, with a review after its next 30 pieces; it enters the operating model only after a trial shows a gain. *Gain:* the most room for your "faith"; a promising method is not lost to a small trial. *Cost:* it keeps a method that missed the line set before its trial, against "Killed, not tuned" and the plan's risk of adopting what is popular rather than what helps; with one-source entry (your answer to part 4), a method could be in use with neither two sources nor a shown gain.
  C. **Your two cards; one look only.** No second look; each "not shown" result comes to you to reject or to approve a new, truly different trial. *Gain:* trials are shorter and simpler. *Cost:* more useful methods come back "not shown" (about 7 and 4 in 10 found, against 9 and 7).

RECOMMENDATION: A, with medium confidence. It is your design, it finds the most useful methods, and every method you keep can go on into the operating model. If you would rather keep promising methods in use while they prove themselves, B; if shorter trials matter more than finding every useful method, C.
