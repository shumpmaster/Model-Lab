# Ruling needed: your two approvals on every screened method, and whether you may adopt one a trial did not prove

Milestone: Discovery-2

WHY: Options A and B below go outside a written rule of the operating model, "Killed, not tuned": "A design that fails its predeclared gate dies. It may be replaced by a genuinely different design (§5), never adjusted until it passes." C and D stay inside it. This card replaces part 3 of the value-target question, which you answered with a design of your own, because "An automated pipeline could end up killing a lot. I think ultimately every suggestion needs a very tight review with approval from me. Effectively another gate." It sets how that gate works. It is part of the value target, so it is yours alone and never defaults.

Your words on how it should work: "the new ideas enter with a very plain English summary. Effectively just a very clear clean assessment of what the change would be, how we do it today, why we would potentially do it, and what is the impact & risks - based on the initial assessment. Then if I approve, it’s tested and tried, then the results come in a similar more detailed format à la the gate of which I approve or reject the final implementation to the model".

**Words** (from the value-target question): a **piece** is a merged pull request that changes built work, a plan or research; a **trial** runs 30 pieces with the method and 30 without, chosen by coin toss; a method is **shown** when its pieces needed you less often by more than chance at the 1 in 10 line you chose, and **holds up** when its work does no worse on two checks (passing its first independent check, and not being reopened for a fault). **"Not shown"** is any other result.

**Under every option:**
- **Entry card (your design).** Each method comes to you first as a short plain-English card: what the change would be, how we do it today, why we might do it, and its impact and risks. Nothing is tried unless you approve it. *Added by the Chief of Staff:* the card also says what the first assessment rests on (its one confirmed source, its conditions read against ours, outside code checked with no data out) and carries the trial's plan, which is then fixed.
- **Results card (your design).** After the trial, the results come to you in more detail, like a gate card, and you approve or reject putting the method into the operating model. *Added:* it carries the Critic's view and a recommendation. Nothing enters the model or the desk without your approval.
- **Nothing is dropped by the pipeline.** Every result comes to you.

**What a trial can show at your 1 in 10 line**, before the hold-up checks: with one look, a method that truly halves how often work needs you is shown about 7 times in 10 if half of pieces need you today, and about 4 in 10 if 3 in 10 do. With a second look planned in advance (30 more each way if the first leaned the right way, that is, its pieces needed you less often; judged together, on a line set slightly stricter so useless methods are not shown more often), about 9 in 10 and 7 in 10. The hold-up checks turn away a harmless method about 1 time in 7 with one look, and up to 1 in 5 with two. So many useful methods will come back "not shown".

OPTIONS (these letters are new; they do not match the earlier part 3's):
  A. **You decide every result, as you worded it.** On any result, shown or not, you approve or reject putting the method into the operating model. The results card says plainly when a method was not shown or did not hold up. *Gain:* your approval decides, every time; nothing promising is lost to a small trial. *Cost:* methods with no shown gain can enter the operating model, against "Killed, not tuned" and the plan's first risk, adopting what is popular rather than what helps; with one-source entry, a method could be in the model with neither two sources nor a shown gain. *Reversible:* yes; a method in the model comes out by a model change, at its review date.
  B. **You decide every result; the model takes only shown ones.** On a "not shown" result that held up, you may approve the method for use in this project, reviewed after its next 30 pieces and removed then if it still is not shown. It enters the operating model only once a trial shows it. *Gain:* your approval keeps a promising method in use; the model takes only what was shown. *Cost:* the same departure as A within this project, and a method may be in use for months on faith. *Reversible:* yes; removed at its review or whenever you say.
  C. **You approve shown results; for the rest, reject or one new design.** On a "not shown" result, you may reject the method, or approve one genuinely different trial design on a fresh entry card; the Critic checks it is genuinely different, and a method that fails twice goes back to the screening. The second look is planned on the entry card, so it is one trial planned whole, not a re-run after seeing results. *Gain:* stays inside the rules, so every method kept can enter the model. *Cost:* your approval cannot keep a "not shown" method; about 3 to 5 trials in 10 run twice as long. *Reversible:* yes, for trials not yet started.
  D. **As C, one look only.** *Gain:* shorter, simpler trials. *Cost:* fewer useful methods shown (about 7 and 4 in 10, against 9 and 7). *Reversible:* yes, for trials not yet started.

Under every option a method that was shown but did not hold up is shown so on its results card; under C and D it is not adopted, under A and B you decide.

RECOMMENDATION: B, with medium confidence. It gives your approval the last word on every result, which is what you asked for, while the operating model takes only methods a trial has shown, so the model stays something that can be trusted. What would change it: if your judgement should carry into the model too, A; if you would rather stay wholly inside the model's rules, C, or D for shorter trials.
