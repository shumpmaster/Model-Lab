# Ruling needed: eight short questions that shape the screening's research

Milestone: Discovery-3

The screening means deliberately looking for, and judging, ways of building with AI that others report, to decide which (if any) the operating model should take up. Before more research, the map of what an expert would ask about it ends in eight questions only you can answer, each in a click or a sentence. Your answers are recorded in your own words and added to the project's intent; none is a final rule, and you can change any later. They decide what the research looks at next and what the plan proposes, and you rule the whole plan at the Design gate. Nothing here is irreversible.

WHY: the research and the plan depend on these answers, and the research must not guess your preferences: the Researcher is kept from seeing them, so they come from you directly (S-020, D-055).
OPTIONS: every question allows your own words, and "Later" (see the recommendation).
  1. Should the team deliberately look for and judge outside ways of building with AI, or rely on what already happens (model updates, the operating model's own review of its rules, and what you read yourself)?
     A. Look deliberately.
     B. Rely on what already happens.
  2. If it does look: once, on a regular schedule, or only when something prompts it?
     A. Once.
     B. On a schedule.
     C. Only when something prompts it.
  3. Which worries you more?
     A. Missing a change that would have helped.
     B. Adopting a change that turns out to hurt.
     C. Both equally.
  4. What makes a product "ready for real business use" for you? Pick any that apply, or say it in your words.
     A. You rely on it daily.
     B. Someone else uses it or pays for it.
     C. It passes a written checklist.
  5. When an outside change is adopted in the operating model, should it also reach the status page you already use, or only new work? (Today a product in use takes a method change only when you raise one.)
     A. Also the status page.
     B. Only new work.
     C. Case by case.
  6. May agents try outside tools, prompts or code? (Which of your data may go where is not yet written down.)
     A. No.
     B. Yes, but only ones that send none of your project data outside.
     C. Yes, with no such limit.
  7. The prompt engine, already under way, measures the agents' written instructions and aims to improve them, and its research already overlaps with the screening's in places. Should the two stay separate, or should one own every change to those instructions, wherever the idea came from?
     A. Stay separate.
     B. The prompt engine owns every such change.
     C. The screening owns them.
  8. Roughly how much of your time per month, and how much money, would you put into this? Your words, or "set it at the Design gate".
RECOMMENDATION: none on questions 1 to 8, since each is your preference. On the process, answer what you can now; for any you'd rather see more evidence on first, say "Later": the question then stays open, and the research covers its ground without being told how you lean.
WHO HAS CHECKED IT: the Critic, three rounds (reviews/P-002-groundwork-shortlist/critic.md); the questions come from the Researcher's groundwork map, and the Researcher is never shown your leanings (OPERATING_MODEL §5).
