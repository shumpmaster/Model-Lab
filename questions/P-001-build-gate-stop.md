# Ruling needed: how the two Build-gate cards go on after failing their third check

Milestone: Design-6

The two cards you will rule the Build gate with have failed their independent check three times, so they have not come to you. One asks you to move the project into Build and choose the test's size; the other settles the test's eleven terms. No rule limits how many times a card may be checked: the model's three-round limit is written for plans and built work. Three rounds were treated as the limit for these cards, as you asked, and you may instead rule that it does not apply to cards. An independent trace of the three checks found that of the last check's two faults, one was a misreading and the other is a fix of a sentence or two. It also found a fault in the plan itself: the plan gave its shortest total time, about 32 to 64 weeks, to an order of collecting work that cannot achieve it. The plan's writer has corrected that, and two smaller slips about how much you would have to do while the test runs; each correction passed its check. The trace's root cause is that the cards copy too much of a long plan, so each round's fixes added new claims to check. Nothing here cannot be undone, and the gate itself still comes to you whichever you choose.

WHY: the cards cannot come to you without passing their check, and accepting them despite a failed check is never an option; how they go on is yours to choose, since no grant lets anyone choose for you (Personal-Org-Operating-Model OPERATING_MODEL.md §4 "Stop rule" and §2 "Delegation grants"; docs/LEDGER.md; intake/P-001-owner-instruction-2026-11-04.md).
OPTIONS:
  A. One focused check of named fixes — What happens: the Chief of Staff changes only these parts of the cards. Where they say a failure "comes to you first", they name the four checks of the running that put a question to you, and say the wake messages simply fall to you. Each size's chance says it is for built work, and the 100-piece size says plans stay at about 4 in 5. The term on the order of collecting is rewritten to the plan's corrected question, with each order's true weeks, and its recommended answer is looked at again, since the 32 to 64 weeks it rested on belong to an order that goes further beyond your ruling; the gate card's own 32-to-64 figure is labelled the same way. The term on your part adds the Orchestrator's wake fallback, as the corrected plan states it. One check in a fresh session reads only those parts and a comparison showing nothing else changed; any claim outside them fails it, and a failure comes back to you the same way (triage/P-001-build-gate-round-3.md, "One focused confirmation review"). Gain: the least rewriting. Cost: the rewritten order term is new text, the part most likely to fail, and may change what that term recommends; the cards stay as long as they are, so the root cause stays; the last check's other, non-blocking points stay open. Rules out: nothing.
  B. Cut the cards down to the decisions — What happens: the Chief of Staff redrafts both cards in the decision-card form, two or three options and a short background. The gate card gives the sizes, hold, recycle and kill, and points to the plan's tables for runs, weeks and your part instead of copying them. The terms card argues only the terms that touch your earlier rulings, the value target or anything that cannot be undone, and lists the rest with the plan's proposed answer; you still answer every term, none is decided for you (triage/P-001-build-gate-round-3.md, "Re-scope"; OPERATING_MODEL.md §2 "Decision cards"). Gain: far fewer claims that can be wrong, which is the root cause. Cost: more checks before the gate reaches you, how many no rule says (the Chief of Staff would count from a first round, as the plan rule does for a rewritten plan, which is an assumption, not a rule); you read the figures in the plan rather than on the cards. Rules out: nothing.
  C. Redraft both cards in full — What happens: both cards are written again from scratch at their present length and checked as new cards, with the same unset count as B (triage/P-001-build-gate-round-3.md, "Re-specify"). Gain: a fresh start. Cost: it rebuilds the same long text that failed three times, and reopens points that passed. Rules out: nothing.
  D. Rule that the limit does not apply to cards — What happens: the Chief of Staff fixes every point the last check raised, blocking or not, and the plan's corrected figures, and the cards get a plain fourth check of their whole text; if it fails, they are fixed and checked again, with no set limit unless you name one. Gain: every open point is fixed and the whole of both cards is checked. Cost: nothing stops the pattern the trace found, each fix adding new faults, from going on; and your ruling becomes the only record of how the limit is read for cards. Rules out: nothing.
  Dropping the cards is not offered: the gate and the plan's freeze need your answers to the plan's open questions (specs/S-001.md, "Open questions for the owner" and "Needed outside this spec's lane"); ending the test is the gate's own "kill", which stays on the gate card whatever you choose.
RECOMMENDATION: A, with medium confidence (the trace's reading of the evidence, and the Chief of Staff's): the last check's two blocking faults can be fixed inside the cards, the one figure that needed the plan is now corrected there, and limiting the check to named parts stops new faults slipping in elsewhere. What would change it: a focused check that again finds new faults, or preferring to cure the root cause over speed (then B); or wanting every open point fixed now and reading the limit as not covering cards (then D).
WHO HAS CHECKED IT: the Critic, on a different AI model from the writer: round 1 failed this card on how it put the limit and the options (record: reviews/P-001-build-gate-stop/critic.md).
