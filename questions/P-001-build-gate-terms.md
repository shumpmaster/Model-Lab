# Ruling needed: the fair test's terms, settled before its plan freezes

Milestone: Design-6

The fair test's plan leaves eleven terms for you to settle at the Build gate, before it freezes; together they carry every part of the plan's nine open questions except the budget and size, which are on the gate card beside this. Two touch your own earlier rulings, and the recommended answer reads them in a way you may not have meant, so both say so. One touches your value target's wording, which only you can change. One, letting copies of private repositories' work into public Model-Lab, could not be undone. The plan cannot freeze until every term is answered.

WHY: the plan's open questions must be ruled before the freeze, and the value target and anything irreversible are yours alone (specs/S-001.md, "Open questions for the owner"; Personal-Org-Operating-Model OPERATING_MODEL.md §2).
OPTIONS:
  1. Wording of the value target and of the fair-test milestone (plan question 1, 2). Both still say "on the same past tasks", which the test cannot use. A (recommended): reword both to "a paired test on new work, each piece done or checked both with and without the change from the same starting point over the same period", as the plan proposes, and restate the "about 300 runs" line with the plan's figures. Gain: the target matches the test. Cost: none beyond the change. B: keep the words. Cost: the test as planned would not meet its own target's wording.
  2. Collecting the kinds (plan question 4). A (recommended; it reads your ruling "every kind, incrementally" as allowing this): the next kind's pieces start collecting while the previous kind is judged and analysed, still analysed one kind after another. Gain: about 32 to 64 weeks for the full test instead of 60 to 121. Cost: more running at once for you to follow. B: strictly one kind after another, the literal reading of the ruling. Cost: the longer wait. C: all three at once (this goes against "incrementally"). Gain: the shortest wait. Cost: the most at once, and nothing learned from one kind before the next.
  3. A time limit (plan question 3). A (recommended): no fixed limit; each kind has review points 16 weeks after it opens and at each token checkpoint, where you may stop it on counts alone. Gain: the test can reach its size. Cost: it may run for over a year. B: a firm limit you name. Gain: a known end. Cost: a kind stopped early can show less; the plan gives the power at each stopping size.
  4. Testing research at all (plan question 3). The change tested on research aims at a cause worth at most about 11 to 16 points, so "not shown" is the likely result even if it helps. A (recommended; it follows "every kind"): test research last, as planned. Gain: an answer for every kind. Cost: about 200 runs and 32 to 64 weeks for research alone. B: leave research until the built-work and plan results are in. Gain: those runs saved or deferred. Cost: research may never be tested.
  5. Recording each piece at dispatch (plan question 5, first part). Plans and built work can only enter if each piece's brief, start commit and model are recorded when it is dispatched; today none is. A (recommended): the Chief of Staff records them for the Service Desk's and Model-Lab's work until the brief-and-model record is built, and Model-Lab's brief records gain a start-commit field. Gain: plans and built work can enter. Cost: a record at each dispatch. B: no recording. Cost: only research can enter.
  6. The secret side's reading lists (plan question 5, second part). A (recommended): approve them, by your verdict and merge on the pull request that adds them, which an independent Reviewer has passed. Gain: the secret sheets can be written. B: decline. Cost: the secret sheets cannot be written, so the test does not start.
  7. The judges' AI provider (plan question 6). A (recommended): judges stay within your AI plan (Fable 5.1), with the stated limit that every judge comes from the same provider as the work, so a bias they share would not show. Gain: no spend beyond your plan. B: override your spend ceiling for an outside-provider judge on your sample cards only. Gain: one judgement from outside the provider. Cost: spend beyond your plan, not estimated.
  8. Research's false-pass tolerance (plan question 7). A false pass is a memo the source check passes although the secret check finds a fault. A (recommended): accept the plan's rule that the changed check may have no more than one false pass more than the unchanged one. Gain: one unlucky memo does not sink the change. Cost: one more unverified claim could pass. B: a stricter rule you name, such as none. Gain: no extra false pass. Cost: a single memo could reject the change.
  9. Judging sessions (plan question 8, first part). Your ruling said "the sheet writer and each judging in their own sessions". A (recommended; it reads that as one judging session per kind, each judge a helper inside it, which may not be what you meant): you start 9 sessions for the full test. Gain: the least for you to do. Cost: judges share their session's machine, kept apart by their working folders and a check of what they opened. B: one session per judging, the plainer reading: about 336 sessions for you to start for the full test, or fewer if the judging session starts them with tools no record has tested. Gain: each judge fully apart. Cost: your time, or untested tools.
  10. Your part, and the fallbacks (plan question 8, second part). Per kind you start three sessions, pass short records and say "go on" at each checkpoint; if a first-use check fails, the plan's fallbacks bring it to you, and your part can grow to the ranges the gate card gives. A (recommended): accept this. B: say what you would change; the plan would then be revised before it freezes.
  11. Copies of private repositories' work (plan question 9). Each piece's start tree is copied into public Model-Lab for judging. A (recommended): only pieces from public repositories enter (today the Service Desk and Model-Lab); the headline weeks do not change. Gain: nothing private is published. Cost: the operating model's own work is not tested. B: pieces from the private operating-model repository enter too. Gain: its work is tested. Cost: its tree at each piece's start becomes public, for good.
