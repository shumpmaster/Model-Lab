**Q-012 (wider than map item 3)**

1. BLOCKING, "The question", lines 5-6. The two added options are not put evenly beside the others.
   - One-off, scheduled and event-started reviews each get a few words. Standing intake gets a long clause: "feeds or alerts … people or automated agents reading it, deciding what deserves research, and proposing what to adopt or decline." That is a full design of one option, and it reads as a description of a pipeline someone intends to build.
   - Only automation is asked "with what went wrong". The other options get cost, catch and miss, and checking. That invites a failures-only search for automation and a neutral one for the rest. It should be symmetric: what worked, what failed, with what evidence.
   - "Any attempt to automate the reading, triage or research steps" is the only option that gets an explicit instruction to search for it. Nothing asks for evidence on human-only intake.
2. BLOCKING, "What each answer would change", lines 15-16. Each branch states only one direction.
   - "A short shelf life argues for repeated or continuous intake and against one-off review" has no branch for a long shelf life. A long one would argue for the opposite.
   - The automation branch only covers "fails in known ways". It has no branch for "works".
   - This signals that short shelf life and automation failure are the expected findings, and it favours continuous intake.
3. Non-blocking, "The decision it serves", line 12. "How much of it may be automated" presumes some automation. The map's item 3 decision included "or not done"; this one drops it, so the option of no screening is lost. Also, the decision is the only one that bundles three decisions (cadence, automation, shelf life) behind one question, which is too wide for one depth-3 memo.
