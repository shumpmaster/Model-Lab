## Round 1
Verdict: FAIL

I read the question the way a Researcher would. I checked PROJECT.md sections 6 and 7 only to confirm the "decision it serves" claims. Those citations hold: V2 and the starting figures, U5, U1, and the stop rule all say what the question claims. The neutral-drafting checklist from LL-02 is mostly met. "Whether" comes before "how", there is an out-of-scope line, there is no recommendation, and the Q-006 figures are not quoted, so they don't anchor the Researcher. The problems are gaps, plus some wording that leans.

1. Blocking: "avoidable" is never asked for. The decision section needs the share of *avoidable* returns the instructions cause (U1 and the stop rule). The question body only asks for returns to be classified by cause, with no avoidable or unavoidable marking. "the check catching a real fault in the work" is listed as a cause with no statement of whether it counts as avoidable. A Researcher cannot answer U1 from the question as written. Either the question asks for an avoidable marking with a stated meaning, or the decision section drops the word.

2. Blocking: the cause classes overlap and there is no rule for a return with several causes. "missing or wrong material handed over" overlaps "the written instructions (role file or brief)", because a brief that omits something is both. "how the work was split" overlaps with the brief too. The question gives no rule for the primary cause, for multiple causes, or for a tie. Different Researchers would count differently, and any split between instructions and other causes would then depend on the Researcher's choice of rule, not on the records. This is exactly the split the stop rule depends on.

3. Blocking: "in a stated sample" has no selection rule and no per-kind requirement. Nothing says how the sample is drawn, how large it is, or that it must cover every kind. U5 asks for the share of returns with a shown cause, per kind. With a free choice of sample, the cause counts can be steered, even by accident. The question should require either all returns or a stated drawing rule, with the sample size and the denominator given per kind.

4. Non-blocking: no denominator or missing-records requirement. "how many pieces passed at the first try" needs the total pieces per kind. The records have known holes: PROJECT.md R7 lists dropped work, about a third of dispatches, and anything after 8 October. The question does not ask the memo to state which pieces have no check record or are excluded, or what "passed at the first try" means. Examples are a different checker, a later-added check, or a piece with no recorded verdict. "Say plainly where the records cannot show a cause" covers causes only, not the counts.

5. Non-blocking: leaning towards instructions in the surrounding sections. The question body never says "not the limiting factor". The only such branch is in the third section, as "If the instructions cause few or none of the avoidable returns...". There the instructions are the only cause named, and the project's fate hangs on it. Naming the project's stake lets the Researcher infer the hoped-for answer (D-055). In the body, "the written instructions (role file or brief)" gets a parenthetical and the other classes do not. I suggest two changes. Put an explicit line in the body to report every class on equal terms, including that no class may dominate. Phrase the consequences for every class, not only the one that stops the project.

6. Non-blocking: location of Q-006-memo.md is not named. "Earlier counts exist in research/Q-006-memo.md" does not say which repository holds it, though the other records are listed by repository. A Researcher may not be able to find it from the named records.

7. Non-blocking: "few or none" and "meaningful" have no threshold. Section 3 and the stop rule use these words. The Researcher only counts, so this is fine, but the memo should be told to report counts per kind and leave the "meaningful" ruling to the owner.

## Round 2
Verdict: PASS

I re-read the whole of /home/user/wt/ml-orch/research/Q-008.md against the same standard.

Per finding:

1. "Avoidable" was never asked for. Resolved. Item 3 now asks for an avoidable / not avoidable / cannot-say mark on each return, with the test stated. The decision section's "avoidable returns" (U1) now matches what the body asks for.

2. Cause classes overlap and there is no primary-cause rule. Resolved. Item 4 asks for every supported contributing cause, judged on the same terms. It then asks for one primary cause by a rule stated before classifying, and for ties to be handled. It also covers the brief-that-failed-to-pass-on-material case under both causes.

3. The sample is unstated. Resolved. Item 2 asks for every return, or a sample drawn by a rule fixed before looking, with the sample size and the total it came from, per kind.

4. No denominator or missing-records requirement. Resolved. Item 1 asks for the definition of "passed at the first try" before counting. It also asks per kind for the pieces left out and why, and item 5 asks for the pieces with no check record and the returns with no recorded cause.

5. Leaning towards the instructions. Resolved. The cause list is now balanced, and each cause carries its own parenthetical. The line "Report every figure per kind and every cause on equal terms; whether any cause is large or small is not for this memo to judge" is in the body. The consequences section now says the causes found, "whichever they are", decide where changes are looked for, and the owner decides to go on, re-aim or close. The "not the limiting factor" outcome is no longer a single branch tied to the instructions only. It is covered by "re-aimed at other causes". I would have liked it spelled out, but it is not blocking.

6. Location of Q-006-memo.md. Resolved. It now reads "Model-Lab's research/Q-006-memo.md".

7. "Few or none" and "meaningful" have no threshold. Resolved. The memo is told not to judge whether a cause is large or small, and the "meaningful" call is left to the owner. The decision section's citations (section 6, U5, U1) still match PROJECT.md.

New findings:

8. Non-blocking: classifying every return, with an avoidable mark, several causes and a primary cause for each, is a large task at depth 3. The sample fallback in item 2 covers this, so I do not count it as a fault.

9. Non-blocking: item 3's avoidable test describes only "a real fault the work should not have had". Where the check itself was wrong, the Researcher has only the "could not have avoided" or "cannot say" marks. This is workable but could be stated. It is a note, not a required change.

Nothing new is blocking.
