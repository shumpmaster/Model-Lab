## Round 1

The round's verdict was FAIL.

The form is sound and the coverage is broad. Three wordings presume the owner's wanted outcome, and those need fixing before this goes out.

1. BLOCKING (neutrality), line 5, "screening outside practice ... and carrying the practices that hold up into a written operating method".
   - The title assumes the screening exists.
   - It assumes some practices will "hold up".
   - It assumes they will then be carried into the method.
   - Q-001's title names only the ground. This one names the intended pipeline, which signals the owner's aim.
   - Line 9 does say "whether and how to set up such a screening", but the title and line 7 contradict that openness.

2. BLOCKING (neutrality), line 16, "what reaching a product already in use (such as the status desk) involves once a change is adopted in the method".
   - It takes for granted that adopted changes should reach the desk.
   - It never asks whether they should, or what the cost, risk or alternative is (for example new projects only, or leaving existing products alone).
   - This is the "reach the desk" signal you asked about. Reword it so it asks whether, when and how, with the options open.

3. BLOCKING (neutrality and gap), lines 9 and 13, with no baseline anywhere.
   - "how often a screening is run" and "how long a finding stays valid" assume a screening is run, and a cadence is implied.
   - Q-001 required comparison "against ... leaving things as they are". Q-009 has no equivalent.
   - Add the do-nothing option: what the method gains or loses by not screening, and by relying on ordinary model and vendor updates or the owner's own reading.
   - Line 13 does ask "one-off or standing", but it excludes "none".

4. Non-blocking (neutrality), line 7. "vibe coding" is named as the one informal style. It is a loaded term and the only example of its kind. It favours that source type and invites a view of it. Either drop it or cover informal and formal practice evenly.

5. Non-blocking (neutrality), lines 11 and 19. The source list is fine. The risk list (line 19) is risks only, with no matching "what could go right or be missed". The risks are all about adopting too much, such as popularity, stakes, speed and false passes, and none are about missing something. Add the cost of not adopting, such as falling behind or missing a real improvement.

6. Non-blocking (gap). An expert would also expect these areas:
   - Security, licence, data-leak and supply-chain risk in adopting outside tools or prompts, including injected instructions arriving through screened material.
   - Source contamination, meaning that much "practice" content is itself AI-written, duplicated or promotional.
   - Rollback and recording of an adopted change. The tail of line 15 gestures at this but does not ask it.
   - Who does the screening, which is agents, human or both, and the checker for it. Line 17 touches the cost only.

7. Non-blocking (plainness), lines 5, 7, 15, 17 and 22.
   - The Researcher knows nothing of the project, and several terms are undefined.
   - "business-ready" is in quotes with no meaning given and could be read as the owner's own standard. Line 9 does ask what it means.
   - "screening", "the desk" and "status desk" (one phrase, "an internal status desk", with no description) are not explained.
   - "two such efforts" (line 15) refers to a second effort the Researcher has never been told about.
   - "this project" and "Discovery" (line 22) are unexplained. Q-001 shares the last one, so it passes for form, but it still reads as jargon.
   - Give one plain sentence each for screening, the desk and the second effort.

8. Form: the question, decision served, what each answer changes, topics and depth are all present. They match Q-001 and the groundwork form in OPERATING_MODEL §4 and §6. Nothing blocking here. The depth line (3, "groundwork map; known items graded and sourced") matches Q-001.

9. Scope, non-blocking. Eleven bullets cover two distinct things: the screening itself, and the method's absorption and measurement of changes. The map will be broad. That is acceptable for a groundwork map, but the overlap bullet (line 15) is the only link between them.

## Round 2

The round's verdict was PASS.

Round 2 fixes hold. The title (line 5) now says "found, judged and adopted, or not adopted". Line 7 defines screening as "any deliberate way of finding and judging such practice; whether to have one at all is part of the question". The baseline is the first bullet (line 10). It covers no screening, ordinary vendor and model updates, the method's own rule review and the owner's reading. It counts the cost of missing a real improvement as well as the cost of adopting a bad one. Cadence is open too: line 14 reads "one-off, repeated or not done". Line 18 offers "or apply only to new work" as an alternative to reaching the product in use. The risks are named on both sides (line 21), and other fields' failures are asked for (line 22). I found no phrase that signals a hoped-for answer, a recommendation or an owner leaning. The form matches Q-001: the same four headings, a topics line and a depth line, plus the same pointers to the pipeline's documents. Wording is plain enough for an outsider. Jargon such as "supply-chain compromise" and "technology radars" comes with context or examples.

1. Line 7, non-blocking. "A separate effort is already measuring whether changing the written instructions ... reduces how often their work is sent back." This states the other effort's hypothesis as if it were its purpose, and "reduces" leans toward a positive result. "Whether changing ... affects how often" would be neutral. It is context only and the Researcher is not asked to judge it.

2. Line 11, non-blocking. The bullet on what "ready for real business use" means for AI-built software is not obviously part of finding and adopting outside practice. A Researcher who knows nothing of the project may not see why it is here, and it could widen the map into a general topic. A short clause linking it to screening would help, such as what the screening would be judged against. This is a clarity point, not a neutrality fault.

3. Whole question, non-blocking. The question has 13 bullets at depth 3 (Q-001 had 11), and several are separate topics (cost, risk, other fields, product reach). A Researcher can still cover it as a map, but the Chief of Staff should expect thin treatment of some bullets. Consider telling the Researcher that breadth takes priority over depth where the two conflict.

4. Gap check, non-blocking. Nothing essential is missing. Two small additions would be allowed but are not needed: what a "bad adoption" looks like in practice, and whether the owner's own reading is already a form of screening. Bullet 1 covers the second implicitly.

No blocking findings.
