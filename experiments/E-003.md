**The question.** For each kind of work, does one candidate change raise the first-time pass rate by enough that chance alone would give the gain at most 1 time in 40 (V1), when each new piece of work is done, or checked, both with and without the change, from the same starting point, at the same time?

## Who does what
- **The Check author** writes each kind's held-back check before that kind opens (Method, step 1). It is given experiments/E-003/check-author-extract.md and the existing check standards that file names, and nothing else of S-001 or of this registration. It never sees the change texts (experiments/E-003/C*.md), S-001, this file, or any arm's output.
- **The Builder** builds the harness (src/E-003/**, tests/E-003/**) and runs the test exactly as registered: it makes the copies, applies the change text, starts every helper session, and records every run. It may not re-run anything for a better answer; the only reruns are those defined below.
- **The owner** looks at a small sample (Method, step 5), through cards the Chief of Staff prepares, and rules at each review point (Sample).
- **The Reviewer**, which did not run it, analyses the results against the thresholds below; the Orchestrator records its verdict verbatim.

## Hypothesis
Each change raises its kind's first-time pass rate over leaving things as they are:
- **C1, plans:** the writer reads the role file of the agent that will check its plan (experiments/E-003/C1-plans-read-the-check.md).
- **C2, built work:** the builder is handed the inputs that bear on its task by name (experiments/E-003/C2-built-named-inputs.md).
- **C3, research:** the source check returns a memo only for a fault it can show, not for a source it could not open (experiments/E-003/C3-research-check-what-it-can-open.md).

"No change helps" is an accepted result for each (decisions/questions/P-001-no-change-result.md). The test is sized for a 20-point gain; a smaller real gain will usually read "not shown" (S-001, "What the test can and cannot show").

## Method

### Step 1 — the held-back checks (before each kind opens)
- For each kind, the Check author writes one held-back check: a short list of pass/fail tests, each naming a fault that sends the work back, written from that kind's existing check standard at the time (the plan check's, build review's or source check's role file, and the project's playbook), as experiments/E-003/check-author-extract.md sets out.
- Each is stored outside every repository, as governance/HOLDOUTS.md says, and only its fingerprint is recorded there. No arm's copy holds it; no doer or checker under test sees it.
- Writing each is one run. A held-back check, once fingerprinted, is never edited during the comparison for its kind.

### Step 2 — which pieces enter
A **new piece** is one unit of work put to its independent check (research/Q-008-memo.md §1.1): a plan's round-1 draft, a built stage's round-1 submission, or a memo's round-1 submission, which meets all of these:
- its task is dispatched after its kind's comparison opens, in a participating project (S-001, AC2);
- for plans and built work: the worker's brief text, the commit it started from and the AI model it ran on are on record at dispatch;
- for research: the memo's round-1 submission commit, **and** the source check's brief text, the commit the check started from and the AI model it ran on, are on record at the check's dispatch (C3 acts on the check's brief, so the check's record is the one the arms need);
- "on record" means a record written at dispatch, before the piece's round-1 verdict: the brief-and-model record (M3) once it exists; the Service Desk's control files (reviews/<item>/_control/*.json, which hold `brief`, `model` and `source_tip`) for its scripted work; or the Chief of Staff's brief record, as Model-Lab's docs/briefs/ holds them now;
- it is of the kind under comparison;
- it is not part of this test (S-001's harness, its results, or a change being carried into the operating model).

Each eligible piece enters in dispatch order until the kind's number of pairs is reached or the owner stops the kind at a review point (Sample).

### Step 3 — the pair
- **Plans and built work (C1, C2): two doer arms per piece.** The harness makes two working copies of the piece's start commit as E-001 step 2.1 does (a fresh repository holding that commit and its ancestors only, checked the same way). In one copy the brief is the recorded brief; in the other, the recorded brief with the change paragraph added. Both get the same confinement paragraph (E-001), with the copy's path. Both run on the same AI model, the model the operating model uses for that role when the kind's comparison opens, recorded with every run; both start within the same hour and within 7 days of the real dispatch.
- **Research (C3): one memo, two checks.** The memo is the real researcher's round-1 submission. The harness makes two copies of the commit the real check started from, and starts two source checks on the check's recorded model, one with the check's recorded brief and one with C3 added, within the same hour. Source checks may use the web, as the real check does; starting both together keeps the web the same for both as far as can be done.
- **Which arm is which** is hidden from every judge and from the owner: each arm's output gets a neutral label from a random draw whose seed is written into the result before the comparison opens.
- **Nothing an arm writes leaves its copy.** No arm's output is merged into, pushed to or copied into any real repository. The real work goes on as it would have, unaffected.
- **No one sees outcomes early.** Verdicts are written to the kind's result file as pairs are judged; status reports and review points carry counts only, until the kind closes.

### Step 4 — judging
- **Plans and built work: two judge sessions per pair.** Each arm's deliverable is judged by its own judge session, on Fable 5.1, a different model from the doers' 5.5 line within the one provider the plan allows (docs/PROJECT.md section 8; the environment check). The judge's brief is E-002's judge-brief template, with "Judge it against" naming the task statement, and the kind's held-back check given in full. The judge sees the deliverable (the files or commit range in a fresh copy), never the arm's hand-back, which could name the change. For built work, the harness also runs the project's locked checks for that stage in the copy, mechanically; an arm passes only if the judge returns PASS and the locked checks pass.
- **Research: one judge session per memo.** The two arms are the two check versions' verdicts on the same memo, so there is one deliverable to judge: one judge session on Fable 5.1 judges the memo against the research held-back check, opening its sources. Each check version's verdict is then read with it (Measure).
- **The judge brief adds** (finding 4 of reviews/E-001/reviewer.md): "Run every command in the foreground and wait for it to end. Do not start background tasks. Your last message must begin with the verdict line."

### Step 5 — the owner's sample (after every pair of the kind is judged, before the analysis)
- **A card is one output**, never a pair: for plans and built work one arm's deliverable; for research one memo. It shows the task in one line, the output's path, and the judge's verdict with its blocking findings and cited lines. It shows no arm label, and for research not the two check versions' verdicts. The question: "Should this work have gone back to its worker? Yes / No / Can't tell." **Yes** is FAIL, **No** is PASS, **Can't tell** is no answer.
- **What can be drawn.** Outputs are sorted into three groups (for built work, outputs whose locked checks failed are left out of all three, since no answer can pass them):
  - **G1, outputs that decide:** both outputs of every pair whose arms' verdicts differ (research: every memo on which the two check versions' counted verdicts differ);
  - **G2, passed:** outputs the judge passed, in pairs where both arms pass (research: memos both versions' counted verdicts pass);
  - **G3, failed:** outputs the judge failed, in pairs where both arms fail (research: memos both versions fail).
- **How many.** 8 cards: 4 from G1, 2 from G2, 2 from G3. A group with too few outputs gives what it has, and the shortfall is drawn from G1, then G3, then G2. So a judge that wrongly passes, wrongly fails, or wrongly splits pairs can each be seen; for plans, where both arms may rarely pass, the G2 cards move to the other groups.
- **The draw.** Each group lists its outputs in the dispatch order of their pieces, and within a pair by neutral label. One generator, Python's `random.Random(seed)` with the kind's seed written into the result before the kind opened, draws `sample(G1, k1)`, then `sample(G2, k2)`, then `sample(G3, k3)`, then any further cards one at a time with `choice` from what is left, in the order G1, G3, G2.
- **"Can't tell."** If fewer than 6 of the 8 cards are answered Yes or No, further cards are drawn, one at a time, until 6 are answered or 12 cards have been shown in all.
- **What an answer does.** It replaces the judge's verdict for that output. Each pair's class (n10, n01 or concordant) is then worked out again from its two outputs' verdicts; for research, each check version's counted pass is worked out again from the new verdict on the memo. Unanswered cards and undrawn outputs keep the judge's verdict.
- **The owner's time:** about five minutes a card, 8 cards (about 40 minutes) per kind as a rule, 12 at most.

## Measure
- **Plans and built work.** For each pair: n10 if the change arm passes and the no-change arm fails; n01 if the reverse; otherwise concordant. First-time pass rate per arm = passes / pairs. Gain = (n10 − n01) / pairs.
- **Research.** For each memo, a check version's verdict counts as a pass only when the held-back check also passes the memo ("a pass the held-back check does not confirm is not a pass"). n10, n01 and the gain are then as above. Reported beside it: **false passes**, memos a check version passes that the held-back check fails, for each version.
- **The judge's standing.** On the owner's cards: how many were answered, and on how many answered cards the owner disagreed with the judge, by group.
- **What the measure is.** The deciding pass is the held-back check's, judged on a different model: a stand-in for V1's first-time pass at the project's real check. The link between the two is not tested here.
- **Reported, not deciding:** each pair's real round-1 verdict, beside the arms' verdicts (how often the held-back judge agrees with the real check is reported, not tested); tokens, tool uses and time per run as each helper reports at return; the served model of every run; pieces left out, with their reasons.

## Sample
**Pairs per kind.** The owner sets it at the Build gate from S-001's options (40, 60 or 100 pairs); this registration is frozen with the number the owner sets, before any run. S-001 recommends 60.

**Review points, not a calendar limit.** No time limit is set by default (decisions/questions/P-001-time-and-limit.md ruled no fixed length for Discovery; S-001, open question 3, asks the owner for Build). Each kind has a review point 16 weeks after it opens, and the owner's token checkpoints (every 20 runs) are review points too. At each, the owner is shown counts only: pairs collected and left out, runs and tokens used, the power at the pairs collected (experiments/E-003/sizing.py), and the weeks to the target at the pace so far. The owner rules "go on" or "stop this kind now". A stopped kind is analysed on the pairs it has, with its power at that size stated. The stop is never by results: no one is shown an outcome before the kind closes.

**Order.** One kind after another (decisions/questions/P-001-worst-work.md), in the order the owner sets (S-001 recommends built work, then plans, then research). A kind's comparison opens when the previous kind's last pair is judged, unless the owner rules that the next kind may start collecting earlier (S-001, open question 4); kinds are still analysed one after another.

**Runs.**
- One run is one helper session started for this test, whatever it does; a session that errors still counts. The Builder's own session, its scripts and the real work are not runs; nor are the owner's cards.
- Per pair: plans and built work 4 runs (2 doers, 2 judges); research 3 runs (2 checks, 1 judge). Per kind: 1 run for its held-back check.
- **Reruns.** (a) A judge that ends without a verdict line gets, in the same session, one fixed message, the same for every judge: "Return your verdict now. First line: `Verdict: PASS` or `Verdict: FAIL`." If it still gives none, one new judge session is started with the same brief (1 run). If that gives none, the pair is left out and listed. (b) A set-up fault (a wrong commit fetched, a copy built wrongly, a brief that differs between the arms other than by the change text) found before judging: the pair is run again once, both arms (2 runs, or 2 checks for research). Nothing else is rerun.
- **Reserve:** 10% of a kind's planned runs, rounded up, for reruns. When the reserve is spent, the next pair needing a rerun is left out and listed.
- **Hard cap per kind:** the planned runs plus the reserve, counted as started. The Builder reports runs and tokens used to the Chief of Staff at every 20 runs, for the owner's token checkpoints (decisions/questions/P-001-test-budget.md).

## Exclusions
Each is listed in the result with its piece and reason; none is re-tried for a better answer.
1. **In-flight inputs** (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md, finding 8). A pair is left out when, between the start commit and the real work's round-1 submission, any file the brief or the task statement names changes on the project's default branch, or the real work's branch takes in a commit that is neither its own nor an ancestor of the start commit. The harness decides this from git, mechanically, before the pair is judged.
2. **Leaks.** The arm's transcript shows a tool call that reads, searches or runs a command on a path outside its copy, other than the system paths E-001's F4 allows and files the harness itself saved of that run's own output, at the path the harness named to it (the reading ruled on in reviews/E-001/reviewer.md, ruling 1, is settled here in advance). Doer arms may not use the web; source checks and the research judge may.
3. **Harness context differs** (finding 5). The harness records each run's full starting context (its first transcript lines). Before judging, it compares the two arms': they must match except for the change paragraph and the copy path. A pair that differs otherwise is a set-up fault (Sample, reruns). The instructions file each run is given is the copy's own repository's, never Model-Lab's or the Builder's; whatever the harness cannot remove must be the same in both arms, and is named in the result.
4. **No deliverable.** A doer arm that ends without the deliverable of its kind counts as a FAIL for that arm, not an exclusion: not finishing is part of the work.
5. **No verdict** after the reruns above.
6. **Not eligible** at entry (Method, step 2): no record at dispatch of the brief, start commit or model (for research, of the source check's); no role file for C1's `<path>`; an empty list for C2 (experiments/E-003/C2-built-named-inputs.md, cases); the piece belongs to this test.
7. **A served model changes mid-comparison.** Both arms of a pair always run on the same served model; a pair whose two arms report different served models is a set-up fault. If the served model changes between pairs, pairs are analysed by model, and the deciding test is run on the larger group only; the split is reported.

## Deciding threshold
Set now, per kind, after the owner's answers have replaced the judge's verdicts on their outputs:
- **Judge confirmed:** at least 6 cards answered Yes or No, and the owner disagreed with the judge on at most 2 of them. **Not confirmed** otherwise, with the reason (too many disagreements, or too few answered after 12 cards): no change is kept for that kind from this run; the result is "not shown: judge not confirmed", with the figures, and the owner rules whether to re-judge or stop.
- **Kept:** judge confirmed, **and** the one-sided exact binomial test on the discordant pairs gives P(X ≥ n10 | n10 + n01, ½) ≤ 1/40. For research also **the false-pass guard**: C3's false passes are no more than the unchanged check's plus one.
  - *Why plus one.* Forgiving what it cannot open is the one way C3 can do harm, and a false pass sends an unverified claim forward. The gain already counts no false pass, so the guard is a harm limit, not part of the gain. No tolerance at all would refuse C3 over a single memo on which the two versions read one borderline source differently, which eight cards cannot settle. Two or more extra false passes is a pattern: at 60 memos, over 3% of the kind, more than the one source-access return in the 14 research returns Q-008 classified (research/Q-008-memo.md, tallies). The figure is a judgment, stated here for the owner to change at the Build gate.
- **Not kept:** otherwise. The record states the gain and its 95% range (Newcombe's method for paired proportions). A positive gain that does not clear is reported as "not shown", with its range, never as "no effect" (pre-mortem R2).
- A kind with fewer than 10 pairs when it closes is reported as "too few pieces to test", not dropped (docs/PROJECT.md section 8).

## Holdout use
Each held-back check is used for its own kind's comparison only, by every judge session of that kind, and never for another kind or a later test; "used once" means once per kind. None is ever seen by any doer or checker under test. Pieces E-001 used (src/E-001/RESULT.md, "Pieces used") are past work and cannot enter, since only new pieces do.
