Each trial copies it into a trial record of its own, `experiments/E-004/trials/<trial id>.md`, fills the blanks this file marks *per trial*, and freezes that record before its first piece is drawn (T27: the entry card "carries the trial's plan, which is then fixed"). Nothing in a trial record may change a rule of this file; a trial that needs a different rule is not a trial under E-004, and its own design and odds come to the owner first (T30; U5's fallback, docs/projects/P-002.md section 7).

## Who does what
- **The Chief of Staff** writes each entry card and results card (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4), keeps the queue (P15), logs by hand every question put to the owner in conversation (T29), asks the Orchestrator for a piece's entry and draw before it writes the brief of a piece it dispatches itself (Step 4), and checks, before each entry card is put, that the trial keeps clear of the prompt engine (section 4, "Keeping clear of the prompt engine's work"; P12).
- **The Orchestrator** records each piece's entry and draw, with the Builder's draw script, in the trial's log in its own lane before the piece's brief is written (Step 4); runs the Builder's count script and commits its output (S-002, J1); starts each withdrawal check, within 7 days of the record that ended the piece's work or of the end of its 60 days, and writes its answer to the trial's log with the draw script (Exclusions); writes a void line to the trial's log with the draw script within 7 days of the record that makes an entry void (Exclusions, Void entries); and records the Reviewer's verdicts verbatim.
- **The Definer** writes each trial record from this file and the approved entry card's method, copies into it the line 0.097 and the sides of places 1 to 60 of each look that the draw script gives for its trial id (Step 4), and freezes it before the first piece is entered.
- **The Critic** checks each entry card before it goes to the owner, including how the method is switched on and off piece by piece (U5) and the P12 check; and gives its view on each results card (T28).
- **The Builder** builds the count and the trial records' scripts (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4), among them `src/E-004/count.py` and `src/E-004/draw.py`, in Build, before the first trial (M13); it does not run them for a trial.
- **The Reviewer**, which did not run the trial, re-derives every draw (Step 4) and works out each result against the line set in its trial record (T29); the Orchestrator records its verdict verbatim. A fresh Reviewer session, not told the piece's side, checks each withdrawal (Exclusions). The Reviewer that judges a look reads the trial's records through a per-task pack; the withdrawal check reads through a second per-task pack that keeps the trial's records and the brief records out (S-002, "Needed outside this spec's lane").
- **The owner** approves each entry card before anything is tried (T12), reads 5 pieces per look (P14), and approves or rejects putting the method into the operating model on its results card, on any result, passed or not (T13).
- **The agents doing the pieces** do them as usual. A trial adds no run to any piece: each piece is done once, with or without the method.

## Hypothesis
For one method and one kind of work: the method's pieces need the owner less often than the pieces without it, by more than chance at the line in the trial record, while its work holds up (T24). "Not passed" is an accepted result and comes to the owner like any other (T14).

## Method

### Step 0 — before the first trial (P20, P5)
No trial runs before the trial design (M9) is frozen, the starting count (M11) is on record and the owner has set the ceilings (P20; the ceilings are set: decisions/questions/P-002-design-gate.md, Parts 2 and 3). If the starting count shows fewer than 6 of 60 pieces needed the owner, the work stops and comes back to the owner (P5), and no trial starts.

### Step 1 — the entry card and the trial record (T12, T27, T31)
Each method comes to the owner first on a plain-English entry card: what the change would be, how it is done today, why, and its impact and risks (T12), with what the first assessment rests on (T27). The card also carries, from its trial record:
1. **The kind of work** the trial runs on, one of built work, plans or research (S-002, rule K2).
2. **How the method is switched on and off piece by piece** (U5; T30), in the form of Step 3: the exact text or step applied to a "with" piece, where it is put, who puts it, and how it is kept off the "without" pieces. The Critic checks this before the card goes to the owner. A method that cannot be switched this way is not tried under E-004; its card says so, and the owner rules whether to try it another way, whose design and odds would come to the owner first, or not at all (U5's fallback).
3. **Its expected length** (U3), from Step 2.
4. **The evidence it was let through on**: one source someone else has opened and confirmed (T8), its conditions read against ours (T9), and any outside code, prompt or skill checked before it runs, with no data out (T10), each with its record (S-002, M10).
5. **The guards beside the prompt engine** (Step 7): which kinds the prompt engine is testing or counting on the day the card is put, and whether the method could change how often work passes its first check.
6. **The line** (Deciding threshold), the hold-up checks, the stops and the short reads, as in this file, and what this trial cannot catch (Deciding threshold), the sides being known ahead among it, with the choices it leaves to the session that dispatches: which brief goes next, delaying a dispatch, merging two entered briefs into one pull request, entering a brief twice, or, for the Orchestrator, the time within the 7 days at which it starts a withdrawal check or writes a void line.
7. **What it costs the owner**: this card, a results card (T31), 5 short reads per look (P14) and the method's own touches.

At most 3 entry cards wait on the owner at once; the next waits in a queue the desk shows (P15). Nothing is tried without the owner's approval (T12). Once the owner approves, the Definer writes the trial record, copies the line and the sides into it and freezes it; only then is the first piece entered. The trial's plan is then fixed (T27).

**A method already judged on a kind** (its results card given) is not entered again on that kind under this plan as it stands: a repeat entry would be a re-run (D-047). It needs a genuinely different design, which the Critic checks is genuinely different before its entry card goes out.

*Per trial:* trial id (`T-<n>`, numbered in order of approval), method, kind, the switch (Step 3), the expected length, the entry card's record, the line (copied from this file's frozen Deciding threshold; the trial record copies the line, not a starting share, decisions/questions/P-002-two-look-line.md, "How it is carried out"), the sides of places 1 to 60 of each look (Step 4; reserve places 61 to 63 have no side in the record, Exclusions), the date of the commit that freezes the record.

### Step 2 — each kind's expected length (U3)
The count (M11) runs from the start of this project's Build (the owner's ruling beside the prompt engine: "the count of touches may run from the start of this project's Build", docs/projects/P-002.md section 6, last bullet). From its first 4 full weeks, the Builder's script gives, for each kind, the number of pieces that would have been eligible (Step 4), dispatched per week, and from that pace:
- weeks to the first look: 60 pieces at that pace, plus 30 days (P1);
- weeks to the second look, if taken: 120 pieces at that pace, plus two waits of 30 days (this plan's reading of P1 with two looks: Step 5).

The same figures are given again from the latest 4 weeks in each status update (P17) and on each entry card. *This plan's design, for the owner's scenario card:* if, from the first 4 weeks, a kind would take more than 26 weeks to its first look, no entry card for that kind is put until the owner rules on U3's fallback for it: try fewer kinds, wait longer, or narrow the target (docs/projects/P-002.md section 7, U3). The figure 26 is half the 12 months to the target's check (T5), and is the owner's to change.

The record count gives the pace so far only as upper bounds (research/Q-017-memo.md §1, "By kind": in the 60 days to 9 November 2026, pull requests touching built work 124 across the three repositories, plans 150, research 55, counted under every kind they touched). The count's own first weeks replace them.

### Step 3 — switching a method on and off piece by piece (U5)
A method is applied only through what is handed to the agent doing one piece: its brief, or a file named in its brief that no other piece's brief names. The entry card and the trial record state, for the method:
1. **On:** the exact text added to a "with" piece's brief (a fenced block, applied as its own paragraph after the brief's task statement), or the exact step, file or setting named in the brief, and who adds it: the session that writes the brief (the Orchestrator or the Chief of Staff), at dispatch, after the draw.
2. **Off:** the "without" piece's brief is written as it would be with no trial. The method's text is in no role file, no AGENTS.md, no template, no pack and no shared file that a "without" piece's agent reads; a method that must live in any of those cannot be switched per piece (T30).
3. **Leak check:** a marker the method's text or step carries (a phrase of at least 5 words that appears nowhere in the repositories at the trial record's frozen commit). At each look, the Reviewer searches every "without" piece's brief and merged files for it; a hit is a leak, listed with the piece. Leaks are reported on the results card; a leak is not a reason to drop the piece (Exclusions).
4. **Removal:** stopping the method is not adding it; nothing has to be undone outside the "with" pieces, whose merged work stays and can be undone as any work can (T26).

The draw is recorded in the log, and the brief carries no label of the trial or the arm beyond the method itself; the agents and the Chief of Staff can still know which pieces carry the method (T29: "only the owner's sample is unlabelled").

### Step 4 — which pieces enter, and the shuffled draws (T23)
- **Eligible:** a new piece of the trial's kind, by its brief's role alone (S-002, rules K2 and K4; a piece whose kind K2 cannot give at dispatch is not eligible), in any of the three repositories, dispatched after the trial record is frozen, whose brief is recorded at dispatch, and which is not a record of this trial or of the prompt engine's test (its sheets, exports, verdicts or cards). **A look's weeks** run from the entry of its place 1 to the entry of its place 60, and again, for each reserve place it uses, from the later of the entry of place 60 and the withdrawal or void line that opens that reserve place, to that reserve place's entry (Exclusions). Every eligible piece dispatched in a look's weeks is entered, in the order it is dispatched. Since a piece is entered before its brief is written (T29), its brief record comes just after its entry: for a look's weeks and for which piece takes a reserve place (Exclusions), an entered piece counts as dispatched at its entry's time (`entered_at`, S-002, J2), so the order of dispatch among entered pieces is the order of their entries, and an eligible piece with no entry counts as dispatched at the commit time that first adds its brief record; the Reviewer checks that each entered piece's brief record follows its entry, and that the brief records follow in the same order as the entries. An entered piece's dispatch time for its 60 days (Exclusions) is the commit time that first adds its brief record, or its `entered_at` if no brief record is ever written. An eligible piece dispatched outside a look's weeks, such as while a look waits to be judged with no reserve place open, is outside the trial; it is listed and not entered. Entry in dispatch order does not stop the session that dispatches from choosing which brief goes next, since it can read the next place's side (What this trial cannot catch).
- **The draw:** "each new piece of one kind is assigned, before work on it starts, to 'with the method' or 'without', by shuffled draws, so each side gets 30, in the same weeks" (T23). This plan's design of the shuffle, fixed before any entry: each look has 60 places, numbered 1 to 60 in the order pieces are entered, in six blocks of 10 (places 1 to 10, 11 to 20, and so on). Within each block the 10 places are put in the order of the SHA-256 of the text `E-004:<trial id>:<look>:<place>`, lowest first; the first 5 are "with" and the other 5 "without". So each side gets 30, every block of 10 has 5 each way, and at no point in a look do the two sides' counts of entered pieces differ by more than 5. The sides of places 1 to 60 depend on the trial id, the look and the place number alone: no one chooses them, and no commit, time or text made during the trial can change them. Before the trial record is frozen, the Definer copies into it the 120 sides (60 per look) that `src/E-004/draw.py` gives for the trial id. Places 61 to 63 of each look are reserve places for withdrawals and void entries (Exclusions). Their sides are not in the trial record: a reserve place takes the side of the withdrawn piece or void entry it refills, which the log's withdrawal or void line names.
- **Entry and draw:** before the piece's brief is written, the Orchestrator runs `src/E-004/draw.py`, which writes the piece's entry (trial id, entry number, look, place, repository, brief number, kind, the time of entry and the model the doer runs on; the dispatch time, the commit time that first adds the brief record, does not exist yet, and the Reviewer joins it from the brief record at judging, or takes the time of entry if no brief record is ever written) and its draw (for places 1 to 60, the side the trial record fixes for that place; for a reserve place, the side named in the withdrawal or void line it refills) to the trial's log, `status/E-004/trials/<trial id>.jsonl` (S-002, J2), and the Orchestrator commits it. A piece the Chief of Staff dispatches is entered the same way: the Chief of Staff asks the Orchestrator for its entry and writes the brief only once the draw is committed. Each piece has exactly one entry and one draw; a second entry for the same brief is void, listed, and refilled as a withdrawal is (Exclusions, Void entries), and a second draw line for one entry is void and listed. The draw is recorded in the log before the brief is written (T29).
- **The Reviewer re-derives every draw:** at each look it recomputes the side of every place 1 to 60 by the rule above and compares it with the trial record and the log. For each reserve place used, it checks that the side in its draw line is the side in the withdrawal or void line it refills, and that this is the withdrawn piece's or void entry's side in its own draw line. It checks that reserve places are used in order (61, then 62, then 63), one per withdrawal or void line, each by the next eligible piece not already entered that was dispatched after both the entry of place 60 and that withdrawal or void line. It checks that the brief records follow in the same order as the entries, lists any eligible piece dispatched in the look's weeks that has no entry, and lists the eligible pieces dispatched outside them. Any difference is listed, and the trial comes to the owner.
- **The same weeks:** pieces are entered in the order they are dispatched, and each block of 10 places fills both sides, so both sides fill over the same weeks.
- **The second look**, if taken (Step 5), uses its own 60 places and look 2's sides in the trial record.

### Step 5 — the two looks (T15, T16, P1)
"Two looks are planned per trial" (T15).
1. **Look 1** is judged 30 days after the last of its 60 counted pieces (reserve pieces in place of withdrawn pieces and void entries, Exclusions) merges or is settled unmerged at 60 days (Exclusions) (P1, "Each trial is judged 30 days after its last piece", read at each look; this plan's reading). No new piece is drawn for the trial while look 1 waits, except into a reserve place that a withdrawal or a void entry opens (Exclusions). A look with a reserve place opened and not yet filled waits for it, for at most 60 days from the withdrawal or void line that opens it; a place still unfilled then is left empty, and the look is judged as Exclusions, "A place left empty", says. The same holds at look 2.
2. At look 1: the method's pieces are **passed** if they needed the owner less often by more than chance at the line, and the work held up (T24; Deciding threshold); the result comes to the owner.
3. **The second look:** "if the first look leaned the right way (the method's pieces needed the owner less often) but did not pass, 30 more pieces each way are assigned, and all are judged together on a slightly stricter line, so that a useless method passes about as often as at one look (…); it is planned before the trial starts, so it is one trial, not a re-run" (T16). "Leaned the right way" means a strictly smaller share of the method's pieces than of the other side's pieces needed the owner; with 30 each way, which the reserve places keep while withdrawals and void entries stay within the limit of 3 a look (Exclusions), that is strictly fewer of the method's 30 pieces than of the other 30 (a tie does not go on; this plan's reading, the same as the odds scripts, intake/P-002-value-target-odds-2026-11-10.md, twolook.py, `x<y`). A look with a place left empty is decided as Exclusions, "A place left empty", says.
4. Look 1 does not go on to the second look if its hold-up checks fail (P6, Step 6).
5. **Look 2** is judged 30 days after the last of its 60 new counted pieces (reserve pieces in place of withdrawn pieces and void entries, Exclusions) merges or is settled unmerged at 60 days, on all 120 pieces, 60 each way (for a place left empty, Exclusions), at the same line as look 1.
6. Look 1 with no lean the right way, or look 2 not passed, is **not passed**; nothing is dropped automatically, and every result comes to the owner (T14).

### Step 6 — the hold-up checks and the short reads (T25, P14, P6)
- **Holds up:** "the method's pieces do no worse, by more than chance at 1 in 10, at passing their first independent check and at not being reopened for a fault within 30 days; a reopening is marked by the reviewer of the later change, not the piece's maker" (T25). Each is a one-sided Fisher's exact test in the harm direction at 0.10 (the method's pieces pass less often, or are reopened more often), on the look's pieces (30 each way at look 1, 60 each way at look 2; for a place left empty, Exclusions). A piece with no independent check is left out of the first measure only, and listed (S-002, rule C1). The work holds up when neither test clears 0.10.
- **Short reads (P14):** "at each look the owner reads 5 of the trial's pieces, chosen at random and unlabelled, 10 for a trial that takes its second look; one the owner would not have accepted counts as reopened" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 6, last bullet). This plan's design: at look 1, 5 drawn from its 60 pieces; at look 2, 5 drawn from its 60 new pieces; each draw by the order of the SHA-256 of `<trial id>:read:<look>:<entry number>`, lowest first, so the Chief of Staff does not choose. The Chief of Staff shows each as its merged work, with no brief and no arm, and with the method's marker phrase removed wherever it appears in what is shown, the merged files included. Other traces of the method in the merged work, such as its own words beyond the marker, can still show the side; that is a known leak (What this trial cannot catch). A piece the owner would not have accepted counts as reopened in the hold-up check (P14). The short reads are read before the look is judged.
- **Stop at once (P6):** "a trial stops at once if its first look fails the hold-up checks, so the second look does not run and the result comes to the owner".

### Step 7 — stops, voids and the guards beside the prompt engine
- **P7:** "Stop a trial at once if it breaks work the owner relies on". No new piece is drawn; the result so far comes to the owner on a results card.
- **P8:** "a change of AI model during a trial voids it". The model each piece's doer runs on is in its entry (Step 4); a piece on a different model from the trial's first piece voids the trial, and the void comes to the owner. A change of AI model also triggers a re-check of the methods kept under the old one (P10).
- **P11:** one method per kind of work at a time. So at most three trials run at once, one per kind.
- **P12:** "no method that could change how often work passes its first check is tried on a kind of work while the prompt engine is testing that kind or counting its 30 real-use pieces". *Testing* is read as from the opening of that kind's comparison in the prompt engine's test (experiments/E-003.md) until its result is recorded; *counting* as from the day a change of the prompt engine enters the operating model until 30 pieces of that kind are counted under it (docs/PROJECT.md section 6, V2). A method is taken as one that could change how often work passes its first check unless its entry card shows why it could not and the Critic agrees. If the prompt engine opens that kind while such a trial runs, no new piece is drawn and the owner rules whether the trial waits or is voided (this plan's design).
- **P19:** "no method enters the operating model while the prompt engine is testing or counting the kind of work it changes, unless the owner rules otherwise for that method on its results card". The results card states the prompt engine's state for that kind on its day.
- **P20:** Step 0.
- **P4:** after the first five trials a summary comes to the owner, who rules whether to go on. No sixth entry card is put before that ruling.

### Step 8 — the results card (T13, T19, T28, T29, P16, T18)
After a trial is judged, or stopped, or voided, a results card in more detail, like a gate card, comes to the owner, who approves or rejects putting the method into the operating model on any result, passed or not (T13). It carries:
- the result the Reviewer worked out (T29), with the counts each way, the line, the p-value at each look, the hold-up checks, the short reads, leaks, the sides re-derived and any that differ, reserve places used, pieces left out and why, void entries by side with the reserve place each opened, any place left empty by side with the result worked out with each such place filled in every way (Exclusions), entries counted from pull requests that are not pieces, entries whose work merged in two or more pull requests, eligible pieces dispatched outside the look's weeks, and the kind's pace;
- each look's result both with and without the touch-log lines committed after their piece merged (Exclusions), and which of the two decides;
- what this trial cannot catch (Deciding threshold), the sides being known ahead among it, with the choices it leaves to the session that dispatches: which brief goes next, delaying a dispatch, merging two entered briefs into one pull request, entering a brief twice, or, for the Orchestrator, the time within the 7 days at which it starts a withdrawal check or writes a void line;
- the line and, where the counted starting share for the trial's kind falls in one of (a)'s two bands (about 0.31 to 0.43 and about 0.57 to 0.69; Deciding threshold), that a method that does nothing would pass a little more than 1 in 10 of the time there (decisions/questions/P-002-two-look-line.md, "How it is carried out");
- plainly, when the method was not passed and why, including when its work did worse (T19);
- the Critic's view and a recommendation (T28);
- the guards: the prompt engine's state for the kind (P19);
- if the owner may approve a method whose trial did not pass: that doing so goes against "Killed, not tuned", and the departure goes to the operating model as a proposal (T18).
Results cards are never held back, reach the owner within two weeks of a trial being judged, and never outnumber the entry cards approved (P16). A method the owner approves enters the operating model only through the model's own change process (T17), with a review date six months on and a way to remove it (P9).

## Measure
- **The deciding measure (T3, T24):** per look, the number of pieces each way that needed at least one owner touch before merging (S-002, rules RT1 to RT3, RT6 and RT7; rule RT5's rulings are outside the count until the owner rules on them).
- **The hold-up measures (T25):** per look, the number each way that passed their first independent check (S-002, rule C1), and the number each way reopened for a fault within 30 days of merging (S-002, rules RR1 to RR3, with the short reads, P14).
- **Reported, not deciding:** each look's result without the late touch-log lines (Exclusions), reserve places used, withdrawals and their reasons, void entries, any place left empty, leaks, pieces left out, the pace of the kind, the touches per piece, and the owner's short-read answers.

## Exclusions
- **Withdrawn** (this plan's design; the 3, the 60 days and the 7 days are this plan's figures for the owner's scenario card): a drawn piece is withdrawn only when all three hold: (i) no owner touch on it, in the records or in touch-log lines committed so far; (ii) its work has not merged: its pull request was closed unmerged, never opened, or is still open; (iii) a recorded brief or decision record ended its work, or it is still unmerged 60 days after its dispatch (below). Each withdrawal is decided by a withdrawal check: a fresh Reviewer session that the Orchestrator starts, given only the piece's repository, brief number and dispatch time, its pull request's number and state if it has one, the record that ended its work if (iii) rests on one, and the records and touch-log lines that name the piece, for (i). It is not given the piece's brief, which on a "with" piece carries the method, nor the trial record, the trial's log or the piece's entry or place, and it reads through a pack that keeps them out (S-002, "Needed outside this spec's lane"); so it is not told the side, and the conditions leave it no choice. It returns `withdrawn: yes | no` with the records it relied on, and the Orchestrator writes its answer to the log with the draw script, which copies the piece's side into that line from the piece's draw line (S-002, J2). The Reviewer that judges the look checks every withdrawal again, and lists any piece that met (i) to (iii) with no withdrawal line in the trial's log committed within 7 days of the commit that added the record that ended its work, or of the end of its 60 days (the 7 days is this plan's figure), since the Orchestrator, which knows the sides, starts each check and could otherwise start some early and others late. A withdrawn piece is left out and listed with its side. A withdrawal opens the look's next reserve place (place 61, then 62, then 63). That place has no side in the trial record: it takes the withdrawn piece's side, as the withdrawal line names it, and goes to the next eligible piece not already entered that was dispatched after both the entry of place 60 and the withdrawal, so a withdrawal does not shorten either side. The Reviewer checks each reserve place's side against its withdrawal line and the withdrawn piece's draw line (Step 4). Withdrawals and void entries (below) count together toward one limit of 3 per look: a fourth piece that meets the three conditions is not withdrawn, and a fourth void entry opens no reserve place; at either, the trial stops, no new piece is drawn, and the result so far comes to the owner on a results card, as under P7. The results card reports withdrawals by side.
- **Unmerged at 60 days:** a drawn piece still unmerged 60 days after its dispatch is settled then. One with an owner touch stays on its side as a piece that needed the owner, and is left out of the hold-up measures only, since it has no merge to check; it is listed. One with none goes to the withdrawal check, which withdraws it under (ii) and (iii), unless it would be the fourth withdrawal or void entry in the look, which stops the trial (above). So no piece in places 1 to 60 holds its look for longer than 60 days after its dispatch, plus the 30 days before judging. A reserve piece adds its own wait: the time until an eligible piece is dispatched after both the entry of place 60 and the withdrawal or void line that opens its place, which depends on the kind's pace and is at most 60 days from that line (A place left empty, below), then up to 60 days after the reserve piece's own dispatch, then the 30 days.
- **Closed by the owner:** a drawn piece the owner closes or sends back for good stays in its side, as a piece that needed the owner (a send-back, S-002, rule RT3).
- **An entry and its pull requests** (this plan's design, for the owner's scenario card). An entry is per brief and the measure counts pieces, which are pull requests (S-002, rules RP1 and RP2), so:
  - *One pull request carrying the work of two entered briefs* (S-002, E-RP4: one piece): counted once, in the first entry's look and place and on its side; the later entry is void and listed, and opens the look's next reserve place, which takes its side (Void entries, below). Why: the pull request is one piece, and it is kept on a side the draw fixed before either work began; the later entry's side is refilled, so a choice to merge the two works, made after both sides were known, shortens neither side. The merge still moves the later work, with its touches, onto the first entry's side. When the first entry is "without" and the later "with", the method's text in it is a leak, found by the marker search and not a reason to drop it (Step 3), and a leak of the method's text shrinks the difference; but the later work's touches then count on "without", which widens the difference in the method's favour when that work needed the owner. So a merge can move the result either way; it is capped by the limit of 3 a look that void entries share with withdrawals, and is among what this trial cannot catch (The sides are known ahead).
  - *An entered brief whose work merges as a pull request that is not a piece* (S-002, rule RP2: records, questions or other only): counted once on its entry's side, and listed. Why: as with a kind (S-002, rule K4), a piece's place in the trial is fixed at the draw and does not change with what its files turn out to be, so a method that turns work into records alone cannot take its own pieces out of the count.
  - *An entered brief whose work merges in two or more pull requests*: counted once on its entry's side; its trial piece is the first of its pull requests to merge, whose J1 line is joined, with every touch on any of its pull requests from its dispatch up to that merge; the others are listed. A pull request of it closed unmerged is not one that merges: when the owner closed it, rule RT3(b) counts its close as a send-back on the one that merges. Why: the draw assigned the entry, not a pull request; taking the first merge keeps "its work has not merged" in (ii) and the 60-day rule as written; and leaving the entry out would let a choice to split the work, made after its side was known, take it out.
  - *A piece that never merges* (Unmerged at 60 days with an owner touch; Closed by the owner): it has no J1 line, so its touches are counted by rules RT1 to RT3, RT6 and RT7 with "its merge" read as its settle date (the end of its 60 days, or the time the owner closed it); the owner's close of a piece closed by the owner counts as a send-back (rule RT3) on that piece itself; and the Reviewer joins them at judging (S-002, J2).
- **Void entries** (this plan's design; the 7 days is this plan's figure, as for withdrawals): a duplicate entry, a second entry for a brief already entered (Step 4), and a merged entry, the later of two entries whose work merges in one pull request (above). A void entry is left out and listed with its side. When an entry becomes void, the Orchestrator writes a void line to the trial's log with the draw script within 7 days of the record that makes it void (the commit of the second entry, or the merge of the one pull request), and the script copies the void entry's side into that line from the entry's draw line (S-002, J2); no judgement is needed, since both facts are in the records. A void line opens the look's next reserve place exactly as a withdrawal does: the place takes the void entry's side and goes to the next eligible piece not already entered that was dispatched after both the entry of place 60 and the void line, so a void entry does not shorten either side. Void entries count with withdrawals toward the limit of 3 per look (Withdrawn, above). The Reviewer that judges the look checks every entry for duplicates and for two entries merged in one pull request, and lists any void entry with no void line in the trial's log committed within 7 days of the record that made it void. A second draw line for one entry is void and listed; it takes no place, so it opens none.
- **A place left empty** (this plan's design). The reserve places keep 30 each way while withdrawals and void entries stay within the limit of 3 a look, which is what the figures in Deciding threshold are worked out for (A2). A place is left empty only when a void entry has no void line in the trial's log when the look is judged, when a fourth void entry stops the trial and opens no reserve place, or when a reserve place that a withdrawal or void line opens is not filled within 60 days of that line (the 60 days is this plan's figure, the same span after which a drawn piece still unmerged is settled). A look with a reserve place opened and not yet filled waits for it and is not judged; if no eligible piece is dispatched after both the entry of place 60 and that line within the 60 days, the place is left empty at their end and takes no piece after, and an eligible piece dispatched after then is outside the look's weeks (Step 4). An empty place has no piece, so the 30 days before judging run from the last piece the look has (Step 5); a look with a reserve place left empty at the end of its 60 days is judged no sooner than that end. A look with an empty place, or the result so far of a trial stopped with one, is judged on the pieces it has: "leaned the right way" compares the shares of each side's pieces that needed the owner, not their counts (Step 5); the touch test and the hold-up tests are run on the actual counts; and the Reviewer also works out the result with each empty place filled in every way (as a piece on its side that needed the owner or did not, passed its first check or did not, and was reopened or was not). The result decides only if it is the same in every way; otherwise the look has no deciding result, the trial stops, no new piece is drawn, and the results card brings the results to the owner, as under P7. Each empty place is listed with its side, and the results card says that the figures are worked out for 30 each way.
- **No first check:** left out of the first hold-up measure only (Step 6).
- **Touch-log lines:** a line counts in a look only when the commit that adds it is before the look is judged; the Reviewer reports each line's commit time beside the touch time it states, and lists as late any line committed after its piece merged. A late line committed before the look is judged counts in the result that decides; the Reviewer also works out each look's result without the late lines and reports both, and the results card states both (Step 8).
- **Not excluded:** a leaked piece (Step 3), a piece with an incomplete record (it is completed from the records and from touch-log lines committed before the look is judged, and a gap is listed), a piece of an agent that knew its side (T29).

### What this trial cannot catch
- **Only large effects show.** At 30 pieces each way a smaller real gain mostly reads as "not passed", which is "not shown", not "does not help" (the value-target card as put, questions/P-002-value-target.md at commit 964da47, "Context": "Few-run trials find only large effects"; docs/projects/P-002.md section 2).
- **The hold-up guard is coarse.** A method that doubles how often pieces fail a check is caught only about 3 to 7 times in 10 at 30 pieces each way, and less often the rarer the fault (the same card, "Limits of this design", "It is a coarse guard against harm"). The usual checks and the owner's merge stay the main protection.
- **Trials are not blind.** The agents and the Chief of Staff know which pieces carry the method; only the owner's short reads are unlabelled, and even those can show the side through the method's own words in the merged work (Step 6; the same card, "Not blind"; T29). Agents could ask the owner less on "with" pieces; the hold-up checks are there to catch that.
- **The sides are known ahead.** The sides of places 1 to 60 are fixed in the trial record so that no one can steer a draw, so a session that reads it can know the next place's side, and a reserve place's side is known once the withdrawal or void line that opens it is logged. The session that dispatches can therefore choose which brief takes the next place, or delay a dispatch, knowing the side it will get. It can also merge two entered briefs into one pull request, or enter a brief twice, and so void an entry of its choosing: the void entry's place is refilled on its side, so neither side is shortened while the limit holds, and void entries share the limit of 3 a look with withdrawals, but the choice moves which side later pieces fall on, and a merge moves the later work's touches onto the first entry's side (Exclusions). The Orchestrator, which knows the sides, also chooses when, within the 7 days, it starts a withdrawal check or writes a void line (Exclusions), and that time decides which later piece takes the reserve place the line opens and which later pieces fall outside the look's weeks (Step 4). Eligibility by role alone, entry in dispatch order and the Reviewer's check of every entry against the brief records' times stop a dispatched piece being skipped or entered out of order, and list the eligible pieces with no entry and every void entry; they do not catch a choice of which brief to dispatch next, a dispatch delayed on purpose, a merge of two entered briefs into one pull request, a brief entered twice on purpose, or the time within the 7 days at which a withdrawal check is started or a void line is written, of which the Reviewer lists only a line that is late or missing; the hold-up checks catch them only in part (Step 4). This limit is on the entry card and the results card (Steps 1 and 8).
- **Leaks into "without" pieces** beyond the marker phrase are not found, and any leak shrinks the difference (Step 3; U5).
- **What the figures assume.** They hold only if exactly 30 pieces fall each way at each look (A2), pieces are independent with one fixed chance on each side (A1) and the starting share is the same at both looks (A6); they come before the hold-up checks, the short reads and any stop (A5). Sides of other sizes, pieces that depend on each other or a share that changes between looks are not worked out; a look with a place left empty is judged as Exclusions, "A place left empty", says.
