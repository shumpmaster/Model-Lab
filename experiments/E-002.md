**The question.** Will the checks that judge the fair test give the same verdict on the same work when asked again, and does a judge on the same AI model as the work judge it more kindly than a judge on a different model?

## Hypothesis
A judge on a different AI model from the work gives the same verdict when it judges the same piece twice, and agrees with a judge on the work's own model, closely enough that the fair test's verdicts reflect the work rather than the judge. Either outcome is a result: if it holds, M2 uses that judge, with the one-provider limit stated; if not, M2 uses the brief's fallback (below).

## Method
**The models.** All helpers come from one provider (the environment check). The work is done on Opus 5.5 (the replays of E-001). The **test judge** is Fable 5.1: of the four models available it is the only one outside the work's 5.5 line, so it is the nearest this plan offers to "a different model family" (docs/PROJECT.md section 8). Whether it is a different family is not established. The **same-model judge** is Opus 5.5.

**What a judgement is.** One helper session runs the piece's check as it was then (the checker's role file, pack and rules at the piece's commit), on the piece as submitted, and returns PASS or FAIL with its findings. Each session is fresh and sees no other judgement.

**The pieces.**
- **Set B (8 pieces, preferred):** the 8 replay results of E-001, whose worker model is known. Each is judged three times: by the test judge twice (the first is E-001's own check run) and by the same-model judge once.
- **Set A (4 pieces):** past round-1 submissions with a recorded round-1 verdict, recovered from git history at the commit the round-1 check names. Each is judged once by the test judge, against the record. They are the pieces of Q-008's §3 samples not audited in E-001, in list order, alternating the Service Desk (SD) and the operating model's repository (POOM), plans first: SD job-prs-recorded (plan), POOM S-023 (plan), SD desk-v2 stage 1a (built), POOM S-015 proof-diag (built). If one cannot be recovered, the next in the same order takes its place (SD records, POOM S-024, SD desk-v2 stage 3, POOM S-032, …), and the skip is listed.
- **If E-001 gives fewer than 8 replay results**, each missing place is filled from the same list as Set A, continuing its order, and that piece is judged twice by the test judge (2 runs, the same as a Set B piece). Its worker model is unknown, so it adds to the consistency measure only, and the judges' agreement rests on fewer pieces.

**Research.** A source check must open every cited source, and this check reads no web. A research piece is judged here only if its sources are all repository records; E-001's replays are the only way one enters.

## Measure
- **Consistency (deciding):** on how many pieces the test judge's two verdicts agree. Agreement on the blocking findings is reported beside it.
- **Judges' agreement (deciding):** on how many Set B pieces the test judge's first verdict and the same-model judge's verdict differ, and in which direction. Disagreements that all go the same-model judge's way (it passes, the test judge fails) are the sign of self-preference (R3).
- **Agreement with the record (reported, not deciding):** the test judge's verdict beside the recorded round-1 verdict, on Set A and any substitutes. Not deciding: the original judge's model is not recorded, and every Set A piece failed round 1 (they come from samples of returns).
- **Cost:** tokens, tool uses and time per run, as each helper reports at return.

## Sample
8 pieces for consistency and up to 8 (Set B) for judges' agreement, plus 4 (Set A) against the record.

**Runs.** One run is one helper session started for this check, on one piece, whatever it does; a session that errors still counts. E-001's check runs count in E-001, not here. E-002 adds, per Set B piece, the test judge's second verdict and the same-model judge's verdict (16 runs), and one run per Set A piece (4 runs): **at most 20**. With E-001's at most 20, together at most 40 of the 100 runs allowed through Design (docs/PROJECT.md section 8).

**What this size can show.** Only gross inconsistency. A judge that flips 1 verdict in 4 would still clear the consistency bar below about 37 times in 100 (binomial, 8 pieces, at most 1 flip); a judge that flips half its verdicts would clear it about 4 times in 100. A pass here means "no gross inconsistency found", not "consistent".

## Exclusions
- Kinds other than plans, research and built work, as in E-001.
- Research pieces whose check needs outside web pages (above).
- Pieces with no recorded verdict (Q-008 lists them), for Set A only.
- Any piece whose round-1 submission or check pack cannot be recovered from history, listed with its reason.

## Deciding threshold
Set now:
- **Consistent, judges agree:** the test judge disagrees with itself on at most 1 of the 8 pieces, **and** it and the same-model judge differ on at most 2 of the Set B pieces. M2 uses the test judge (Fable 5.1) to judge both arms, and its record states the one-provider limit below.
- **Fallback (U3, docs/PROJECT.md section 7)** if the test judge disagrees with itself on 2 or more pieces, **or** the two judges differ on 3 or more Set B pieces in either direction: a held-back set of checks the work never sees and the owner's own sample decide the test's verdicts, and M2's record states the limit.
- The direction of the judges' disagreements is reported with either result: all going the same-model judge's way is reported as a sign of self-preference, even if fewer than 3.
- If fewer than 6 pieces reach two test-judge verdicts, or fewer than 4 Set B pieces reach both judges' verdicts, the check has not answered U3 and the fallback applies.

## Where the one-provider limit weakens this check
Every judge here comes from the same provider as the work. Two models from one provider may share training methods, data and habits, so a bias they share, such as preferring work written the way that provider's models write, would make them agree with each other and would not show in this check. A pass therefore shows only that two models from one provider agree and repeat themselves; it cannot show the judges are unbiased in the sense R3 asks. Whether Fable 5.1 counts as a different model family is not established, so the brief's "different family" may not be met at all. Only a judge outside the provider could test the shared bias, and that needs the owner to override the spend ceiling in their own words (decisions/questions/P-001-spend-ceiling.md). Within the plan, the owner's own sample in the fallback is the only judgement from outside the provider.

## Holdout use
None. The held-back set of checks the fallback would use is M2's and is not seen here. The pieces used here are listed in the result, so M2 can keep them out of its held-back set and its test pool.
