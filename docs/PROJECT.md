## 2. Strategy
*In plain language: the chain from the deliverables to the value — how this work produces it.*

**The problem, stated without reference to any industry (Discovery-4, draft for the Critic).** Work passes from one worker to the next through a written hand-over: what to do, what to read, and what counts as done. An independent check then accepts the work or sends it back. Some returns are the check doing its job, catching a real fault; others are avoidable, and the record seldom shows why work went back: the written instructions, the material handed over, how the work was split, the worker's own capability, or the check itself. The problem is to make work pass its check first time more often where the return was avoidable, by finding which of those causes each return and changing only what helps (which may be nothing), without adding more written rules than a worker reliably follows, and without judging the change only by checks that can be fooled. The count of past work (the milestone "The causes of rework are known") separates returns that caught a real fault from avoidable ones as far as the records allow.

**What other fields found, and what it may mean here.** Only findings the source checks cleared for filing are used (research/Q-002-to-Q-007-corrections-confirmation.md); each is graded C (seen in search extracts from two or more independent hosts, not opened by the Researcher) unless marked. Every "may mean here" is a candidate to test, not a conclusion; each names the conditions the finding depends on and whether they hold for AI agents. Those transfer judgements are the Researcher's own opinions (Q-005 §4), not filed facts, and are labelled so.
- *The same written tool gave different results in different places.* A surgical checklist mandated in 101 Ontario hospitals showed no significant change in adjusted deaths or complications (LF-Q005-02), while a randomised trial in two hospitals found complications falling from 19.9% to 11.5%, though its fall in deaths was not significant (LF-Q005-03). A shift-handover bundle that included training reported 23% fewer medical errors at nine sites (LF-Q005-06a), and 47% fewer handoff-related adverse events with 18 months of coaching at 32 hospitals (LF-Q005-06b); a review of another handover tool found only moderate evidence (LF-Q005-07). *Conditions:* the effective programmes came inside training, coaching and peer routines, and no study separates the written tool's own effect; why Ontario saw nothing is not established. *Transfer (the Researcher's opinion, unfiled, Q-005 §4):* agents have no training, culture or peer pressure, so for them the text and the checks are nearly the whole intervention, and effect sizes from these fields cannot be carried over. *May mean here:* a change to instructions alone may do much less than these figures suggest, or nothing; only a test on our own work can say.
- *A procedure written for one situation failed in another.* The NTSB found an airliner's dual-engine-failure checklist assumed high altitude and could not be completed at low altitude (LF-Q005-08). *Conditions:* the authors assumed a situation that did not hold, and following the text used up the scarce resource, time. *Transfer (the Researcher's opinion, unfiled, Q-005 §4):* plausible: agents follow text literally and their scarce resources are turns and context; rules carrying exceptions for other situations are the agent analogue. *May mean here:* look for instructions written for one kind of task that misfire on another.
- *Rules are not always followed, and warnings that multiply are overridden.* Flight-crew audits reported intentional non-compliance on 49% of archived flights, travelling with more unintentional errors (LF-Q005-10); drug-safety alerts are overridden in 49–96% of cases (LF-Q005-14). *Conditions:* human attention, fatigue and judgement. *Transfer:* fatigue does not apply (the Researcher's opinion, unfiled, Q-005 §4); an analogue may, since agents' instruction compliance falls as the number of simultaneous instructions rises (Q-002 F-d, a filed single-source fact), but its threshold is unknown. *May mean here:* check from the transcript whether an instruction was followed, not only whether it was written; treat added length as a cost. (A "mechanised check" below means one a script runs, such as a tool permission or a test, rather than an instruction an agent must remember.)
- *Rules go out of date.* Half of 17 clinical guidelines were outdated by 5.8 years, and the authors recommended reassessment every 3 years (LF-Q005-15). This is the authors' recommendation, not evidence that scheduled review works. *Transfer (the Researcher's opinion, Q-005 §4 P-11):* agent instructions are tied to model versions, which change faster than clinical evidence; the right interval is unknown. *May mean here:* a candidate: review each instruction on a schedule set from our own data (LP-Q005-09).
- *Before-and-after comparisons can mislead.* In an English replication, an intensive-care infection rate fell from 3.7 to 1.48 per 1,000 catheter-days, but the fall did not accelerate after the intervention training and was likely part of a wider secular trend (LF-Q005-05a, in the paper's wording). *Transfer (the Researcher's opinion, unfiled, Q-005 §4, resting on an assumed premise):* a model or version change during a study may act like such a trend. *May mean here:* compare each change with no change over the same period, ideally on the same tasks, if past tasks can be replayed faithfully, which is not yet known (Q-003 §7).
- *In agent pipelines specifically.* Near-equivalent prompt formats can move accuracy by up to 76 points, about 10 on average, and the sources disagree on what reduces this (Q-002 F-f); so wording can matter a great deal, and a single comparison can mistake noise for an effect (Q-002 P-e). Self-correction without reliable outside feedback does not reliably improve reasoning (Q-002 F-g), and model judges favour their own output, which panels from several model families reduce but do not remove (Q-002 F-h). Accuracy falls as inputs grow longer, well below the context limit (Q-002 F-c). The Researcher's overall estimate, not a filed fact, is that the model and the effort and context a session works with are the larger levers on capability-limited tasks, and instructions on a narrower set of failures, such as underspecified requirements (Q-002 §1 items 4–5). *May mean here:* the instructions are one candidate cause among five, which is why the problem is stated as avoidable returns, not as "better prompts".

## 6. Value and stop rule
*This project's own measure of value, the target and date, and what counts as a miss — including the cost to the owner's time. Write each value target on its own line starting with its ID, like `V1: <measure>, <target>, <date>`; spec criteria trace to these IDs.*

*Ruled by the owner, 2026-11-01 (decisions/questions/P-001-value-target.md, option A). "First-time pass rate" is the share of work that passes its independent check at the first try: a plan its review, a research memo its source check, built work its reviewer.*

V1: first-time pass rate of the kind of work a change targets, raised by 20 percentage points over leaving things as they are in a paired test on the same past tasks (a change is kept when its gain is so large that chance alone would produce it at most 1 time in 40; the test is sized to see a 20-point gain), checked when testing ends, before anything enters the operating model
V2: first-time pass rate of each kind of work (plans, research, built work) over the first 30 pieces checked under the changed operating model, in any project, above the recounted starting figure for that kind by so much that chance alone would produce it at most 1 time in 25, checked when 30 pieces of each kind are done; no calendar date (owner's ruling)

- **Starting figures:** about 1 in 17 plans, 2 in 12 research memos and 15 in 50 pieces of built work passed first time, from the Service Desk's conversation-started records, which cannot show causes (research/Q-006-memo.md §0 items 1–2); the recount under "The causes of rework are known" (M1) replaces them, and the real-use odds are then restated.
- **Miss on V1:** no change clears chance. The result is "no change helps", which the owner accepted in advance (decisions/questions/P-001-no-change-result.md); the record of each agent's brief and AI model (M3) still goes into the operating model, and the project closes.
- **Miss on V2:** a kept change that falls short is removed at the operating model's next scheduled review of its rules, not adjusted to pass ("Killed, not tuned", OPERATING_MODEL.md §1).
- **Too few pieces:** if fewer than 30 pieces of a kind are checked within six months of the change entering the operating model, that kind is judged on what has been checked, with its uncertainty stated.
- **Stop and go to the owner:** if the recount shows the instructions are not a meaningful cause of rework for any kind of work (the owner rules whether to re-aim or close); or if the 100 test runs allowed through Design are used before the fair test is ready (decisions/questions/P-001-test-budget.md).
- **Cost to the owner's time:** no cap (decisions/questions/P-001-time-and-limit.md); each status update reports the tokens the helpers report using, and says the plan's remaining allowance cannot be read from a session.
- **Testing budget:** testing all three kinds at this strength takes about 300 runs; the budget for the test itself is the owner's at the Build gate.
