## 2. Strategy
*In plain language: the chain from the deliverables to the value — how this work produces it.*

*Draft by the Chief of Staff, 2026-11-09, revised after the Critic's round 1 (reviews/P-002-problem-premortem/critic.md). The chain from the work to the value, which is where the design goes, follows once the value target is ruled.*

**The problem, stated without reference to any industry (Discovery-4).** One person supervises a team of delegated workers who do most of the planning and making. The person wants more of that work carried by the workers from start to finish, with the person trusting what comes back and the work holding up in use (the owner's aim, decisions/questions/P-002-target.md).

Three things stand in the way:
- **Which ways of running the team would help is unknown.** The ways change quickly, and few have been tested by anyone independent of the people reporting them; where claims were re-tested, fewer held (Q-011-1, Q-011-4, below). In the owner's view, waiting for full proof means adopting late: "Cutting edge is usually not something that you can truly review and find fully validated and tested" (same record).
- **The person cannot yet measure the share of work carried, the trust in it, or whether it holds up.** So no change can yet be shown to help or harm (pre-mortem R12).
- **The person's time to check changes is limited.** What is read from outside may carry harm, and a rise in the share of work done can hide a fall in its quality (pre-mortem R3, R7).

The problem is to raise the share of work the team carries end to end, and the person's justified trust in it, under those three limits.

**What other fields found, and what it may mean here.** Each point gives the finding with its source and its strength, then the conditions it depended on, then whether those hold here. *Transfer* and *may mean here* are the Chief of Staff's opinions, candidates to test, not findings. Library entries are in the portfolio library (library/facts/ and library/patterns/); none is load-bearing on its own.

- **Where claims were re-tested, fewer held.**
  - *Fact, weak (secondary citation; the 2014 study was not opened):* in one mapping study of software-engineering replications, 26% of external replications conformed to the original study, against 82% of internal ones (Q-011-1).
  - *Fact, preprint abstract, one study:* a replication of five popular prompting techniques across six models found "a general lack of statistically significant differences across nearly all techniques tested" (Q-011-4).
  - *Conditions:* academic studies and their replications; prompting techniques on benchmark tasks.
  - *Transfer:* plausible for reported AI practice, which is often reported by its makers; untested.
  - *May mean here:* expect a reported gain to be smaller on our own work.
- **Searching outside was associated with innovation, up to a point.**
  - *Fact, one study:* in 2,707 UK manufacturing firms, searching widely and deeply was "curvilinearly (taking an inverted U-shape) related to performance"; it is an association, not a cause (Q-011-11).
  - *Fact:* no study gathered compares looking for outside practice with not looking in AI work (research/Q-011-memo.md, answer).
  - *Conditions:* manufacturing firms; innovation output as the measure.
  - *Transfer:* weak. It suggests only that more sources need not mean more value.
  - *May mean here:* a few chosen sources, with what each yields counted.
- **What was copied without checking carried harm.**
  - *Fact, one source (a project page):* of 1.3 million Android apps, 15.4% contained security code copied from a programming Q&A site, and 97.9% of those had at least one insecure snippet (Q-011-10).
  - *Fact, attributed (an abstract, and a security vendor's blog with a stake):* scanners labelled up to 46.8% of one ecosystem's agent skills malicious, against 0.52% after analysis that took each repository's context into account; the two figures measure different things (Q-011-19).
  - *Conditions:* code copied into apps; downloadable skills for agents.
  - *Transfer:* close. Agents here may run outside code, prompts and skills.
  - *May mean here:* check outside material before it runs.
- **Hosted models changed behaviour within months, and prompting gains differed by model.**
  - *Fact, one study's abstract:* two hosted models' behaviour changed "substantially in a relatively short amount of time" between March and June 2023; this is drift of hosted models, not decay of practice findings (Q-012-3).
  - *Fact, weak, one study's abstract, one task type:* on Python functions, newer GPT models showed "diminishing or even negative marginal gains from structured prompting", while Qwen models "continue to benefit substantially from Few-Shot and CCoT" (Q-012-4).
  - *Conditions:* hosted models updated by their provider; code-writing tasks.
  - *Transfer:* direct for the models; how far it reaches methods of running a team is unknown.
  - *May mean here:* re-check a kept method when the model changes.
- **Few runs can only show large differences.**
  - *Estimate, the memo's own calculation and assumptions (independent runs, normal differences):* at 80% power, a paired comparison of 10 runs detects about one standard deviation of the run-to-run differences, and 20 runs about two-thirds of one. Below that, significant results often overstate the effect, and looking at results repeatedly without a pre-set rule raises false alarms (research/Q-013-memo.md, answer).
  - *Conditions:* the memo's assumptions; no study measures this for process changes in agent work (same memo).
  - *Transfer:* the arithmetic holds wherever its assumptions hold.
  - *May mean here:* most small trials will end "not shown", which is not the same as "does not help" (pre-mortem R2, R9).
- **No way of predicting in advance whether a practice would carry to a new setting has been tested.**
  - *Fact:* no conditions checklist was found that was applied before a practice moved and then scored against what happened, in any field (research/Q-010-memo.md, short answer).
  - *Transfer:* direct. The absence is general.
  - *May mean here:* a method's conditions can be read before a trial, to rule out clear misfits, but only a trial shows whether it helps here.
- **Stopping a practice and keeping a scan going both took effort.**
  - *Fact, a news report of insurer data (lead author at the insurer):* of 7 low-value medical services flagged by one campaign, 2 declined, 3 stayed flat and 2 rose (Q-014-13).
  - *Fact, one scoping review:* "Active change interventions were associated with the greatest likelihood of de-adoption" (Q-014-12).
  - *Fact, a company's self-report:* Zalando's first technology radar was easy to ignore without a formal process (Q-014-18).
  - *Opinion, a consultancy's audit summaries at two sites:* formally close out changes, set a time limit on temporary ones, and review all open ones routinely (Q-014-4; pattern Q-014-P22, whose transfer to a small AI-build organisation is untested).
  - *Conditions:* doctors' practice, a large company, chemical plants.
  - *Transfer:* partial. A one-owner team has fewer people to persuade, but the same need to remove what no longer helps.
  - *May mean here:* each kept method needs a set review, and the screening needs a set rhythm.
- **Cheap alerts found less than a full search, in one test.**
  - *Fact, one prospective study of one review:* each of two cheap alerts found 1 of the 4 new trials a full search found; the four new trials "did not alter the results nor certainty in the evidence" (Q-012-15).
  - *Fact, one study of two vendors:* two threat-indicator feeds overlapped by "only 2.5% to 4.0%". This is an overlap, not a miss rate (Q-012-17).
  - *Conditions:* medical literature searching; security indicator feeds.
  - *Transfer:* weak. These show sources differ, not that a missed item matters (pattern Q-012-P1).
  - *May mean here:* use more than one source, and count what each finds.

# Part 2 — The intent

## Intent
The owner's purpose, preferences and trade-offs for this project, and what they would hate. Drafted by
the Chief of Staff from conversations, portfolio defaults and past rulings; confirmed on the
move-to-Design card.

*Draft, for the owner to confirm on the move-to-Design card. Each line cites the record of the owner's words.*

- **Purpose.** "a methodology for screening the current cutting edge of AI and Vibe coding, identifying the practices that prove most successful in outputting business ready products, and develop a pipeline to integrating those back into the operating model and ultimately service desk" (portfolio ideas/IDEAS.md, 2026-10-15).
- **Order.** The prompt engine first, then this: "first the prompting idea, then the cutting edge idea" (portfolio ideas/IDEAS.md, 2026-11-01).
- **Full cycle, then into the model.** "I want them to go through the full operating model cycle, but then I want them integrated into the operating model completely" (portfolio ideas/IDEAS.md, 2026-11-01).
- **One place for the result.** The owner does not want "6 different repos all feeding information into the same workflow"; the result moves fully into the operating model repository, and Model-Lab is archived once both ideas are in (decisions/questions/P-001-work-repo.md).
- **Beside the prompt engine, not over it.** "Do not disturb the prompt engine's running work" (the owner's first message to this session, 2026-11-08, session_01QBwHGNSLmSrYQgS6BPjV8p); the route that keeps it so (decisions/questions/P-002-route.md).
- **Standing preferences carried from the owner's rulings in this repository and the desk**, the Chief of Staff's summary, each to be confirmed: questions at natural points, each filed and checked by the Critic before it is asked; every slip in a lessons log in the session it is found (decisions/questions/P-001-lessons-log.md); no separate status pages once a project is on the desk.
- **Deliberate screening, as a standing pipeline.** "Look deliberately". "In my mind it works like this: Feeds set up that target preminent sites/sources/content - those feeds are read by screeners who derive enough information from the feeds to establish automated research prompts that get kicked off and then proposed to build or not. If accepted they become a standard part of the operating model and/or service desk" (decisions/questions/P-002-groundwork-shortlist.md, 1 and 2).
- **Both risks weigh the same.** Missing a helpful change and adopting a harmful one: "Both equally" (same record, 3).
- **Ready for real business use.** All three: "I rely on it daily, Others use or pay, Passes a checklist" (same record, 4). The owner later said "I think your success question was answered incorrectly" and set the aim in the next line (decisions/questions/P-002-target.md).
- **What it makes cutting edge: running and optimising the multi-agent system.** "I don’t want it exclusively the operating model. I’m really looking for how multi agent systems are run and optimized. For example, when anthropic says “90% of their work is performed by Claude” I want to achieve that level of agentic efficiency, trust, success in the strategy ideation and coding work from my operating model" (decisions/questions/P-002-target.md).
- **Promising, not only credentialed.** "Cutting edge requires a little validation and testing and faith"; "Faith to me meant that we test/adopt methods that are promising rather than only those that are fully credentialed" (same record).
- **The desk is open to improvement, never updated automatically.** "The desk should absolutely be subject to potential  changes. I dont want the desk to automatically get updated, but none of our architecture is immune from proposals to improve." (same record, 5).
- **Outside tools only if no data leaves.** Agents may try outside tools, prompts or code: "Yes, no data out" (same record, 6).
- **Separate from the prompt engine, which it could have proposed.** "They should stay separate - in a sense the prompt engine could be seen as a proposal from the screening tool." (same record, 7).
- **Time and money.** "Set at Design gate" (same record, 8).
