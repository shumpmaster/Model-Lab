Open question 9 (specs/S-002.md:257):
> 9. **A piece in two entries, or two pieces in one:** a piece handed out twice is counted once, at its first entry, and its second entry is dropped; when the work of two handed-out pieces merges as one pull request, it is counted once, at the first piece's place and on its side, and the later entry is dropped. Each dropped entry's place is refilled, as a withdrawal's is, from the same three spare places of the look, on the same side, so each side keeps 30. Withdrawals and these dropped entries share one limit of three a look; a fourth of any of them stops the trial and brings it to you. A place is left empty only if a dropped entry is not logged by the time the look is judged, at a fourth, or if a spare place opened to refill a drop or a withdrawal gets no new piece within 60 days; while a spare place waits for its piece, the look waits too, for at most those 60 days (this plan's figure, the same span after which a handed-out piece still unmerged is settled). The other choice would be for the look to wait for the spare place however long the kind's pace takes, which leaves no place empty this way but sets no end to the wait. When a place is left empty, the look is judged on the pieces it has, and also with each empty place filled in every way, and it decides only if every way gives the same result; otherwise the trial stops and comes to you. A piece that merges in two pull requests is counted once, at its first merge, and one that merges as records only is counted on its side all the same. Whoever hands out the work could merge two pieces or hand one out twice on purpose, to move which side later pieces fall on; the refill and the limit cap this, but do not catch it. Keep this rule, or another?

Risks: the sides are known before pieces arrive (the fold) (specs/S-002.md:235):
> - **The sides are known before pieces arrive.** The sides of places 1 to 60 are fixed in the frozen trial record so that no one can steer a draw, and a reserve place's side is known once its withdrawal or void line is logged; so the session that dispatches can know the next place's side, and could choose which brief takes that place or delay a dispatch, or merge two entered briefs into one pull request or enter a brief twice, voiding an entry of its choosing, or fold an entered brief's work into a pull request that carries another brief's work, whose owner questions after the entered brief's dispatch then count on the trial piece, its window starting there ("What a touch is"; J2); and the Orchestrator, which knows the sides, chooses when, within the 7 days, it starts a withdrawal check or writes a void line, which decides which later piece takes the reserve place the line opens and which later pieces fall outside the look's weeks. Mitigation, in part: eligibility is by the brief's role alone (K4), every eligible piece dispatched in a look's weeks is entered in dispatch order, and the Reviewer checks each look's entries against the brief records' times and lists any eligible piece with no entry. These stop a dispatched piece being skipped or entered out of order; a void entry's place is refilled on its side, and void entries share the limit of 3 a look with withdrawals; they do not catch a choice of which brief to dispatch next, a dispatch delayed on purpose, a merge of two entered briefs into one pull request, a brief entered twice on purpose, a fold of an entered brief's work into a pull request that carries another brief's work, which voids no entry, is not capped and is only listed, or the time within the 7 days at which a withdrawal check is started or a void line is written, of which the Reviewer lists only a line that is late or missing; the hold-up checks catch them only in part.
