## What I could not find or open, by part of the question

- **Is looking worth it at all:** I found no study that compares looking for outside AI or agent practice with not looking, in any field of AI development. The evidence is indirect, from other fields: innovation search (F011-1, F011-2), guideline implementation (F011-3) and copy-paste harm (F011-4). What each counted as paying: innovation output (F011-1), technical performance (F011-2), process-of-care indicators (F011-3), and insecure code, i.e. harm (F011-4). Cost of looking: measured only as systematic-review time (F011-30). Grimshaw notes that cost data are mostly unreported. For AI work, unknown. The Katz & Allen abstract was not opened, and the Axios 2025 and BMJ Open pages returned 403.
- **Held up, vendor:** one opened case (F011-11). There is no systematic rate. The vendor-ROI search surfaced only consultancy blogs with their own stake, and no study of how often vendor claims replicate.
- **Held up, practitioners:** F011-9 and F011-10 are independent tests of popular tips, and most showed no effect. There is no systematic rate for practitioner accounts in AI development. I also found no study comparing practitioner blog claims with academic evidence.
- **Held up, academic and industry studies:** SE gives 26% confirmatory for external replications (F011-6, secondary); ML gives about 63% (F011-8, one replicator, old papers). I found no rate specific to recent LLM or agent papers. Shepperd & Counsell's 8.64 odds ratio was not opened (403).
- **Held up, open-source projects:** nothing measures how often open-source projects' claims hold up. F011-8 (code is a weak indicator) and F011-29 are adjacent. The fake-star work is excluded as already in the library.
- **Held up, benchmarks and leaderboards:** F011-12 to F011-14 give flaw and inflation sizes, not a hold-up rate. Arena and SWE-bench memorisation are excluded as already in the library.
- **Share promotional:** nothing measured. The only adjacent evidence is sponsorship bias in medicine (F011-16).
- **Share duplicated:** nothing measured. The only adjacent evidence is obsolescence (F011-22) and link rot (F011-23).
- **Share machine-written:** web articles about 50% (F011-17, F011-18), Medium 40–47% (F011-19), CS abstracts about 17.5% by Feb 2024 (F011-21). All rest on detectors, whose reliability is disputed (F011-20). I found no figure specific to AI-engineering blogs or documentation.
- **How others filtered:** F011-24, F011-25, F011-27 and F011-31 are methods, but none measures whether filtering improved outcomes. The Thoughtworks Radar method is already in the library.
