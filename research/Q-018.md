# Research question — Q-018

## The question

A small organisation does its work through AI agents, each started for one task, and one person, the owner, who reviews, decides and merges. Its work is kept in three git repositories, Model-Lab, Service-Desk and Personal-Org-Operating-Model. The rules below say what counts as a piece of work, a touch by the owner, a first independent check and a reopening, and which kind of work a piece is. Apply them, from the extract alone, to every pull request and commit in the drawn sample, and report each result.

The extract is in /home/user/Model-Lab/research/Q-018-extract/. Its README.md says how it was made and what each field means; extract.py is the script that made it. sample.json names the drawn sample, and sample.jsonl holds the extract's line for each drawn pull request or commit (the same lines as in the <repo>.<frame>.jsonl files), with each drawn pull request's comment and review fields from GitHub (README, "GitHub's records"); brief 294 widened it with the fields the corrected rules need, and re-coded the sample as drawn (README, "The widened fields").

For every drawn pull request and commit, give its result under each rule: piece or not (RP1, RP2); its kind and the rule that gave it (K1 to K3; K4 applies to trials only); each touch found, with its rule and the record it rests on (RT1 to RT6; RT7 and the touch-log parts RT1(c), RT2(b) and RT3(c) find nothing in past records); its first check (C1); reopened, not reopened or pending (RR1 to RR3); and any rule that is "untested" and why. List every case you could not decide from the extract, and why. List the sampled awkward cases seen and not seen, from sample.json's edge-case part. Reopening is tested by RR1(a) and the E-RR2 candidates only: the `Reopens:` mark (RR1(b)) cannot exist on past pieces, and the short reads (RR1(c)) are for trial pieces.

Report every result on equal terms; whether any count or share is large or small is not for this memo to judge.

## The rules (specs/S-002.md at ae772cb, verbatim, from "How rules and terms are named" to the end of the line holding E-RR1 to E-RR3, and the edge-case table, with the three parentheses giving Q-017's counts, in E-RP1, E-K1 and E-RT3, left out)

**How rules and terms are named.** The counting rules have ids of their own: K1 to K4 (path classes and kinds), RP1 and RP2 (pieces), RT1 to RT7 (touches), C1 (the first independent check) and RR1 to RR3 (reopenings); their awkward cases are E-RP1 to E-RP6, E-K1 to E-K3, E-RT1 to E-RT6 and E-RR1 to E-RR3. In this spec a ruled term of docs/gates/P-002/trial-terms.md is always written "ruled T<n>" or "ruled P<n>", and a risk of the pre-mortem "pre-mortem R<n>".

**Path classes (rule K1).** Every changed file is put in one class by its real path, by research/Q-017-memo.md §1's twelve path rules, in that order, with three changes: (i) a question or decision file is one under a top-level `questions/` or `decisions/` folder only (the memo's labels went by folder name at any depth and so caught test and template copies, §3 and §5); (ii) in the Personal-Org-Operating-Model repository, the operating model's own text (its OPERATING_MODEL.md and playbook files) is built work, since it is that repository's product; (iii) nothing else changes. The memo's rules 1 to 3 are written in labels; K1 reads each label as the real paths that research/Q-017-extract/README.md, "Labels in place of paths", gives that label, with change (i) applied to rule 1. The classes are built, plan, research, records, owner question or answer, and other.

**What a piece is.**
- **RP1.** A pull request merged into the default branch of Personal-Org-Operating-Model, Model-Lab or Service-Desk, as GitHub's record of the pull request says (so a squash or rebase merge counts, though the commit extract coded only two-parent merges, research/Q-017-extract/README.md).
- **RP2.** It is a piece when at least one changed file is built, plan or research (K1). When every changed file is records, owner question or answer, or other, it is not a piece (ruled T1).
- *E-RP1:* work that lands by a local merge or a direct push, with no pull request: not a piece; the count gives their number per month apart, as work the measure cannot see.
- *E-RP2:* a pull request closed without merging: not a piece; an owner's close can be a send-back of the piece that later carries the work (RT3(b)).
- *E-RP3:* rule and guidance prose outside the operating model repository (AGENTS.md, readmes, governance prose: K1 "other"): not a piece.
- *E-RP4:* one pull request carrying the work of several briefs: one piece.
- *E-RP5:* a pull request merged by a workflow bot that changes built, plan or research files: a piece; the bot's merge is not a touch.
- *E-RP6:* a revert merged as a pull request: a piece of its own, and a reopening of the piece it reverts (RR1(a)).

**Which kind a piece is (U3: a piece that touches several kinds).**
- **K2.** A piece has one kind: the kind of the brief that dispatched its work, by the role dispatched alone, as the brief record's name or the `Agent-Session:` trailer gives it: Builder, Check author, tools-builder or release → built work; Definer → plans; Researcher, Source checker or Input reader → research. A brief to the Chief of Staff or the Orchestrator, or to any role not in this list, is not mapped by K2, since its kind would depend on what the brief says, which the count does not read; K3 gives its kind.
- **K2's short forms.** A trailer may give its role in a short form between hyphens. K2 reads the short forms below as their roles, whether or not the brief record the trailer names exists (a piece with no brief record takes its dispatch time from its first commit, "What a touch is"); a short form not in this table is not read as a role, and K3 applies, listed.

| Short form in the trailer | Role | Seen in the records |
|---|---|---|
| `-def-` | Definer (plans) | Service-Desk, `session_<id>-def-s001` |
| `-tb-` | tools-builder (built work) | Personal-Org-Operating-Model, `session_<id>-tb-s017` to `-tb-s019` |

- **K3.** A piece with no brief, with a brief K2 does not map, or whose briefs are of more than one kind, has the kind with the most changed lines among its built, plan and research files (K1); a tie goes to built work, then plans.
- **K4.** For a trial, the kind is fixed at the draw from the brief's role by K2, and does not change with what the piece's files turn out to be; a piece whose merged files are mostly of another kind keeps its kind and is listed. A piece whose kind K2 cannot give at dispatch (a Chief of Staff or Orchestrator brief, a role not in K2's list, or no brief) is not eligible for a trial, since its kind is not known when its side is drawn.
- So a piece that touches several kinds counts once, under one kind, for the count by kind and for a trial; it is eligible only for a trial of that kind. V1's own figures count all kinds together (ruled T32), so the kind rule changes no figure of V1.
- *E-K1:* a piece touching several kinds. *E-K2:* a brief role not in K2's list, the Chief of Staff's and the Orchestrator's included (K3 applies, listed). *E-K3:* Service-Desk's scripted research, dispatched by control files, not briefs (research by K2, the Researcher's role).

**What a touch is.** A touch counts on a piece when it falls inside the piece's window: after its dispatch, not at it, and before its merge (the window excludes its start). A piece's dispatch is the commit time of the first commit that adds its brief record (the file that holds the brief whose number the piece's `Agent-Session:` trailers name: in Model-Lab `docs/briefs/<n>-<role>.md`; in the other two repositories the file their briefs or control files are kept in, which the extract's README states and the Critic checks against the repositories before the sample is drawn, step 1 below); with no brief record, the author time of the piece's first commit, which is, here and below, the piece's own commit with the earliest author time. A record **names a piece** when its text holds, as a whole word or path, `#<n>`, `PR <n>` or `pull request <n>` for the piece's pull request number n; `brief <b>` or `briefs/<b>-` for a brief b whose work the piece carries; or the spec id that the piece's `Spec:` trailers give, except in Service-Desk, where nearly every `Spec:` trailer gives S-001, so that id names no one piece there (this exclusion is kept in place of a general one, that a spec id in the `Spec:` trailers of more than one piece under way at the same time names none, which would need a field the extract does not have). A question file or decision record that the piece's own commits add or change also names the piece. A record that names the piece is never "a question about another piece" (RT4), even when its text is about other work; until the owner rules on Open question 11, each record that names the piece only by being one of its own files, and whose text is about other work, is counted and listed. A record that cites the path of another file the piece changes (a shared plan, spec or project file) does not name the piece by that path. A question file or decision record is read as the commit that first adds it to the default branch left it, whether or not it is still there at the cut-off (a question file deleted when its answer was recorded is read so), and each later commit's added lines as that commit adds them. A record whose text as first added does not name the piece names it from the first later commit whose added lines do: that append, and each later one, is read for the piece; the record as first added, and the appends before that one, are not.
- **RT1, a question.** (a) A question put to the owner, as a question file (under top-level `questions/`) or in conversation as a decision record (under top-level `decisions/`), counted once per question (Q-018/U4): a question file `questions/<name>.md` and the decision record of the same name, `decisions/questions/<name>.md`, are one question; a decision record with no question file (in Service-Desk also one under `decisions/<item>/`) is one question; and each later commit that adds the owner's words to an existing decision record (an append, such as an answer to a follow-up question or a further ruling) is one more. It counts on a piece when its record names the piece and, inside the piece's window, its question file is first added or the owner's words are added to its decision record, appends included; its time is the commit time of the first such commit in the window, so a question filed before the dispatch and answered after it counts at its answer. Each record and each append is timed by the commit that adds it, never by a date its text gives. For a piece whose dispatch is its first commit (one with no brief record), a record first added in that commit is, as first added, the ruling that started the piece (RT4); a later commit that adds the owner's words to it is an append, and counts. For a piece dispatched by its brief record, a record first added in its first commit is timed by that commit like any other, and one added at the dispatch is outside the window. Not counted under (a): a file whose name ends `-notes.md` (a role's notes on a ruling); a decision record or append that adds no owner words (no `Ruling:`, `Answer:` or `Decision:` line, and no quoted line under a line naming the owner's words, answer, note or ruling), such as a role's verdict recorded verbatim; and a ruling RT5 puts outside the count. Where the records cannot show whether a record or an append holds the owner's words, or which piece it is about, the touch log decides (RT1(c)) and the case is listed; (b) any comment by the owner on the pull request, or a review the owner left as "commented", before merging, counted without its text being kept. A comment or review under the owner's account that carries an agent mark, or that a session's records show it posted (such as a session record naming "the reviewer comment(s) on PR #n" among its returns), is the agent's, not the owner's, and is not a touch (E-RT1). The agent marks are these, and no others until a revision adds one: the footer line `_Generated by [Claude Code]_`, the only footer the Critic's reading of comments has found (on all 8 comments under the owner's account that its reading covered, made before merging on Service-Desk #11 and #78 and Personal-Org-Operating-Model #18, #30 and #35, reviews/Q-018/_critic-verbatim.md, finding 3; the comments before merging on Service-Desk #6 and Personal-Org-Operating-Model #11, and those on frame U's pull requests, research/Q-018-extract/github_reads.json, were not in that reading); and a link to an agent session, `claude.ai/code/session_<id>`. A comment with neither mark and no session record counts as the owner's only in a repository whose agent comments the Critic's reading has shown to carry the footer: Service-Desk and Personal-Org-Operating-Model. In Model-Lab, where no comment has been read (its sampled pull requests have none, research/Q-018-extract/github_reads.json), and in any repository where the marks cannot be read, the owner's comments cannot be told apart from an agent's, and RT1(b) is counted from the touch log instead (step 6), with the comments listed beside it, until a reading of that repository's agent comments shows the footer; (c) a question in the touch log (U1's fallback, below) that names the piece.
- **RT2, a fix.** (a) A commit in the pull request under the owner's identity that changes a built, plan or research file, when it was committed through GitHub's web editor or carries no `Agent-Session:` trailer; (b) a fix in the touch log.
- **RT3, a send-back.** (a) A review by the owner asking for changes; (b) a pull request the owner closed unmerged whose work later merges in another pull request (the same head branch, or a brief number in both's `Agent-Session:` trailers), closed before the later one merges, which counts on the later piece; (c) a send-back in the touch log.

Build review: round 1 FAIL (…)`). A record that names an earlier round and gives none of these wordings gives no round-1 verdict. A round in parts is FAIL if any part is FAIL and PASS if every part is PASS. Over a piece's records of its kind (two reviewers of one plan, the build review of each of its briefs, the checks of each of its research questions), the piece's first check is FAIL if any record's round 1 is FAIL, and PASS if every record that gives a round-1 verdict is PASS; if none gives one, the result is "undecided, listed" (Q-018/U3).
- **RR1.** A piece is reopened for a fault when, within 30 days of its merge, (a) it is reverted, in whole or in part, by a merged commit whose subject starts "Revert" and whose message names one of the piece's commits after "This reverts commit", or whose subject names the piece's pull request number; (b) the reviewer of a later change marks, in that change's review record, the line `Reopens: <repository>#<pull request number>` for a fault the later change fixes (a reviewer, never the piece's maker, ruled T25); or (c) for a trial piece, the owner would not have accepted it on a short read (ruled P14).
- **RR2.** Not reopenings: later work that builds on the piece; a change made because the owner ruled something new; a records update.
- **RR3.** A reopening found after 30 days does not count. A piece whose 30 days have not passed is pending, never counted as held up.
- *E-RR1:* a revert not worded "Revert" (Q-017 §4: such reverts are not found): caught only by RR1(b). *E-RR2:* a later pull request that shares a file or names the number (Q-017 §4's coarse tests): not a reopening by itself; the hand check lists them as candidates. *E-RR3:* a fix that names nothing it fixes: caught only by RR1(b).

- "pre-mortem R<n>" in the naming sentence: how the spec names a risk from the project's pre-mortem, its list of the likeliest reasons the project could fail. The quoted lines name none.
