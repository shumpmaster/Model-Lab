# Research question — Q-005

## The question

In fields that pass work between people or teams through written instructions (for example, clinical handover, surgical and aviation checklists, military orders, software runbooks, standard operating procedures), under what conditions did written procedures improve outcomes, make no difference, or cause harm? How did those fields decide when to add, change or retire a written rule, and what did they find about rules added in response to single incidents? For each finding, what conditions does it depend on that may or may not hold for AI agents?

The pipeline under study is the operating model in shumpmaster/Personal-Org-Operating-Model (OPERATING_MODEL.md; role definitions in template-v3/.claude/agents/; briefs and packs in template-v3/governance/BRIEFS.toml and PACKS.toml). Treat it as the subject being studied, not as evidence of what works.

## The decision it serves

The Discovery of P-001, the prompt engine (a project on how the written instructions that pass work between the agents of that operating model are written, checked and changed): which principles from other fields transfer, under which conditions, and what limits they set on how instructions are added to and retired.

## What each answer would change

Principles that transfer shape how instructions are written and changed; those whose conditions don't hold are set aside with the reason.
