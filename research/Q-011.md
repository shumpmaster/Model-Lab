# Research question — Q-011

## The question

Is deliberately looking for outside practice in building with AI models and agents worth doing at all, compared with not doing it? And where it is done, how often have claims from each kind of source held up when someone independent of the claimant tested them (vendor documentation and studies, practitioners' accounts, academic and industry studies, open-source projects, benchmarks and leaderboards)? Here "held up" means an independent test found an effect in the same direction. Separately, what share of each kind is promotional (written to sell), duplicated or machine-written, and how have others filtered such material? For "worth doing", report what each study counted as paying (for example better outcomes, fewer failures or saved time) and what the looking cost. Say "unknown" where nothing measured exists. This question owns whether looking at all is worth it; how often to look, and how proposals are judged, are separate questions.

Facts already in the portfolio library (/home/user/portfolio/library/, index in README.md) need not be re-researched: entries Q-009-F3-4 to F3-7 and Q-009-F12-1.

## The decision it serves

Whether to look for outside practice at all; and if so, which kinds of source to read and how to weigh their claims.

## What each answer would change

Evidence that looking adds little over not looking argues for not doing it; evidence that it pays argues for doing it. Kinds of source with a poor record are read with a higher bar or not at all, kinds with a good record more cheaply; the share of promotional or machine-written material sets the cost of filtering.
