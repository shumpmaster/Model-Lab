# Research question — Q-014

## The question

What have organisations that deliberately scan outside practice, and adopt or decline it, found: benefit, no benefit or harm? Cover for example technology radars, health technology assessment, standards bodies, and engineering and process-safety change control. How were proposals judged, including proposals to change something already in use; how were they approved; what did adopting, and what did declining, a proposal cost; how were adopted practices later reviewed and retired; and when, if ever, did an organisation stop scanning, and why?

This question owns judging, approving, declining, retiring and stopping; how often to scan, and whether scanning is worth it at all, are separate questions. Facts already in the portfolio library (/home/user/portfolio/library/, index in README.md) need not be re-researched: entries Q-009-F7-7, Q-009-F13-1 to F13-3, F13-8, F13-9, F13-11 and F13-12.

## The decision it serves

How proposals from any scanning are judged and approved, how a change to something already in use is decided, and the rules for declining, retiring and stopping.

## What each answer would change

Patterns that held up elsewhere become candidates; documented harms become things to avoid; finding no benefit, or a high cost of declining, weighs on whether and how to scan; retirement and stopping rules that worked, or failed, inform any such rules adopted.
