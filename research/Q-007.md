# Research question — Q-007

## The question

In multi-step pipelines of language-model agents, what is known about (a) information lost or distorted when work is handed from one role to the next, and how the design of what each role is shown affects it; (b) instructions being overridden by content in the inputs, ignored, gamed, or followed too literally; and (c) the benefits and the costs of changing instructions in response to individual failures, compared with changing them on a schedule or not at all?

The pipeline under study is the operating model in shumpmaster/Personal-Org-Operating-Model (OPERATING_MODEL.md; role definitions in template-v3/.claude/agents/; briefs and packs in template-v3/governance/BRIEFS.toml and PACKS.toml). Treat it as the subject being studied, not as evidence of what works.

## The decision it serves

The Discovery of P-001, the prompt engine (a project on how the written instructions that pass work between the agents of that operating model are written, checked and changed): how instructions and what each role is shown are designed and changed, and what safeguards a change must keep (the model's rules on what each role must not see, and on untrusted text).

## What each answer would change

Known handoff losses set what a brief must carry; known override and gaming risks set what a change must be tested against; the evidence on changing after failures sets the project's rule for when to change.
