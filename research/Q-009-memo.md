## Design traps to avoid
- **Taking a vendor's own study as independent evidence.** Most quantitative claims gathered here come from parties with a stake. (C1, L2)
- **Treating "passes the checks" as "ready".** Test-passing agent work is often unmergeable, and gaming is documented. (B2, L4)
- **Re-researching what the instruction-measuring effort has already filed,** or both efforts changing the same instructions at once so that neither can read its effect. (H1–H3)
- **Adopting several changes in one batch,** with no record of which helped. (G5, H3)
- **Growing the role files with every adoption.** Compliance falls with instruction count, and critic.md:14 already carries a dated exception. Each adoption needs a retirement rule. (G1, G2, G8)
- **Adopting faster than the owner can approve.** Rule changes are R3 and never delegable, and the method already changes version weekly. (A6, G6, L3)
- **Carrying a practice over from human teams, another model or another product type** without checking the conditions it depends on. (F1–F4)
- **Using popularity as a proxy** (stars, leaderboards, how often a practice is mentioned). (C2, C3, L1)
- **Letting the screening role read outside text directly.** The template researcher.md lists web tools against the rule that only the Input reader reads untrusted text. Adopted prompts are themselves untrusted text. (J1, K1, K2, K9)
- **Adopting outside tools while the data-class table and the spend-ceiling field are unfilled.** (K6, J5)
- **Findings with no expiry,** where the evidence turns over in months. (E1–E3)
- **Pushing a method change silently into the product in use,** which has no route for it by default and, at T1, no canary or rollback requirement. (I1–I3, B6)
- **Using impressions as measurement,** the owner's or the agents'. (A5)
- **Adding a practice to fix a failure that rarely happens.** Measure the base rate first. (D2)
- **A screening with no "decline" outcome.** That is pro-innovation bias built into the process. (L1, M2)
