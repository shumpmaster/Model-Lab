# Research question — Q-015

## The question

How are systems of several AI agents, working with one or a few human supervisors, organised and run in practice for planning, strategy and software work, and what is reported for and against each way of doing it? Cover, evenly:
- how work is divided between agents, and between agents and people;
- how much each agent may do without a person's approval, and how that is decided and changed;
- how work is handed from one agent to the next;
- how agents' work is checked, whether by other agents, by tests or by people;
- what agents are allowed to reach (tools, data, permissions), and how that is controlled;
- how the instructions, tools and memory agents work with are kept and changed;
- how failures and incidents are found, recorded, traced and fixed;
- what it costs to run, in money and in time.

For each way of doing it, report:
- who reports it (the maker or seller of a tool or method, a practitioner, an academic study or an independent test), with any stake the source has, and whether anyone outside the source has checked it;
- the kind of work, the scale and how much human oversight it had;
- what was claimed, and what was measured;
- its costs, limits, failure modes and reported incidents, and the conditions outside which it was not tested, asked for as fully as its claims;
- how it compared, where compared, with a single agent, with people working without agents, or with a simpler setup, including where it did no better or worse;
- whether it is still in use where it was reported.

Methods reported only by their makers belong in the answer, marked as such, with the same questions asked of them; a method's being new is not a reason to leave it out, nor a reason to include it.

This question owns the ways of running and checking agents. How the share of work agents do, the trust in it and whether it holds up are measured, including review and override rates and the rates of failures and defects, belongs to Q-016.

Facts already in the portfolio library (/home/user/portfolio/library/, index in README.md) need not be re-researched; in particular the entries from Q-002 (agent self-correction, model judges), Q-003 (judging), Q-005 and Q-007 (handovers between workers and agents), Q-006 and Q-008 (one pipeline's own records), and Q-009.

## The decision it serves

For one small organisation of agents supervised by one person: which ways of running a system of AI agents to set aside, because their conditions do not hold or their record is poor, and which, if any, to try.

## What each answer would change

A way found with its conditions holding could be tried; one whose conditions do not hold, or whose record shows it no better than a simpler setup, is set aside and recorded; areas where nothing is reported are stated as gaps.
