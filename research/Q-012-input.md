**What I could not find or could not open, by part of the question**
- **Shelf life:** I found no study measuring how fast findings about practice with AI models or agents lose validity, beyond model-drift studies (F012-3, F012-4, F012-6) and benchmark saturation (F012-5). The decay estimates I found come from medicine (F012-1, F012-12) and Stack Overflow (F012-2), not agent practice.
- **No deliberate review:** I found no study of what organisations doing AI work lose by never reviewing. The evidence is indirect (F012-2, F012-7).
- **One-off review:** I found no evaluation of a one-off review's yield. Only its cost is evidenced (F012-8 opened; F012-9 search-only).
- **Scheduled review:** All the NICE pages, the PMG49 surveillance guide, and Newberry 2013 (the AHRQ surveillance programme's cost and yield) were blocked (403 or captcha). I found no study comparing the yield of different fixed intervals outside medicine.
- **Event-triggered review:** I could not open the OpenAI postmortem, the FAA airworthiness directive rules (redirected) or Garner 2016 (the BMJ update checklist). The evidence that event triggers catch or miss things is thin; F012-15 is a small pilot.
- **Continuous intake:** I found no figures for what it costs to produce a living review or guideline. Only the Australian Living Evidence Consortium's own benefit estimate turned up (search-only, from its funder, not included above). Packer 2012 on 10 years of horizon scanning: abstract results not available.
- **Reading by people, tools or both:** I found nothing on keeping up with outside practice in software or AI specifically, beyond evaluation advice. Practitioner accounts (Hamel Husain and others) came up only as secondary summaries, with no cadence stated. The medRxiv full texts (otto-SR, the dual-model ensemble) were blocked. I found no direct head-to-head comparison of people, tools and both for surveillance, beyond LOCATE (F012-21) and the screening studies.
- **Balance of the record:** Most of the cost and failure evidence comes from medicine and evidence synthesis. The AI-specific sources are mostly 2025–2026 preprints.
