# Research question — Q-003

## The question

How is "good work" from an agent session defined and judged in practice, and how reliable are model-run checkers (reviewers, critics, graders) as the yardstick, including when they share a model family with the work? What methods detect a real change in a pipeline's outcomes when only tens of runs are affordable (for example, paired comparisons on replayed tasks, repeated trials, clustered errors, power analysis), what sample sizes do they need, and how can repeated runs be designed so they are not re-running for a better answer? Include the case where measurement at that scale cannot separate a change from noise.

The pipeline under study is the operating model in shumpmaster/Personal-Org-Operating-Model (OPERATING_MODEL.md; role definitions in template-v3/.claude/agents/; briefs and packs in template-v3/governance/BRIEFS.toml and PACKS.toml). Treat it as the subject being studied, not as evidence of what works.

## The decision it serves

The Discovery of P-001, the prompt engine (a project on how the written instructions that pass work between the agents of that operating model are written, checked and changed): whether and how the project measures the effect of a change to instructions, what counts as the outcome, who or what judges it, and how many runs a test needs within the owner's limit of about 100 test runs.

## What each answer would change

A workable method sets the experiment's design and baseline; an unreliable yardstick changes who judges; infeasible measurement at this scale shifts the project towards other evidence or a smaller claim.
