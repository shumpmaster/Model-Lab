# Research question — Q-017

*Drafted by the Chief of Staff; revised after the Critic's round 1 (reviews/P-002-research-q017/critic.md). Checked by the Critic for gaps and neutral phrasing (D-032, D-055).*

## The question

A small organisation does its work through AI agents, each started for one task, and one person, the owner, who reviews, decides and merges. Its work is kept in three git repositories, available locally at /home/user/Service-Desk, /home/user/Model-Lab and /home/user/Personal-Org-Operating-Model (on GitHub as shumpmaster/Service-Desk, shumpmaster/Model-Lab and shumpmaster/Personal-Org-Operating-Model). The commit history of each, as of 2026-11-09, is given to you as an extract in /home/user/Model-Lab/research/Q-017-extract/ (one file of commits and one of merged pull requests per repository; its README says how it was made and what each field means). Count, from the extract and the repositories' files, who made and merged the changes, how often the owner was asked to decide, how many rounds of checking each piece of checked work took, and how often merged changes were later reverted or changed again. Give every count at both ends (for example, with and without a commit by the owner).

1. **Units.** Count merged pull requests, per repository and per month of merged_at (UTC), from <repo>.prs.jsonl. Class each by kind from the paths it changes, with a stated rule: built work (code, tests, tools), a plan or spec, research, records only, an owner question or answer (paths labelled <questions:…> or <decisions:…>), or other; a pull request that fits two kinds is counted under each and flagged. Count separately the commits on the default branch's first-parent line that belong to no merged pull request. Give the number of changed lines as a second view beside each count.

2. **Who made and merged each change.**
   - Identities: the extract classes each commit's author as agent, owner or other, and gives author and committer names. List the names in each class, and say how you treat the "other" names.
   - For each merged pull request, from prs.jsonl: its commits by author class (commit_author_classes), its co-author classes, and the author of its merge commit, which for a merge is the account that merged it. For a squash the merging account is not recorded, and the commit's author is the pull request's author: count squashes separately and say what they can and cannot show. Give the shares at both ends (with and without a commit by the owner's identity, and with each kind of merging account), and the same counts at commit level.
   - Limit: an identity is not proof of who did the work. An agent session may commit under the owner's git settings, and work from before the agents.invalid convention began carries another identity. Say from what date the convention appears in each repository.

3. **Questions put to the owner.** The number of files first added with a <questions:…> label, and the number first added with a <decisions:…> label, per month and per merged pull request, from commits.jsonl and prs.jsonl. A question and its answer have different labels, so say whether and how they can be matched (for example by a pull request that adds the answer and removes the question); where they cannot, say so. Limit: a question file does not prove the owner was asked, nor an answer file who answered, and decisions given in conversation leave no file.

4. **Rounds of checking.** A *checker* is the independent role that judges a piece: the reviewer of a plan or of built work, the source checker of a research memo (which opens each source it cites), or the critic of a question put to the owner; a *gate case* is the case put to the owner for moving a project to its next phase. This is a recount on the same records as two earlier counts (research/Q-006-memo.md and research/Q-008-memo.md), extended to 2026-11-09 and linked to merged pull requests. Use these definitions:
   - a *piece* is one item sent to one kind of independent check: a plan to its review, a research memo to its source check, built work to its reviewer, an owner question to its critic, or a gate case;
   - a *round* is one recorded verdict on a piece by its checker; a round is identified by piece, checker and date, so a verdict recorded both in a pull request's verdict file and in a series round file counts once;
   - *passed at the first try* means the piece's first recorded verdict is a pass;
   - a piece *reached the stop rule* when three verdicts in a row on it are fails; any check after that (a focused, confirmation or deciding check) is counted as a round but reported separately;
   - a check added later to a piece that already passed counts as a new round of that piece, reported separately; a piece with no recorded verdict is counted separately.

   Records of rounds: the verdict files under reviews/, the round sections of research/*-source-check.md and research/*-entries-check.md, and Service-Desk's dispatch-log/, which for scripted items is the only per-round record, since their source-check files keep only the last verdict. Date each round from its verdict file's own text, or, where the text gives no date, from the commit date of the file in commits.jsonl. Give pieces, first-try passes, rounds per piece, and pieces that reached the stop rule, per kind. Say where your figures differ from the two earlier counts, and which record decides it; open those two memos only for that comparison, after your own counts are made.

5. **Merged changes later reverted or changed again.** For each merged pull request, whether a later commit is marked revert or names it (mentions_prs), or a later merged pull request changes the same files, within 14, 30 and 60 days of its merged_at. Same files is a coarse test, since the extract has no line-level history; say so, and report the reverts and the naming separately from file overlap. Leave out files that are edited in turn by design (logs, ledgers, digests, generated files, and the session and dispatch records), and list the files left out. Report separately pull requests merged less than 60 days before 2026-11-09, whose windows are not complete. This counts later changes; it does not judge whether the earlier change was at fault.

6. **What the records cannot show.** For example: decisions and approvals given in conversation; the owner's time; work dropped before any record; fixes that do not name what they fix; files with no recorded verdict; gaps in the dispatch log; and the limits named above.

Report every figure on equal terms; whether any figure is large or small is not for this memo to judge.

**What you may read.** The extract and the three repositories' current files. You have no git or GitHub access; what is in neither is a limit to state (for example, pull request titles, reviews and comments, and pull requests closed without merging). You may not read: the contents of anything under questions/ or decisions/; docs/projects/ or docs/PROJECT.md; any ideas/ folder; sessions/chief-of-staff/; any file named *-notes.md; and, in Model-Lab, reviews/P-002-research-q017/ and research/Q-017*.md other than this question. Service-Desk has its own, unrelated items numbered Q-017 (for example status/Q-017.toml); they are not this question and may be read like any other record. Use labelled paths only to count and match; do not report the labels.

Out of scope: outside literature, and any recommendation of what to change.

## The decision it serves

A described baseline of the organisation's own records: who makes and merges its changes, how often its owner is asked to decide, how its checked work fares, and how often merged changes are changed again.
