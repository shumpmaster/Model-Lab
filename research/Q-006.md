# Research question — Q-006

## The question

What do the records of the Service-Desk project (shumpmaster/Service-Desk: reviews/, dispatch-log/, sessions/, the control records under reviews/*/_control/, specs/) show about review rounds per item, first-pass rates, and the kinds of findings that sent work back, by kind of task (research, definition, build, review, triage, questions to the owner)? Which of those findings trace to what the agent was told, which to its inputs, its model, the task's division or the checker, and which cannot be traced? Are the records complete and consistent enough to serve as a starting point for comparison, and what is missing?

The pipeline under study is the operating model in shumpmaster/Personal-Org-Operating-Model (OPERATING_MODEL.md; role definitions in template-v3/.claude/agents/; briefs and packs in template-v3/governance/BRIEFS.toml and PACKS.toml). Treat it as the subject being studied, not as evidence of what works.

## The decision it serves

The Discovery of P-001, the prompt engine (a project on how the written instructions that pass work between the agents of that operating model are written, checked and changed): whether a starting point for comparison can be drawn from the existing records or must be collected fresh, and which kinds of task show the most rework.

## What each answer would change

Usable records give the project a baseline at no new run cost; unusable ones mean new runs from the owner's test budget; the pattern of findings sets where work starts within "every kind, incrementally".
