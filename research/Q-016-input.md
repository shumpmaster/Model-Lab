**What I could not find or open, by part**
- **Share of work:** No published method for Google's 75%, Microsoft's 20–30% or Coinbase's 40%, and no outside check of any of them except Redwood's informal one for Anthropic (F016-4). Nothing found on how to divide "the work" by time or cost, as opposed to lines or characters. Only Agent Trace and Git AI tell a person's part from an agent's, and neither has been tested for accuracy.
- **Reliance and trust:** No study found that measures whether review or override rates of agent work match the agents' actual correctness. The appropriate-reliance measure (F016-18) and the 2015 aversion paper (F016-22) were search-only. No figures for review depth on agent PRs beyond Xia & Miller's no-review rate.
- **Holding up:** The three code-survival studies (F016-23 to F016-25) disagree, and I read abstracts only. I found little on how long after delivery defects appear, apart from those studies' observation windows (Xia & Miller: one year, per search).
- **Time saved and value:** No study found that ties a share-of-work figure to measured time saved in the same organisation, beyond Anthropic's rough support from merged PRs (F016-13).
- **Collection and cost:** Little published on what collecting these measures costs. The only items are: expert grading is called expensive (F016-35), DORA warns custom integrations may not be worth it, and telemetry gaps are listed (F016-11).
- **Non-code measures:** Exist mainly as benchmarks (RLI, GDPval) and one-off RCTs. I found no routine organisational measure of whether plans or analyses hold up later. The legal hallucination database (F016-28) is the closest to failures found in use.
- **Comparison without AI:** Covered by RCTs on non-code work (BCG, the writing study, customer support).
