# Research question — Q-002

## The question

In multi-step pipelines of language-model agents, what determines the quality of the work a session produces? Weigh the instructions it is given (their content and design: length, structure, examples, specificity, tone) against the model, the size and quality of the inputs and context it is handed, how the task is divided, the tools and context design available to it, and the checks applied after it (including the number of checkers or rounds). For each failure kind found, which of these levers has been shown to address it, and with what evidence? Include evidence on both sides: where instructions have been found to be the limiting factor, and where they have been found not to be. Name the failure kinds you cover and which you leave out.

The pipeline under study is the operating model in shumpmaster/Personal-Org-Operating-Model (OPERATING_MODEL.md; role definitions in template-v3/.claude/agents/; briefs and packs in template-v3/governance/BRIEFS.toml and PACKS.toml). Treat it as the subject being studied, not as evidence of what works.

## The decision it serves

The Discovery of P-001 (a project on how the written instructions that pass work between the agents of that operating model are written, checked and changed): which levers the project works on — instructions, inputs and context, task division, tooling, checks — and in what proportion.

## What each answer would change

If instructions explain a large share, they are the main lever; if other factors dominate, those enter scope or the project narrows; if evidence is thin, the project's own measurement carries more weight.
