# Research question — Q-012

## The question

Do findings about practice in building with AI models and agents lose validity, and if so how fast; and how have organisations in any field kept their practice up to date? Compare these ways, evenly: no deliberate review; a one-off review; reviews repeated on a schedule; reviews started by events; and continuous intake of outside material. For each, and for each way of doing the reading, choosing and summarising (by people, by automated tools, or both), report what it cost, what it caught and missed, how its output was checked, and what worked and what failed, with the evidence for each. Living reviews (evidence reviews kept continuously up to date) are one example to cover. Where breadth and depth conflict, cover every option, even thinly. This question owns how often to review and how the reading is done; whether looking is worth it at all, and how proposals are judged, are separate questions.

Facts already in the portfolio library (/home/user/portfolio/library/, index in README.md) need not be re-researched: entries Q-009-F5-2, F5-3, F5-6 to F5-8, Q-009-F13-5 and Q-009-F13-6.

## The decision it serves

How often, if at all, outside practice is reviewed; how the reading and choosing is done; and how long a finding is trusted before it is checked again.

## What each answer would change

A short shelf life favours more frequent review, and a long one less frequent or none; evidence that people, tools or both do the work well sets who or what does which step, and evidence that they fail sets where checks sit; cost evidence sets what is affordable.
