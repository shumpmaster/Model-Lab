## In plain words
This plan sets down three things before anything is counted or tried. First, the rules for counting: what a piece of work is, what counts as you stepping in on it, what counts as it being reopened for a fault, and which kind of work it is. The records cannot show what you said in conversation, so those touches are logged by hand as they happen. Before the rules are trusted, they are tried on a sample of 60 past merged pull requests, 20 from each repository or all it has, with the shortfall listed, whether or not each turns out to be a piece, with up to 2 more for each awkward case the records can show; someone other than this plan's writer codes them, and a third checks every awkward case against the records, including the cases no sample can find. Second, the trial: each method you let through is tried on 30 new pieces of one kind against 30 without, each piece's side fixed in advance by a shuffle no one can steer, judged no sooner than 30 days after the last piece; because the sides are written down in advance, whoever hands out the work can see which side the next piece will fall on, and could choose which piece to hand out next or hold one back, or merge two handed-out pieces into one pull request or hand one out twice, or fold a handed-out piece's work into a pull request that carries other work, whose questions from you after its brief was dispatched then count against it (its counting starts when its brief is dispatched, whatever other work it carries), and whoever keeps the trial's log can pick when, within the 7 days it has, it checks whether a piece is dropped or records a piece handed out twice or merged with another, which the checks catch only in part; on a trial piece every question or note of yours counts against it, even one that asks for later work, so no one who knows the sides codes which of them count, and both sides' counts run a little high, by the same rule, and a little high against the starting count, which still leaves out questions that ask for later work (you chose this on 2026-11-13, Open question 19); but the Chief of Staff, who keeps the log of what you say in conversation, knows the sides, and decides whether a touch you give in conversation is logged at all and which piece it is put against, and the monthly cross-check cannot catch a touch that was never logged, so before each trial look is judged you confirm or add to that look's conversation lines through the desk, and a line you add counts in the look (you chose this on 2026-11-13, Open question 20); there is no time limit for your confirmation, so a look, and new pieces of its kind, wait for it; this plan's design, for you to approve on the scenario card, adds that the list is shown to you without the sides; that you may strike a line, which then does not count; that you are shown every logged touch on the look's pieces, not only those from conversation, and at the second look all 120 pieces, with what was logged on the first look's pieces since you confirmed them; that the desk keeps your answer, with the list as you were shown it, in a record you make yourself, and the list and the log are checked against it before the look is judged; that a touch counts only if a list you confirmed holds it or you added it, so one left off your list, or logged after you confirm, does not count in that look unless you confirm again; and that in two cases your correction does not do what it would on a piece still open, a touch given on a piece before it was withdrawn, whose place has since been refilled, not counting at all, whether you add it or confirm one that was logged, and a piece never merged whose every touch you strike staying in as one that did not need you rather than being withdrawn, so for each the result is also worked out the other way, and if the two differ the trial comes to you, and a first look that would have gone on to the second stops there instead; a touch you too do not recall is still missed, and so is a line you confirm on the wrong piece without noticing; a piece dropped, handed out twice or merged with another has its place refilled from three spare places, on the same side, and at most three such places are used a look, a fourth stopping the trial and bringing it to you; if the first look leans the method's way without passing, one planned second look adds 30 more each way. The fixed plan is experiments/E-004.md. One choice in it was yours, and it was put to you on a card of its own: the line at two looks, which is how sure a trial must be before a method passes (the smaller the line, the surer). You chose 0.097 on 2026-11-11 (decisions/questions/P-002-two-look-line.md): the line as worked out so far, which lets a method that does nothing pass up to about 0.106 of the time at some starting shares (a starting share is the share of a kind's pieces that need you without the method, which no one knows before a trial), rather than one held at most 1 in 10 at every share (0.093), which, at the nine starting shares worked out (1 in 10 to 9 in 10), passes a true halving up to about 1 in 100 less often, and at any starting share takes the second look, with its 60 more pieces, up to about 4 in 100 more often. Third, the sources: four kinds of feed read every two weeks, the leads the Chief of Staff picks from them, and the path from a lead to research, a source check and an entry card, with outside code checked in a place that holds none of your data. Nothing here is irreversible; no trial starts before this plan is frozen, the starting count is on record, the ceilings are set and, by this plan's design, the desk can keep your answer at each look in a record you make yourself.

## The design

### M8 — the counting rules

M8 is done "when what counts as a piece, a touch and a reopening is written as rules, the rules have been applied by hand to a sample of past pieces from all three repositories with every case and its result listed, and what they cannot catch is on record" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 3). The terms are section 8's and section 6's: a **piece** "is a merged pull request, in any of the three repositories, that changes built work, a plan or research; one that only files or answers a question, or only adds records, is not a piece" (ruled T1); a **touch** "is a time the owner stepped in on a piece before it merged: a question about it, a fix the owner made, or a send-back; merging is not a touch" (ruled T2); a **reopening** is "a piece reopened for a fault within 30 days of merging", "marked by the reviewer of the later change, not the piece's maker" (section 8; ruled T25); a **kind of work** is "built work, plans or research, the three kinds a piece may change" (section 8). The rules below write these out. Each rule has its awkward cases (E-codes), which the hand check tests.

**How rules and terms are named.** The counting rules have ids of their own: K1 to K4 (path classes and kinds), RP1 and RP2 (pieces), RT1 to RT7 (touches), C1 (the first independent check) and RR1 to RR3 (reopenings); their awkward cases are E-RP1 to E-RP6, E-K1 to E-K3, E-RT1 to E-RT6 and E-RR1 to E-RR3. In this spec a ruled term of docs/gates/P-002/trial-terms.md is always written "ruled T<n>" or "ruled P<n>", and a risk of the pre-mortem "pre-mortem R<n>".

**Path classes (rule K1).** Every changed file is put in one class by its real path, by research/Q-017-memo.md §1's twelve path rules, in that order, with these changes: (i) a question or decision file is one under a top-level `questions/` or `decisions/` folder only (the memo's labels went by folder name at any depth and so caught test and template copies, §3 and §5); (ii) in the Personal-Org-Operating-Model repository, the operating model's own text (its OPERATING_MODEL.md and playbook files) is built work, since it is that repository's product; (iii) a file at one of a repository's owner-answer paths (RT1(a), *Owner-answer paths*), and in Service-Desk every other file directly in `docs/design/references/` whose name starts `owner-` (`owner-request-`, `owner-report-` and the like), is owner question or answer, by rule 1, so that it takes neither rule 9's class, research (Service-Desk's `docs/design/references/` being the design folder's reference-image subfolder), nor rule 5's, records (Personal-Org-Operating-Model's `reviews/<folder>/_owner-answer*.md`), and a pull request whose every file is a question, an answer or a record is not a piece (ruled T1; Service-Desk #237, whose only research files by the memo's rules were its owner-answers and owner-request files); (iv) nothing else changes. The memo's rules 1 to 3 are written in labels; K1 reads each label as the real paths that research/Q-017-extract/README.md, "Labels in place of paths", gives that label, with changes (i) and (iii) applied to rule 1. The classes are built, plan, research, records, owner question or answer, and other.

**What a piece is.**
- **RP1.** A pull request merged into the default branch of Personal-Org-Operating-Model, Model-Lab or Service-Desk, as GitHub's record of the pull request says (so a squash or rebase merge counts, though the commit extract coded only two-parent merges, research/Q-017-extract/README.md).
- **RP2.** It is a piece when at least one changed file is built, plan or research (K1). When every changed file is records, owner question or answer, or other, it is not a piece (ruled T1).
- *E-RP1:* work that lands by a local merge or a direct push, with no pull request (Service-Desk had 434 such plain commits and 73 such merges, research/Q-017-memo.md §1): not a piece; the count gives their number per month apart, as work the measure cannot see.
- *E-RP2:* a pull request closed without merging: not a piece; an owner's close can be a send-back of the piece that later carries the work (RT3(b)).
- *E-RP3:* rule and guidance prose outside the operating model repository (AGENTS.md, readmes, governance prose: K1 "other"): not a piece.
- *E-RP4:* one pull request carrying the work of several briefs: one piece.
- *E-RP5:* a pull request merged by a workflow bot that changes built, plan or research files: a piece; the bot's merge is not a touch.
- *E-RP6:* a revert merged as a pull request: a piece of its own, and a reopening of the piece it reverts (RR1(a)).

**Which kind a piece is (U3: a piece that touches several kinds).**
- **K2.** A piece has one kind: the kind of the brief that dispatched its work, by the role dispatched alone, as the brief record's name or the `Agent-Session:` trailer gives it: Builder, Check author, tools-builder or release → built work; Definer → plans; Researcher, Source checker or Input reader → research. A brief to the Chief of Staff or the Orchestrator, or to any role not in this list, is not mapped by K2, since its kind would depend on what the brief says, which the count does not read; K3 gives its kind.
- **K2's short forms.** A trailer may give its role in a short form between hyphens. K2 reads the short forms below as their roles, whether or not the brief record the trailer names exists (a piece with no brief record takes its dispatch time from its first commit, "What a touch is"); a short form not in this table is not read as a role, and K3 applies, listed.

| Short form in the trailer | Role | Seen in the records |
|---|---|---|
| `-def-` | Definer (plans) | Service-Desk, `session_<id>-def-s001` |
| `-tb-` | tools-builder (built work) | Personal-Org-Operating-Model, `session_<id>-tb-s017` to `-tb-s019` |

- **K3.** A piece with no brief, with a brief K2 does not map, or whose briefs are of more than one kind, has the kind with the most changed lines among its built, plan and research files (K1), a file's changed lines being its added plus its removed lines; a tie goes to built work, then plans.
- **K4.** For a trial, the kind is fixed at the draw from the brief's role by K2, and does not change with what the piece's files turn out to be; a piece whose merged files are mostly of another kind keeps its kind and is listed. A piece whose kind K2 cannot give at dispatch (a Chief of Staff or Orchestrator brief, a role not in K2's list, or no brief) is not eligible for a trial, since its kind is not known when its side is drawn.
- So a piece that touches several kinds counts once, under one kind, for the count by kind and for a trial; it is eligible only for a trial of that kind. V1's own figures count all kinds together (ruled T32), so the kind rule changes no figure of V1.
- *E-K1:* a piece touching several kinds (Q-017 flags 175, 50 and 38 pull requests as touching more than one kind, by its own rule, which counted records and question files as kinds, §1, "By kind"). *E-K2:* a brief role not in K2's list, the Chief of Staff's and the Orchestrator's included (K3 applies, listed). *E-K3:* Service-Desk's scripted research, dispatched by control files, not briefs (research by K2, the Researcher's role).

**What a touch is.** A touch counts on a piece when it falls inside the piece's window: after its dispatch, not at it, and before its merge (the window excludes its start). A piece's dispatch is the commit time of the first commit that adds its brief record (the file that holds the brief whose number the piece's `Agent-Session:` trailers name: in Model-Lab `docs/briefs/<n>-<role>.md`; in the other two repositories the file their briefs or control files are kept in, which the extract's README states and the Critic checks against the repositories before the sample is drawn, step 1 below), and, where the trailers name more than one brief with a record, the earliest of those first adds, as the extract reads it, the briefs with no record being left out and the piece listed (step 6 (v)); with no brief record, the author time of the piece's first commit, which is, here and below, the piece's own commit with the earliest author time. On a trial piece (one whose J1 line gives a `trial`, or an entry the Reviewer takes as a trial piece, J2), the dispatch is its entered brief's, whatever other briefs its trailers name: the commit time that first adds that brief's record, or the entry's `entered_at` if no brief record is ever written, and, where two entered briefs' work merges in one pull request, the first entry's (E-004, Exclusions, "An entry and its pull requests"); the owner chose at the plan stop "A: focused fix (Recommended)", the card's option A: "on a trial piece, counting starts at the entered piece's own start, whatever other work its commits name, and the reviewer lists every trial piece whose commits name other work" (decisions/questions/P-002-plan-stop-2.md; the card as put, questions/P-002-plan-stop-2.md at commit 9c9a855; intake/P-002-owner-answers-2026-11-13.md). So a touch on other work the trial piece carries, given before its dispatch, does not count on it, while one given after its dispatch, whose record names the piece, does, and the Reviewer lists every trial piece whose commits name a brief that is not its entry's, and every trial piece whose first commit's author time is earlier than its entry's `entered_at`, as one folded into work whose commits name no brief would be (J2; E-004, "The sides are known ahead"). A record **names a piece** when its text holds, as a whole word or path, `#<n>`, `PR <n>` or `pull request <n>` for the piece's pull request number n (`PR` and `pull request` in any letter case; `#<n>` with no letter, digit or `_` right before it, or with the piece's own repository's name right before it, as in `Model-Lab#12` or `shumpmaster/Model-Lab#12`, while `#<n>` joined to any other word, such as another repository's name, names nothing); `brief <b>` (in any letter case), `briefs/<b>-` or `briefs/<b>.md` for a brief b whose work the piece carries (so Personal-Org-Operating-Model's brief record `docs/briefs/s-NNN.md` names its brief by its own path); or the spec id that the piece's `Spec:` trailers give, except in Service-Desk, where nearly every `Spec:` trailer gives S-001, so that id names no one piece there (this exclusion is kept in place of a general one, that a spec id in the `Spec:` trailers of more than one piece under way at the same time names none, which would need a field the extract does not have). - **RR1.** A piece is reopened for a fault when, within 30 days of its merge, (a) it is reverted, in whole or in part, by a merged commit whose subject starts "Revert" and whose message names one of the piece's commits after "This reverts commit", or whose subject names the piece's pull request number; (b) the reviewer of a later change marks, in that change's review record, the line `Reopens: <repository>#<pull request number>` for a fault the later change fixes (a reviewer, never the piece's maker, ruled T25); or (c) for a trial piece, the owner would not have accepted it on a short read (ruled P14).
- **RR2.** Not reopenings: later work that builds on the piece; a change made because the owner ruled something new; a records update.
- **RR3.** A reopening found after 30 days does not count. A piece whose 30 days have not passed is pending, never counted as held up.

- **How each piece's side is fixed, so no one can steer it (ruled T23, T29):** the sides of places 1 to 60 of each look are fixed in the frozen trial record before any piece is entered, from the trial id, the look and the place number alone, in blocks of 10 with 5 each way; a reserve place (61 to 63), used only to refill a withdrawal or a void entry, has no side in the record and takes the side of the withdrawn piece or void entry it refills, as the log's withdrawal or void line names it; each eligible piece dispatched in a look's weeks takes the next place in the order it is dispatched; the Reviewer re-derives every side of places 1 to 60 and checks each reserve place's side against its withdrawal or void line (E-004, Step 4). - **Withdrawals and pieces that never merge:** a drawn piece is withdrawn only when three conditions recorded in the records all hold, checked by a fresh Reviewer session that is not given the piece's side; a void entry (a second entry for a brief already entered, or the later of two entries whose work merges in one pull request) is refilled from the same reserve places on its side, as a withdrawal is; withdrawals and void entries together at most 3 per look, a fourth stopping the trial; a piece still unmerged 60 days after its dispatch is settled by a fixed rule, so no piece in places 1 to 60 holds its look for more than 60 days after its dispatch plus the 30 days before judging, and a reserve piece adds its own wait, which depends on the kind's pace (E-004, Exclusions). The 3 and the 60 days, the 7 days within which a withdrawal check is started once a piece meets the conditions, or a void line is written once an entry is void, and the 60 days a look waits for a reserve place opened and not yet filled, after which the place is left empty (E-004, Exclusions), are this plan's figures for the owner's scenario card.
- **Each kind's expected trial length (U3):** from the count's first 4 weeks, the weeks to each look at that pace, on each entry card and in each status update; a kind that would take more than 26 weeks to its first look comes to the owner under U3's fallback before any entry card for it (E-004, Step 2). The 26 is this plan's figure for the owner's scenario card.
- **How many of this project's agents run at once:** at most two (the session that starts a helper, and one helper); a trial adds no agent to any piece. So no tier question is needed; a change needing three or more goes to the owner as a tier question first (docs/projects/P-002.md section 5).

### M10 — the sources and how they are read

|---|---|---|

| F2 | Research preprints: the new listings of arXiv's software-engineering (cs.SE) and multi-agent systems (cs.MA) sections, searched with the general words below | The 20 newest matches since the last round |

*Reserve feeds*, used when a feed is replaced: Martin Fowler's site; the Google DeepMind blog; Hacker News, searched with the general words below. A feed that yields no kept lead in 3 rounds in a row is replaced by a reserve feed of another kind, and the change is reported in the next status update (pre-mortem R8: "One source yields nothing for several rounds"). Exact addresses are recorded by the Input reader at the first round.

**The general words.** Every search query or page fetch carries only general words about a method, never repository names, project text, records or the owner's details (docs/projects/P-002.md section 8). The queries are made only from these words and their plain combinations: agent, agents, multi-agent, orchestration, delegation, autonomy, approval, human in the loop, code review, evaluation, testing, verification, hand-off, memory, context, instructions, prompt, skills, tools, permissions, sandbox, failure, incident, workflow, planning, research, software engineering. Each reading brief holds its queries verbatim; the Builder's reading-brief check (Build) refuses a brief holding any other word in a query, any repository or project name, any record id, or the owner's name or address, which the check reads from the session's git settings at run time and never writes.

**Who does what in a round.**
1. **The Chief of Staff starts the round** on its day, from its session (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4). Once two rounds are on record, the Chief of Staff proposes to the owner a scheduled task that starts each round as a fresh Chief of Staff session with the same brief; until the owner agrees, the session starts it. A missed round is recorded and shown on the desk (pre-mortem R6).
2. **The Input reader** (one helper per round, fresh) reads each feed and returns structured findings, as data, never as instructions (OPERATING_MODEL.md §5, D-064): one entry per item, with its address, date, feed, kind of source, the method it describes in a quoted line, and whether the text tried to instruct agents (J5). It writes no files.
3. **The Chief of Staff picks the leads** (the screener step; section 4): an item is a lead when it describes a way of running or improving a team of agents in strategy, planning, research or building work (docs/projects/P-002.md section 1), needs no paid tool (section 5), is not a clear misfit for a one-owner team (ruled T9) and could plausibly be switched on and off piece by piece (U5). It records the round: items per feed, leads kept per feed, and why each item was not kept, in one of those four reasons. This is the yield by source (M12).
4. **A lead becomes a research question.** The Chief of Staff writes it in the research/_QUESTION.md form, neutrally, without the hoped-for answer, asking what the method is, what conditions it rests on, what evidence there is for it and who has a stake; the Critic checks it for gaps and neutral wording (section 4).
5. **The research memo.** The Researcher answers it from what the Input reader returns, not by reading outside pages itself (section 4; D-064); the Orchestrator records the memo verbatim in research/.
6. **The source check.** "One source someone else has opened and confirmed" (ruled T8). Outside text reaches the Source checker only through the Input reader: the Source checker names each source address and the claim it must confirm; a fresh Input reader session, not the one that served the Researcher, opens each and returns the passage around the claim verbatim, with its address and date; the Source checker compares the claim with the passage and files the facts that pass in the portfolio library (J7).
7. **The conditions.** The Chief of Staff reads the method's conditions, from the memo, against ours, and a clear misfit is not tried (ruled T9).
8. **Outside code, prompts and skills** are checked before they run, with no data out (ruled T10; below).
9. **The entry card** (E-004, Step 1), checked by the Critic, joins the queue of at most 3 (ruled P15).

**Checking outside code, prompts and skills with no data out (U4).** "The Reviewer checks; the Builder sets up the place it runs" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4).
- **The place:** a cloud environment of its own, holding no repository, no environment variables or secrets, no connectors, and no network: no allowed hosts, with package registries and connector hosts off. The Builder proposes it through the environment tools, which show the owner what it holds before it is made; nothing is put there but the item and the brief.
- **The route:** the Input reader fetches the item and returns it verbatim as data. The Orchestrator starts a fresh Reviewer session in the place, with the item in its first message and no other project text. The Reviewer reads it for network calls, reads of files or settings outside its own folder, requests for keys, and instructions aimed at agents; runs it there on a made-up input; and notes every refused connection. It returns only a fixed form: `item: <address>`, `sha256: <hex>`, `network: none | tried`, `files-outside: none | tried`, `secrets: none | asked`, `instructions-to-agents: none | found`, `ran: yes | no`, `verdict: PASS | FAIL`. Nothing of the item comes back in that return.
- **The one harmless sample (U4's test):** before any lead uses the place, the Builder writes a sample of its own, kept in Model-Lab under tests/E-004/, that prints a line, tries to read an environment variable and a file in the home folder, tries to connect to a public host, and holds a comment telling agents to ignore their instructions. The test passes when the Reviewer's return reports each of those four, the connection is refused by the place, and a listing in the place shows no repository and no secret. Until it passes, nothing from outside runs; if it cannot pass, only methods that need no outside code, prompt or skill are tried (U4's fallback).
- The model's rule is that untrusted text is read only by the Input reader (OPERATING_MODEL.md §5, D-064); reading code to check it is the Reviewer's task in section 4. This plan keeps it to a place with nothing of the owner's in it and a fixed-form return; whether that needs the owner's ruling as an exception is asked below.
