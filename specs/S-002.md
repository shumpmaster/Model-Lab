## In plain words
This plan sets down three things before anything is counted or tried. First, the rules for counting: what a piece of work is, what counts as you stepping in on it, what counts as it being reopened for a fault, and which kind of work it is. The records cannot show what you said in conversation, so those touches are logged by hand as they happen. Before the rules are trusted, they are tried on a sample of 60 past merged pull requests, 20 from each repository or all it has, with the shortfall listed, whether or not each turns out to be a piece, with up to 2 more for each awkward case the records can show; someone other than this plan's writer codes them, and a third checks every awkward case against the records, including the cases no sample can find. Second, the trial: each method you let through is tried on 30 new pieces of one kind against 30 without, each piece's side fixed in advance by a shuffle no one can steer, judged 30 days after the last piece; because the sides are written down in advance, whoever hands out the work can see which side the next piece will fall on, and could choose which piece to hand out next or hold one back, or merge two handed-out pieces into one pull request or hand one out twice, and whoever keeps the trial's log can pick when, within the 7 days it has, it checks whether a piece is dropped or records a piece handed out twice or merged with another, which the checks catch only in part; a piece dropped, handed out twice or merged with another has its place refilled from three spare places, on the same side, and at most three such places are used a look, a fourth stopping the trial and bringing it to you; if the first look leans the method's way without passing, one planned second look adds 30 more each way. The fixed plan is experiments/E-004.md. One choice in it was yours, and it was put to you on a card of its own: the line at two looks, which is how sure a trial must be before a method passes (the smaller the line, the surer). You chose 0.097 on 2026-11-11 (decisions/questions/P-002-two-look-line.md): the line as worked out so far, which lets a method that does nothing pass up to about 0.106 of the time at some starting shares (a starting share is the share of a kind's pieces that need you without the method, which no one knows before a trial), rather than one held at most 1 in 10 at every share (0.093), which, at the nine starting shares worked out (1 in 10 to 9 in 10), passes a true halving up to about 1 in 100 less often, and at any starting share takes the second look, with its 60 more pieces, up to about 4 in 100 more often. Third, the sources: four kinds of feed read every two weeks, the leads the Chief of Staff picks from them, and the path from a lead to research, a source check and an entry card, with outside code checked in a place that holds none of your data. Nothing here is irreversible; no trial starts before this plan is frozen, the starting count is on record and the ceilings are set.

## The design

### M8 — the counting rules

M8 is done "when what counts as a piece, a touch and a reopening is written as rules, the rules have been applied by hand to a sample of past pieces from all three repositories with every case and its result listed, and what they cannot catch is on record" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 3). The terms are section 8's and section 6's: a **piece** "is a merged pull request, in any of the three repositories, that changes built work, a plan or research; one that only files or answers a question, or only adds records, is not a piece" (ruled T1); a **touch** "is a time the owner stepped in on a piece before it merged: a question about it, a fix the owner made, or a send-back; merging is not a touch" (ruled T2); a **reopening** is "a piece reopened for a fault within 30 days of merging", "marked by the reviewer of the later change, not the piece's maker" (section 8; ruled T25); a **kind of work** is "built work, plans or research, the three kinds a piece may change" (section 8). The rules below write these out. Each rule has its awkward cases (E-codes), which the hand check tests.

**How rules and terms are named.** The counting rules have ids of their own: K1 to K4 (path classes and kinds), RP1 and RP2 (pieces), RT1 to RT7 (touches), C1 (the first independent check) and RR1 to RR3 (reopenings); their awkward cases are E-RP1 to E-RP6, E-K1 to E-K3, E-RT1 to E-RT6 and E-RR1 to E-RR3. In this spec a ruled term of docs/gates/P-002/trial-terms.md is always written "ruled T<n>" or "ruled P<n>", and a risk of the pre-mortem "pre-mortem R<n>".

**Path classes (rule K1).** Every changed file is put in one class by its real path, by research/Q-017-memo.md §1's twelve path rules, in that order, with three changes: (i) a question or decision file is one under a top-level `questions/` or `decisions/` folder only (the memo's labels went by folder name at any depth and so caught test and template copies, §3 and §5); (ii) in the Personal-Org-Operating-Model repository, the operating model's own text (its OPERATING_MODEL.md and playbook files) is built work, since it is that repository's product; (iii) nothing else changes. The memo's rules 1 to 3 are written in labels; K1 reads each label as the real paths that research/Q-017-extract/README.md, "Labels in place of paths", gives that label, with change (i) applied to rule 1. The classes are built, plan, research, records, owner question or answer, and other.

**What a piece is.**
- **RP1.** A pull request merged into the default branch of Personal-Org-Operating-Model, Model-Lab or Service-Desk, as GitHub's record of the pull request says (so a squash or rebase merge counts, though the commit extract coded only two-parent merges, research/Q-017-extract/README.md).
- **RP2.** It is a piece when at least one changed file is built, plan or research (K1). When every changed file is records, owner question or answer, or other, it is not a piece (ruled T1).
- *E-RP1:* work that lands by a local merge or a direct push, with no pull request (Service-Desk had 434 such plain commits and 73 such merges, research/Q-017-memo.md §1): not a piece; the count gives their number per month apart, as work the measure cannot see.
- *E-RP2:* a pull request closed without merging: not a piece; an owner's close can be a send-back of the piece that later carries the work (RT3(b)).
- *E-RP3:* rule and guidance prose outside the operating model repository (AGENTS.md, readmes, governance prose: K1 "other"): not a piece.
- *E-RP4:* one pull request carrying the work of several briefs: one piece.
- *E-RP5:* a pull request merged by a workflow bot that changes built, plan or research files: a piece; the bot's merge is not a touch.
- *E-RP6:* a revert merged as a pull request: a piece of its own, and a reopening of the piece it reverts (RR1(a)).

**Which kind a piece is (U3: a piece that touches several kinds).**
- **K2.** A piece has one kind: the kind of the brief that dispatched its work, by the role dispatched alone, as the brief record's name or the `Agent-Session:` trailer gives it: Builder, Check author, tools-builder or release → built work; Definer → plans; Researcher, Source checker or Input reader → research. A brief to the Chief of Staff or the Orchestrator, or to any role not in this list, is not mapped by K2, since its kind would depend on what the brief says, which the count does not read; K3 gives its kind.
- **K2's short forms.** A trailer may give its role in a short form between hyphens. K2 reads the short forms below as their roles, whether or not the brief record the trailer names exists (a piece with no brief record takes its dispatch time from its first commit, "What a touch is"); a short form not in this table is not read as a role, and K3 applies, listed.

| Short form in the trailer | Role | Seen in the records |
|---|---|---|
| `-def-` | Definer (plans) | Service-Desk, `session_<id>-def-s001` |
| `-tb-` | tools-builder (built work) | Personal-Org-Operating-Model, `session_<id>-tb-s017` to `-tb-s019` |

- **K3.** A piece with no brief, with a brief K2 does not map, or whose briefs are of more than one kind, has the kind with the most changed lines among its built, plan and research files (K1); a tie goes to built work, then plans.
- **K4.** For a trial, the kind is fixed at the draw from the brief's role by K2, and does not change with what the piece's files turn out to be; a piece whose merged files are mostly of another kind keeps its kind and is listed. A piece whose kind K2 cannot give at dispatch (a Chief of Staff or Orchestrator brief, a role not in K2's list, or no brief) is not eligible for a trial, since its kind is not known when its side is drawn.
- So a piece that touches several kinds counts once, under one kind, for the count by kind and for a trial; it is eligible only for a trial of that kind. V1's own figures count all kinds together (ruled T32), so the kind rule changes no figure of V1.
- *E-K1:* a piece touching several kinds (Q-017 flags 175, 50 and 38 pull requests as touching more than one kind, by its own rule, which counted records and question files as kinds, §1, "By kind"). *E-K2:* a brief role not in K2's list, the Chief of Staff's and the Orchestrator's included (K3 applies, listed). *E-K3:* Service-Desk's scripted research, dispatched by control files, not briefs (research by K2, the Researcher's role).

**What a touch is.** A touch counts on a piece when it falls inside the piece's window: after its dispatch, not at it, and before its merge (the window excludes its start). A piece's dispatch is the commit time of the first commit that adds its brief record (the file that holds the brief whose number the piece's `Agent-Session:` trailers name: in Model-Lab `docs/briefs/<n>-<role>.md`; in the other two repositories the file their briefs or control files are kept in, which the extract's README states and the Critic checks against the repositories before the sample is drawn, step 1 below); with no brief record, the author time of the piece's first commit, which is, here and below, the piece's own commit with the earliest author time. A record **names a piece** when its text holds, as a whole word or path, `#<n>`, `PR <n>` or `pull request <n>` for the piece's pull request number n (`PR` and `pull request` in any letter case; `#<n>` with no letter, digit or `_` right before it, or with the piece's own repository's name right before it, as in `Model-Lab#12` or `shumpmaster/Model-Lab#12`, while `#<n>` joined to any other word, such as another repository's name, names nothing); `brief <b>` (in any letter case), `briefs/<b>-` or `briefs/<b>.md` for a brief b whose work the piece carries (so Personal-Org-Operating-Model's brief record `docs/briefs/s-NNN.md` names its brief by its own path); or the spec id that the piece's `Spec:` trailers give, except in Service-Desk, where nearly every `Spec:` trailer gives S-001, so that id names no one piece there (this exclusion is kept in place of a general one, that a spec id in the `Spec:` trailers of more than one piece under way at the same time names none, which would need a field the extract does not have). A question file or decision record that the piece's own commits add or change also names the piece: from the first of the piece's own commits that adds it or changes it (for a file the piece first adds, as first added), that commit and each later one being read for the piece, as for an append below; and a file one of the piece's own commits adds and a later one deletes before the merge is read so too. A record that names the piece is never "a question about another piece" (RT4), even when its text is about other work; until the owner rules on Open question 11, each record that names the piece only by being one of its own files, and whose text is about other work, is counted and listed. A record that cites the path of another file the piece changes (a shared plan, spec or project file) does not name the piece by that path. A question file or decision record is read as the commit that first adds it to the default branch left it, whether or not it is still there at the cut-off (a question file deleted when its answer was recorded is read so), and each later commit's added lines as that commit adds them; a commit here is any commit the default branch at the cut-off reaches, a merged pull request's own commits included, even where the pull request adds a file and deletes it again (Personal-Org-Operating-Model #32's question file, added and deleted inside the pull request). A record whose text as first added does not name the piece names it from the first later commit whose added lines do: that append, and each later one, is read for the piece; the record as first added, and the appends before that one, are not.
- **RT1, a question.** (a) A question put to the owner, as a question file (under top-level `questions/`) or in conversation as a decision record (under top-level `decisions/`), counted once per question (Q-018/U4): a question file `questions/<name>.md` and the decision record of the same name, `decisions/questions/<name>.md`, are one question; a decision record with no question file (in Service-Desk also one under `decisions/<item>/`) is one question; and each later commit that adds the owner's words to an existing decision record (an append, such as an answer to a follow-up question or a further ruling) is one more. It counts on a piece when its record names the piece and, inside the piece's window, its question file is first added or the owner's words are added to its decision record, appends included; its time is the commit time of the first such commit in the window, so a question filed before the dispatch and answered after it counts at its answer. Each record and each append is timed by the commit that adds it, never by a date its text gives. For a piece whose dispatch is its first commit (one with no brief record), a record first added in that commit is, as first added, the ruling that started the piece (RT4); a later commit that adds the owner's words to it is an append, and counts. For a piece dispatched by its brief record, a record first added in its first commit is timed by that commit like any other, and one added at the dispatch is outside the window. Not counted under (a): a file whose name ends `-notes.md` (a role's notes on a ruling); a decision record or append that adds no owner words (no `Ruling:`, `Answer:` or `Decision:` line, and no quoted line under a line naming the owner's words, answer, note or ruling: a line starting `>` whose nearest line above it, in the record as that commit leaves it, that is neither blank nor itself quoted holds, in any letter case, "owner's words", a word starting "answer" or "ruling", or the word "note" or "notes"), such as a role's verdict recorded verbatim; and a ruling RT5 puts outside the count. Where the records cannot show whether a record or an append holds the owner's words, or which piece it is about, the touch log decides (RT1(c)) and the case is listed; (b) any comment by the owner on the pull request, or a review the owner left as "commented", before merging, counted without its text being kept. A comment or review under the owner's account that carries an agent mark, or that a session's records show it posted, is the agent's, not the owner's, and is not a touch (E-RT1). A session's records show it posted when a file of K1 class records, as the default branch holds it at the cut-off, has a line holding "reviewer comment" or "reviewer comments" (in any letter case) that names the pull request (`#<n>`, `PR <n>`, `PR #<n>` or `pull request <n>`, in any letter case), as a session record naming "the reviewer comment(s) on PR #n" among its returns does; such a line shows the session posted every comment under the owner's account on that pull request, however many there are (Service-Desk #11 has 3, and reviews/11/reviewer.md names "the reviewer comments on PR #11"), and each is listed. A records line that names a comment on the pull request in other words shows nothing. The agent marks are these, and no others until a revision adds one: the footer line `_Generated by [Claude Code]_`, the only footer the Critic's reading of comments has found (on all 8 comments under the owner's account that its reading covered, made before merging on Service-Desk #11 and #78 and Personal-Org-Operating-Model #18, #30 and #35, reviews/Q-018/_critic-verbatim.md, finding 3; the comments before merging on Service-Desk #6 and Personal-Org-Operating-Model #11, and those on frame U's pull requests, research/Q-018-extract/github_reads.json, were not in that reading); and a link to an agent session, `claude.ai/code/session_<id>`. A comment with neither mark and no session record counts as the owner's only in a repository whose agent comments the Critic's reading has shown to carry the footer: Service-Desk and Personal-Org-Operating-Model. In Model-Lab, where no comment has been read (its sampled pull requests have none, research/Q-018-extract/github_reads.json), and in any repository where the marks cannot be read, the owner's comments cannot be told apart from an agent's, and RT1(b) is counted from the touch log instead (step 6), with the comments listed beside it, until a reading of that repository's agent comments shows the footer; (c) a question in the touch log (U1's fallback, below) that names the piece.
- **RT2, a fix.** (a) A commit in the pull request under the owner's identity that changes a built, plan or research file, when it was committed through GitHub's web editor or carries no `Agent-Session:` trailer; (b) a fix in the touch log.
- **RT3, a send-back.** (a) A review by the owner asking for changes; (b) a pull request the owner closed unmerged whose work later merges in another pull request (the same head branch, or a brief number in both's `Agent-Session:` trailers), closed before the later one merges, which counts on the later piece; (c) a send-back in the touch log.
- **RT4, not touches.** Merging; an approving review; the ruling that started the piece: for a piece dispatched by its first commit, a question file or decision record first added in that commit is this ruling, as first added, and not a touch, whatever date its text gives (Q-018/U1), and for a piece dispatched by its brief record, a record added at the dispatch is outside the window; every other record, and every append, that one's included, is timed by the commit that adds it (RT1(a)); the owner reading merged work, short reads included; an agent's FAIL verdict; a question about another piece, which is one whose record does not name the piece ("What a touch is"); a commit under the owner's identity that only records the owner's own answer (it counts under RT1 if that question is about the piece, and not again as a fix).
- **RT5, outside the count for now.** Whether a ruling the operating model requires of the owner (a phase gate, a value target) is a touch on the pieces it rules is open (Open question 3). Such a ruling names a project or a milestone, not a piece, and no field of the extract or the count says which pieces it lands on. Which records are such rulings is read first from each record's own words, its **class**: in a decision record, the sentence of its first `Asked:` line, as first added, that holds ": the owner's own <word>, never a default" (or "…, never a default or a proxy"), its class being that sentence's words before the colon, the sentence starting after the last full stop followed by a space before that colon, or at the line's start (as at `….md. A phase gate, …`, P-002-design-gate.md) (Model-Lab's form: "A phase gate: the owner's own ruling, never a default or a proxy", P-001-design-gate.md; "A choice under the stop rule after the cards' third failed check: the owner's own choice, never a default", P-001-build-gate-stop.md); where the decision record gives none, or there is none, the parenthesis of its question file's `RISK CLASS:` line, as first added, when it ends ", the owner's own <word>)", its class being the words before that comma (such as `R3 (part of a value target, the owner's own ruling)`, questions/P-002-trial-gates.md as first added at daf76d7, since deleted). (a) A record whose class names a stop rule (`stop rule`, as whole words, in any letter case, as in "the plan stop rule") is a choice under that stop rule and is counted under RT1, whatever its name. (b) Otherwise, a record whose class names a phase gate or a value target (`phase gate` or `value target`, as whole words, in any letter case, `value target's` included), alone or as a part of one ("Part of a value target", P-002-trial-gates.md and P-002-two-look-line.md; "Touches the value target (term 1) and a rule change (term 4)", P-001-build-gate-terms.md), is such a ruling and is left out, whatever its name; one whose class is not exactly "A phase gate", "A value target" or "Part of a value target" (in any letter case) names other rulings too (as those of P-001-build-gate-terms.md and P-002-design-gate.md do), and is left out whole, and listed with its class. (c) A record whose class names neither is counted under RT1, whatever its name. (d) Only a record that gives no class is marked by its name: it is such a ruling when its file name holds, as whole hyphen-separated words, a named gate, `design-gate`, `build-gate` or `launch-gate`, or `value-target` (so Service-Desk's `P-001-m3c-launch-gate-2.md`, but not `P-001-gate-card.md`); this field marks it, so that it is left out. - **RT6.** A touch on work that two pieces carry counts on each.
- **RT7.** An owner's instruction given unasked, in conversation, that changes a piece already under way is logged in the touch log as an instruction (J9) and counted as a touch, with its own code, apart from send-backs. - **How each piece's side is fixed, so no one can steer it (ruled T23, T29):** the sides of places 1 to 60 of each look are fixed in the frozen trial record before any piece is entered, from the trial id, the look and the place number alone, in blocks of 10 with 5 each way; a reserve place (61 to 63), used only to refill a withdrawal or a void entry, has no side in the record and takes the side of the withdrawn piece or void entry it refills, as the log's withdrawal or void line names it; each eligible piece dispatched in a look's weeks takes the next place in the order it is dispatched; the Reviewer re-derives every side of places 1 to 60 and checks each reserve place's side against its withdrawal or void line (E-004, Step 4). Since the sides are known ahead, the session that dispatches can choose which brief takes the next place, or merge two entered briefs into one pull request or enter a brief twice, and the Orchestrator can choose when, within the 7 days, it starts a withdrawal check or writes a void line, which this design does not catch (risks, below).
- **Withdrawals and pieces that never merge:** a drawn piece is withdrawn only when three conditions recorded in the records all hold, checked by a fresh Reviewer session that is not given the piece's side; a void entry (a second entry for a brief already entered, or the later of two entries whose work merges in one pull request) is refilled from the same reserve places on its side, as a withdrawal is; withdrawals and void entries together at most 3 per look, a fourth stopping the trial; a piece still unmerged 60 days after its dispatch is settled by a fixed rule, so no piece in places 1 to 60 holds its look for more than 60 days after its dispatch plus the 30 days before judging, and a reserve piece adds its own wait, which depends on the kind's pace (E-004, Exclusions). The 3 and the 60 days, the 7 days within which a withdrawal check is started once a piece meets the conditions, or a void line is written once an entry is void, and the 60 days a look waits for a reserve place opened and not yet filled, after which the place is left empty (E-004, Exclusions), are this plan's figures for the owner's scenario card.
- **Each kind's expected trial length (U3):** from the count's first 4 weeks, the weeks to each look at that pace, on each entry card and in each status update; a kind that would take more than 26 weeks to its first look comes to the owner under U3's fallback before any entry card for it (E-004, Step 2). The 26 is this plan's figure for the owner's scenario card.
- **How many of this project's agents run at once:** at most two (the session that starts a helper, and one helper); a trial adds no agent to any piece. So no tier question is needed; a change needing three or more goes to the owner as a tier question first (docs/projects/P-002.md section 5).

**The line at two looks (ruled P18).** The owner left it open, to rule on a card of its own before this design freezes (decisions/questions/P-002-design-gate.md, Part 7 and "How it is carried out"), and ruled 0.097 on that card on 2026-11-11, after the independent run passed (decisions/questions/P-002-two-look-line.md; reviews/P-002-two-look-line/reviewer.md, PASS at round 3). experiments/E-004/line.py computes both versions, and its output is recorded beside it (experiments/E-004/line-output.txt): (a) the line as worked out so far, 0.097 at each look (any line from 0.09616 up to, not including, 0.09725 decides the same); (b) the largest line at which a method that does nothing passes at most 1 time in 10 at every starting share, 0.093 (any line from 0.09227 up to, not including, 0.09359 decides the same). Their figures are in E-004, "Deciding threshold": how often each passes a method that does nothing and a true halving, the two bands of starting shares (about 0.31 to 0.43 and about 0.57 to 0.69) at which (a) passes a method that does nothing more than 1 in 10 of the time, each worst case at both of its starting shares, how often each takes the second look, and five of the script's eight assumptions (A1, A2, A3, A5 and A6) in plain words. E-004's line names the one the owner ruled, (a) 0.097; (b) was not chosen. Someone other than this plan's writer ran the script before the card went to the owner.

### M10 — the sources and how they are read

**The feeds, read every 14 days** (the owner's design: "Feeds set up that target preminent sites/sources/content - those feeds are read by screeners", decisions/questions/P-002-groundwork-shortlist.md, answer 2). More than one kind of source, since two security feeds overlapped by "only 2.5% to 4.0%" and cheap alerts found less than a full search in one test (docs/projects/P-002.md section 2; pre-mortem R8); few, since more search went with less past a point in one study (Q-011-11, the same section). This plan's choice, for the owner's scenario card:

| Feed | Kind of source | Read in each round |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Makers' engineering write-ups: the engineering blogs of the leading AI labs (Anthropic's, OpenAI's) | Every post since the last round |
| F2 | Research preprints: the new listings of arXiv's software-engineering (cs.SE) and multi-agent systems (cs.MA) sections, searched with the general words below | The 20 newest matches since the last round |
| F3 | Independent practitioners: Simon Willison's weblog, and the Thoughtworks Technology Radar when a new volume is out | Every post on agents and agent practice since the last round |
| F4 | Release notes of the tools the team's sessions run in: the agent tool's published changelog, and GitHub's changelog | Every entry since the last round |

*Reserve feeds*, used when a feed is replaced: Martin Fowler's site; the Google DeepMind blog; Hacker News, searched with the general words below. A feed that yields no kept lead in 3 rounds in a row is replaced by a reserve feed of another kind, and the change is reported in the next status update (pre-mortem R8: "One source yields nothing for several rounds"). Exact addresses are recorded by the Input reader at the first round.

**The general words.** Every search query or page fetch carries only general words about a method, never repository names, project text, records or the owner's details (docs/projects/P-002.md section 8). The queries are made only from these words and their plain combinations: agent, agents, multi-agent, orchestration, delegation, autonomy, approval, human in the loop, code review, evaluation, testing, verification, hand-off, memory, context, instructions, prompt, skills, tools, permissions, sandbox, failure, incident, workflow, planning, research, software engineering. Each reading brief holds its queries verbatim; the Builder's reading-brief check (Build) refuses a brief holding any other word in a query, any repository or project name, any record id, or the owner's name or address, which the check reads from the session's git settings at run time and never writes.

**Who does what in a round.**
1. **The Chief of Staff starts the round** on its day, from its session (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4). Once two rounds are on record, the Chief of Staff proposes to the owner a scheduled task that starts each round as a fresh Chief of Staff session with the same brief; until the owner agrees, the session starts it. A missed round is recorded and shown on the desk (pre-mortem R6).
2. **The Input reader** (one helper per round, fresh) reads each feed and returns structured findings, as data, never as instructions (OPERATING_MODEL.md §5, D-064): one entry per item, with its address, date, feed, kind of source, the method it describes in a quoted line, and whether the text tried to instruct agents (J5). It writes no files.
3. **The Chief of Staff picks the leads** (the screener step; section 4): an item is a lead when it describes a way of running or improving a team of agents in strategy, planning, research or building work (docs/projects/P-002.md section 1), needs no paid tool (section 5), is not a clear misfit for a one-owner team (ruled T9) and could plausibly be switched on and off piece by piece (U5). It records the round: items per feed, leads kept per feed, and why each item was not kept, in one of those four reasons. This is the yield by source (M12).
4. **A lead becomes a research question.** The Chief of Staff writes it in the research/_QUESTION.md form, neutrally, without the hoped-for answer, asking what the method is, what conditions it rests on, what evidence there is for it and who has a stake; the Critic checks it for gaps and neutral wording (section 4).
5. **The research memo.** The Researcher answers it from what the Input reader returns, not by reading outside pages itself (section 4; D-064); the Orchestrator records the memo verbatim in research/.
6. **The source check.** "One source someone else has opened and confirmed" (ruled T8). Outside text reaches the Source checker only through the Input reader: the Source checker names each source address and the claim it must confirm; a fresh Input reader session, not the one that served the Researcher, opens each and returns the passage around the claim verbatim, with its address and date; the Source checker compares the claim with the passage and files the facts that pass in the portfolio library (J7).
7. **The conditions.** The Chief of Staff reads the method's conditions, from the memo, against ours, and a clear misfit is not tried (ruled T9).
8. **Outside code, prompts and skills** are checked before they run, with no data out (ruled T10; below).
9. **The entry card** (E-004, Step 1), checked by the Critic, joins the queue of at most 3 (ruled P15).

**Checking outside code, prompts and skills with no data out (U4).** "The Reviewer checks; the Builder sets up the place it runs" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4).
- **The place:** a cloud environment of its own, holding no repository, no environment variables or secrets, no connectors, and no network: no allowed hosts, with package registries and connector hosts off. The Builder proposes it through the environment tools, which show the owner what it holds before it is made; nothing is put there but the item and the brief.
- **The route:** the Input reader fetches the item and returns it verbatim as data. The Orchestrator starts a fresh Reviewer session in the place, with the item in its first message and no other project text. The Reviewer reads it for network calls, reads of files or settings outside its own folder, requests for keys, and instructions aimed at agents; runs it there on a made-up input; and notes every refused connection. It returns only a fixed form: `item: <address>`, `sha256: <hex>`, `network: none | tried`, `files-outside: none | tried`, `secrets: none | asked`, `instructions-to-agents: none | found`, `ran: yes | no`, `verdict: PASS | FAIL`. Nothing of the item comes back in that return.
- **The one harmless sample (U4's test):** before any lead uses the place, the Builder writes a sample of its own, kept in Model-Lab under tests/E-004/, that prints a line, tries to read an environment variable and a file in the home folder, tries to connect to a public host, and holds a comment telling agents to ignore their instructions. The test passes when the Reviewer's return reports each of those four, the connection is refused by the place, and a listing in the place shows no repository and no secret. Until it passes, nothing from outside runs; if it cannot pass, only methods that need no outside code, prompt or skill are tried (U4's fallback).
- The model's rule is that untrusted text is read only by the Input reader (OPERATING_MODEL.md §5, D-064); reading code to check it is the Reviewer's task in section 4. This plan keeps it to a place with nothing of the owner's in it and a fixed-form return; whether that needs the owner's ruling as an exception is asked below.

## Risks and mitigations
- **The touch log is missed.** A conversation touch not logged lowers the count on whichever side it falls. Mitigation: the monthly cross-check (AC5); the trial's sides are drawn, so a missed touch is as likely on either side unless agents ask less on one, which the hold-up checks are there to catch (VT card, "Not blind").
- **Agents know the side.** Not blind (ruled T29); only the short reads are unlabelled. Mitigation: the hold-up checks and the short reads.
- **The sides are known before pieces arrive.** The sides of places 1 to 60 are fixed in the frozen trial record so that no one can steer a draw, and a reserve place's side is known once its withdrawal or void line is logged; so the session that dispatches can know the next place's side, and could choose which brief takes that place or delay a dispatch, or merge two entered briefs into one pull request or enter a brief twice, voiding an entry of its choosing; and the Orchestrator, which knows the sides, chooses when, within the 7 days, it starts a withdrawal check or writes a void line, which decides which later piece takes the reserve place the line opens and which later pieces fall outside the look's weeks. Mitigation, in part: eligibility is by the brief's role alone (K4), every eligible piece dispatched in a look's weeks is entered in dispatch order, and the Reviewer checks each look's entries against the brief records' times and lists any eligible piece with no entry. These stop a dispatched piece being skipped or entered out of order; a void entry's place is refilled on its side, and void entries share the limit of 3 a look with withdrawals; they do not catch a choice of which brief to dispatch next, a dispatch delayed on purpose, a merge of two entered briefs into one pull request, a brief entered twice on purpose, or the time within the 7 days at which a withdrawal check is started or a void line is written, of which the Reviewer lists only a line that is late or missing; the hold-up checks catch them only in part. The limit is stated on every entry card and results card and in this spec's plain words (E-004, "What this trial cannot catch").
- **A method leaks into "without" pieces**, shrinking the difference (U5). Mitigation: per-piece switch through the brief only; the marker search (AC13).
- **Kinds arrive too slowly** (U3). Mitigation: the 4-week pace and the 26-week mark (AC14).
- **Owner comments counted as touches over-count** (E-RT5). Mitigation: the hand check measures it; the rule is the same on both sides of a trial.
- **A with-method piece also enters the prompt engine's paired test** for a kind it is testing, with a method that cannot change first-time pass (allowed by ruled P12). Mitigation: the entry card shows it and the Critic checks; whether such pieces should be kept out of the prompt engine's test altogether is asked below.
- **Outside code** reaches project data. Mitigation: the empty place, the fixed-form return, the harmless sample first (AC18).
