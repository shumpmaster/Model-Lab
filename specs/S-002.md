## In plain words
This plan sets down three things before anything is counted or tried. First, the rules for counting: what a piece of work is, what counts as you stepping in on it, what counts as it being reopened for a fault, and which kind of work it is. The records cannot show what you said in conversation, so those touches are logged by hand as they happen. Before the rules are trusted, they are tried on a sample of 60 past merged pull requests, 20 from each repository or all it has, with the shortfall listed, whether or not each turns out to be a piece, with up to 2 more for each awkward case the records can show; someone other than this plan's writer codes them, and a third checks every awkward case against the records, including the cases no sample can find. Second, the trial: each method you let through is tried on 30 new pieces of one kind against 30 without, each piece's side fixed in advance by a shuffle no one can steer, judged 30 days after the last piece; because the sides are written down in advance, whoever hands out the work can see which side the next piece will fall on, and could choose which piece to hand out next or hold one back, or merge two handed-out pieces into one pull request or hand one out twice, which the checks catch only in part; a piece dropped, handed out twice or merged with another has its place refilled from three spare places, on the same side, and at most three such places are used a look, a fourth stopping the trial and bringing it to you; if the first look leans the method's way without passing, one planned second look adds 30 more each way. The fixed plan is experiments/E-004.md. One choice in it was yours, and it was put to you on a card of its own: the line at two looks, which is how sure a trial must be before a method passes (the smaller the line, the surer). You chose 0.097 on 2026-11-11 (decisions/questions/P-002-two-look-line.md): the line as worked out so far, which lets a method that does nothing pass up to about 0.106 of the time at some starting shares (a starting share is the share of a kind's pieces that need you without the method, which no one knows before a trial), rather than one held at most 1 in 10 at every share (0.093), which, at the nine starting shares worked out (1 in 10 to 9 in 10), passes a true halving up to about 1 in 100 less often, and at any starting share takes the second look, with its 60 more pieces, up to about 4 in 100 more often. Third, the sources: four kinds of feed read every two weeks, the leads the Chief of Staff picks from them, and the path from a lead to research, a source check and an entry card, with outside code checked in a place that holds none of your data. Nothing here is irreversible; no trial starts before this plan is frozen, the starting count is on record and the ceilings are set.

## The design

### M8 — the counting rules

M8 is done "when what counts as a piece, a touch and a reopening is written as rules, the rules have been applied by hand to a sample of past pieces from all three repositories with every case and its result listed, and what they cannot catch is on record" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 3). The terms are section 8's and section 6's: a **piece** "is a merged pull request, in any of the three repositories, that changes built work, a plan or research; one that only files or answers a question, or only adds records, is not a piece" (ruled T1); a **touch** "is a time the owner stepped in on a piece before it merged: a question about it, a fix the owner made, or a send-back; merging is not a touch" (ruled T2); a **reopening** is "a piece reopened for a fault within 30 days of merging", "marked by the reviewer of the later change, not the piece's maker" (section 8; ruled T25); a **kind of work** is "built work, plans or research, the three kinds a piece may change" (section 8). The rules below write these out. Each rule has its awkward cases (E-codes), which the hand check tests.

**How rules and terms are named.** The counting rules have ids of their own: K1 to K4 (path classes and kinds), RP1 and RP2 (pieces), RT1 to RT7 (touches), C1 (the first independent check) and RR1 to RR3 (reopenings); their awkward cases are E-RP1 to E-RP6, E-K1 to E-K3, E-RT1 to E-RT6 and E-RR1 to E-RR3. In this spec a ruled term of docs/gates/P-002/trial-terms.md is always written "ruled T<n>" or "ruled P<n>", and a risk of the pre-mortem "pre-mortem R<n>".

**Path classes (rule K1).** Every changed file is put in one class by its real path, by research/Q-017-memo.md §1's twelve path rules, in that order, with three changes: (i) a question or decision file is one under a top-level `questions/` or `decisions/` folder only (the memo's labels went by folder name at any depth and so caught test and template copies, §3 and §5); (ii) in the Personal-Org-Operating-Model repository, the operating model's own text (its OPERATING_MODEL.md and playbook files) is built work, since it is that repository's product; (iii) nothing else changes. The memo's rules 1 to 3 are written in labels; K1 reads each label as the real paths that research/Q-017-extract/README.md, "Labels in place of paths", gives that label, with change (i) applied to rule 1. The classes are built, plan, research, records, owner question or answer, and other.

**What a piece is.**
- **RP1.** A pull request merged into the default branch of Personal-Org-Operating-Model, Model-Lab or Service-Desk, as GitHub's record of the pull request says (so a squash or rebase merge counts, though the commit extract coded only two-parent merges, research/Q-017-extract/README.md).
- **RP2.** It is a piece when at least one changed file is built, plan or research (K1). When every changed file is records, owner question or answer, or other, it is not a piece (ruled T1).
- *E-RP1:* work that lands by a local merge or a direct push, with no pull request (Service-Desk had 434 such plain commits and 73 such merges, research/Q-017-memo.md §1): not a piece; the count gives their number per month apart, as work the measure cannot see.
- *E-RP2:* a pull request closed without merging: not a piece; an owner's close can be a send-back of the piece that later carries the work (RT3(b)).
- *E-RP3:* rule and guidance prose outside the operating model repository (AGENTS.md, readmes, governance prose: K1 "other"): not a piece.
- *E-RP4:* one pull request carrying the work of several briefs: one piece.
- *E-RP5:* a pull request merged by a workflow bot that changes built, plan or research files: a piece; the bot's merge is not a touch.
- *E-RP6:* a revert merged as a pull request: a piece of its own, and a reopening of the piece it reverts (RR1(a)).

**Which kind a piece is (U3: a piece that touches several kinds).**
- **K2.** A piece has one kind: the kind of the brief that dispatched its work, by the role dispatched alone, as the brief record's name or the `Agent-Session:` trailer gives it: Builder, Check author, tools-builder or release → built work; Definer → plans; Researcher, Source checker or Input reader → research. A brief to the Chief of Staff or the Orchestrator, or to any role not in this list, is not mapped by K2, since its kind would depend on what the brief says, which the count does not read; K3 gives its kind.
- **K3.** A piece with no brief, with a brief K2 does not map, or whose briefs are of more than one kind, has the kind with the most changed lines among its built, plan and research files (K1); a tie goes to built work, then plans.
- **K4.** For a trial, the kind is fixed at the draw from the brief's role by K2, and does not change with what the piece's files turn out to be; a piece whose merged files are mostly of another kind keeps its kind and is listed. A piece whose kind K2 cannot give at dispatch (a Chief of Staff or Orchestrator brief, a role not in K2's list, or no brief) is not eligible for a trial, since its kind is not known when its side is drawn.
- So a piece that touches several kinds counts once, under one kind, for the count by kind and for a trial; it is eligible only for a trial of that kind. V1's own figures count all kinds together (ruled T32), so the kind rule changes no figure of V1.
- *E-K1:* a piece touching several kinds (Q-017 flags 175, 50 and 38 pull requests as touching more than one kind, by its own rule, which counted records and question files as kinds, §1, "By kind"). *E-K2:* a brief role not in K2's list, the Chief of Staff's and the Orchestrator's included (K3 applies, listed). *E-K3:* Service-Desk's scripted research, dispatched by control files, not briefs (research by K2, the Researcher's role).

**What a touch is.** A touch counts on a piece when it falls between the piece's dispatch and its merge. A piece's dispatch is the commit time of the first commit that adds its brief record (the file that holds the brief whose number the piece's `Agent-Session:` trailers name: in Model-Lab `docs/briefs/<n>-<role>.md`; in the other two repositories the file their briefs or control files are kept in, which the extract's README states and the Critic checks against the repositories before the sample is drawn, step 1 below); with no brief record, the author time of the piece's first commit. A record **names a piece** when its text holds, as a whole word or path, `#<n>`, `PR <n>` or `pull request <n>` for the piece's pull request number n; `brief <b>` or `briefs/<b>-` for a brief b whose work the piece carries; the spec id that the piece's `Spec:` trailers give; or the full path of a file the piece changes.
- **RT1, a question.** (a) A question put to the owner, as a question file (first added under top-level `questions/`) or in conversation as a decision record (first added under top-level `decisions/`), whose record names the piece; its time is the commit time of the commit that first adds the record; (b) any comment by the owner on the pull request, or a review the owner left as "commented", before merging: counted without reading it, since the count holds no free text; (c) a question in the touch log (U1's fallback, below) that names the piece.
- **RT2, a fix.** (a) A commit in the pull request under the owner's identity that changes a built, plan or research file, when it was committed through GitHub's web editor or carries no `Agent-Session:` trailer; (b) a fix in the touch log.
- **RT3, a send-back.** (a) A review by the owner asking for changes; (b) a pull request the owner closed unmerged whose work later merges in another pull request (the same head branch, or a brief number in both's `Agent-Session:` trailers), closed before the later one merges, which counts on the later piece; (c) a send-back in the touch log.
- **RT4, not touches.** Merging; an approving review; the ruling that started the piece, given before its dispatch; the owner reading merged work, short reads included; an agent's FAIL verdict; a question about another piece; a commit under the owner's identity that only records the owner's own answer (it counts under RT1 if that question is about the piece, and not again as a fix).
- **RT5, outside the count for now.** Whether a ruling the operating model requires of the owner (a phase gate, a value target) is a touch on the pieces it rules is open (Open question 3). Such a ruling names a project or a milestone, not a piece, and no field of the extract or the count says which pieces it lands on. So until the owner rules, it is not counted as a touch, has no code in J1 or J9, and is not coded in the hand check or in any trial's deciding measure; if the owner rules it in, a new revision of this spec says which pieces each ruling lands on, and by what field, before any is counted.
- **RT6.** A touch on work that two pieces carry counts on each.
- **RT7.** An owner's instruction given unasked, in conversation, that changes a piece already under way is logged in the touch log as an instruction (J9) and counted as a touch, with its own code, apart from send-backs. This is this plan's reading of "stepped in"; the owner's scenario card asks whether it holds (Open question 2), and its own code lets the count drop it if the owner rules it out.
- *E-RT1:* an owner-identity commit made by an agent session under the owner's settings (Q-017 §2: "An agent session may commit under the owner's settings"): counted as the agent's when it carries an `Agent-Session:` trailer. *E-RT2:* a commit under a tool's default identity, which Q-017 could not tie to anyone (§2): not a touch; listed. *E-RT3:* an owner's web-editor commit adding an answer file (Q-017 §2: all 147 owner-identity plain commits were single-file commits that GitHub committed): a touch only through RT1. *E-RT4:* a question whose record names only a milestone or a project, not the piece: not a touch unless the touch log names the piece. *E-RT5:* an owner comment that is only thanks: counted by RT1(b) all the same; the hand check measures how often that over-counts. *E-RT6:* a question asked in conversation in the past, before the touch log: the records cannot show it (below).

**First independent check and reopening (the hold-up measures, ruled T25 and T32).**
- **C1.** A piece passed its first independent check when the first round of its independent check is PASS: the Critic's plan review for plans (`reviews/<spec id>/plan.md`, by the piece's `Spec:` trailer), the Reviewer's review for built work (`reviews/<pull request number>/reviewer.md`), the Source checker's check for research (`research/Q-<n>-source-check.md`, by the question id in the piece's research file names). The first round's verdict is the first `Verdict: PASS` or `Verdict: FAIL` line after the record's `## Round 1` heading, or, in a record with no round headings, its first such line. A piece with no such record is left out of this measure only, and listed.
- **RR1.** A piece is reopened for a fault when, within 30 days of its merge, (a) it is reverted, in whole or in part, by a merged commit whose subject starts "Revert" and whose message names one of the piece's commits after "This reverts commit", or whose subject names the piece's pull request number; (b) the reviewer of a later change marks, in that change's review record, the line `Reopens: <repository>#<pull request number>` for a fault the later change fixes (a reviewer, never the piece's maker, ruled T25); or (c) for a trial piece, the owner would not have accepted it on a short read (ruled P14).
- **RR2.** Not reopenings: later work that builds on the piece; a change made because the owner ruled something new; a records update.
- **RR3.** A reopening found after 30 days does not count. A piece whose 30 days have not passed is pending, never counted as held up.
- *E-RR1:* a revert not worded "Revert" (Q-017 §4: such reverts are not found): caught only by RR1(b). *E-RR2:* a later pull request that shares a file or names the number (Q-017 §4's coarse tests): not a reopening by itself; the hand check lists them as candidates. *E-RR3:* a fix that names nothing it fixes: caught only by RR1(b).

**What the records cannot show, and how each case is caught.** Research/Q-017-memo.md §5 lists what the records cannot show: "Decisions, approvals and reviews given in conversation, or in pull-request reviews and comments", "Pull-request titles, and pull requests closed without merging", "The owner's time", "Work dropped before any commit", "Fixes that do not name what they fix". Each case:

| Case | What catches it from the start of the count | In past records |
|---|---|---|
| Questions, fixes and send-backs given in conversation | The touch log, kept by hand as they happen (U1's fallback; ruled T29) | Not shown |
| Owner reviews and comments on pull requests | GitHub's pull-request records, read by the count where the session can read them (RT1(b), RT3(a)) | Not in Q-017's extract; the hand check's extract takes them where it can |
| Pull requests closed unmerged | GitHub's records (RT3(b)) | Not in Q-017's extract; the hand check's extract takes them where it can |
| Fixes that name nothing; reverts worded otherwise | The reviewer's `Reopens:` mark (RR1(b)) | Not shown |
| Who acted through an identity | The `Agent-Session:` trailer rule (RT2, E-RT1); otherwise not shown | Not shown |
| Work dropped before any commit | Not caught; a drawn trial piece that is withdrawn is listed (E-004, Exclusions) | Not shown |
| The owner's time | Not counted; touches are the measure (docs/projects/P-002.md section 6) | Not shown |

Where GitHub's records cannot be read for a repository (a risk already on record, "Facts relied on"), every rule that needs them (RT1(b), RT3(a), RT3(b), and RP1 for squash and rebase merges) is recorded as "untested" for that repository, in the hand check and in the count, never as "none"; that repository's pull requests then come from its merge commits alone, as in Q-017.

**U1's fallback, applied from the first piece the count covers.** The owner and the Chief of Staff record each touch as it happens (docs/projects/P-002.md section 7, U1). This plan's design: the Chief of Staff logs every question, fix, send-back and unasked instruction given in conversation as one line in the touch log (J9) when it happens; a touch the owner gives to another session is passed by that session to the Chief of Staff in its handback, and logged from there; the owner may add a line through the desk. A line's time of writing is the commit time of the commit that adds it, and git keeps it; a line counts for a trial's look only when that commit is before the look is judged, and the Reviewer reports each look's result both with and without the lines committed after their piece merged, both of which the results card states (E-004, Exclusions). The count reads the log beside the records. If a monthly cross-check finds touches in the records missing from the log, or the log cannot be kept, the target is put to the owner again (U1's fallback).

**The hand check (U1's test), by the route the owner ruled for the record count** (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4, the M8 to M10 row; decisions/questions/P-002-q017-stop.md, P-002-q017-decide.md and P-002-q017-next.md). It runs on the rules as the Critic's plan review passes them, before the owner's scenario card, so the card carries its result; a rule it shows wrong is corrected in this draft and the Critic re-checks that rule's cases only. Everything it samples, draws and extracts is fixed below, before the extract exists; the Orchestrator chooses nothing.

5. **The Critic checks the coding of every rule's edge cases against the records**: for every pull request or commit the memo lists under an edge case, it opens the records (the repositories and GitHub's records) and says whether the rule's result is right. It reads every owner comment counted under RT1(b) and says how many were not stepping in (E-RT5), and it does the checks the edge-case table gives for E-RT4, E-RR1 and E-RR3.
6. **On record:** the memo and the Critic's check list what the rules cannot catch, which, with the table above, closes M8's last clause. A miscoded edge case that the rules cannot be fixed to catch moves that kind of touch to the touch log (U1's fallback).

The Definer does not build the extract or apply the rules.

### M9 — the trial design

The fixed plan is experiments/E-004.md; every trial copies it into its own trial record before its first piece is drawn. It carries every ruled term in its ruled words, with its number: the shuffled draws, 30 each way in the same weeks (ruled T23); the second look of 30 more each way (ruled T16, T15); the hold-up checks (ruled T25); judging 30 days after the last piece (ruled P1); the stops and voids (ruled P6, P7, P8); one method per kind at a time (ruled P11); 5 short reads per look (ruled P14); the entry card with its fixed plan and the results card with the Critic's view, the owner's two approvals (ruled T12, T13, T27, T28, T31); the queue of 3 and the two-week limit (ruled P15, P16); the summary after five trials (ruled P4); the stop at a low starting count (ruled P5); and the guards beside the prompt engine (ruled P12, P19, P20).

It also sets:
- **How a method is switched on and off piece by piece, and how the entry card shows it (U5):** only through what one piece's brief hands its agent; never through a role file, AGENTS.md, a template, a pack or a shared file; with a marker phrase the Reviewer searches for in every "without" piece at each look (E-004, Step 3).
- **How each piece's side is fixed, so no one can steer it (ruled T23, T29):** the sides of places 1 to 60 of each look are fixed in the frozen trial record before any piece is entered, from the trial id, the look and the place number alone, in blocks of 10 with 5 each way; a reserve place (61 to 63), used only to refill a withdrawal or a void entry, has no side in the record and takes the side of the withdrawn piece or void entry it refills, as the log's withdrawal or void line names it; each eligible piece dispatched in a look's weeks takes the next place in the order it is dispatched; the Reviewer re-derives every side of places 1 to 60 and checks each reserve place's side against its withdrawal or void line (E-004, Step 4). Since the sides are known ahead, the session that dispatches can choose which brief takes the next place, or merge two entered briefs into one pull request or enter a brief twice, which this design does not catch (risks, below).
- **Withdrawals and pieces that never merge:** a drawn piece is withdrawn only when three conditions recorded in the records all hold, checked by a fresh Reviewer session that is not given the piece's side; a void entry (a second entry for a brief already entered, or the later of two entries whose work merges in one pull request) is refilled from the same reserve places on its side, as a withdrawal is; withdrawals and void entries together at most 3 per look, a fourth stopping the trial; a piece still unmerged 60 days after its dispatch is settled by a fixed rule, so no piece in places 1 to 60 holds its look for more than 60 days after its dispatch plus the 30 days before judging, and a reserve piece adds its own wait, which depends on the kind's pace (E-004, Exclusions). The 3 and the 60 days, and the 7 days within which a withdrawal check is started once a piece meets the conditions, or a void line is written once an entry is void (E-004, Exclusions), are this plan's figures for the owner's scenario card.
- **Each kind's expected trial length (U3):** from the count's first 4 weeks, the weeks to each look at that pace, on each entry card and in each status update; a kind that would take more than 26 weeks to its first look comes to the owner under U3's fallback before any entry card for it (E-004, Step 2). The 26 is this plan's figure for the owner's scenario card.
- **How many of this project's agents run at once:** at most two (the session that starts a helper, and one helper); a trial adds no agent to any piece. So no tier question is needed; a change needing three or more goes to the owner as a tier question first (docs/projects/P-002.md section 5).

**The line at two looks (ruled P18).** The owner left it open, to rule on a card of its own before this design freezes (decisions/questions/P-002-design-gate.md, Part 7 and "How it is carried out"), and ruled 0.097 on that card on 2026-11-11, after the independent run passed (decisions/questions/P-002-two-look-line.md; reviews/P-002-two-look-line/reviewer.md, PASS at round 3). experiments/E-004/line.py computes both versions, and its output is recorded beside it (experiments/E-004/line-output.txt): (a) the line as worked out so far, 0.097 at each look (any line from 0.09616 up to, not including, 0.09725 decides the same); (b) the largest line at which a method that does nothing passes at most 1 time in 10 at every starting share, 0.093 (any line from 0.09227 up to, not including, 0.09359 decides the same). Their figures are in E-004, "Deciding threshold": how often each passes a method that does nothing and a true halving, the two bands of starting shares (about 0.31 to 0.43 and about 0.57 to 0.69) at which (a) passes a method that does nothing more than 1 in 10 of the time, each worst case at both of its starting shares, how often each takes the second look, and five of the script's eight assumptions (A1, A2, A3, A5 and A6) in plain words. E-004's line names the one the owner ruled, (a) 0.097; (b) was not chosen. Someone other than this plan's writer ran the script before the card went to the owner.

### M10 — the sources and how they are read

**The feeds, read every 14 days** (the owner's design: "Feeds set up that target preminent sites/sources/content - those feeds are read by screeners", decisions/questions/P-002-groundwork-shortlist.md, answer 2). More than one kind of source, since two security feeds overlapped by "only 2.5% to 4.0%" and cheap alerts found less than a full search in one test (docs/projects/P-002.md section 2; pre-mortem R8); few, since more search went with less past a point in one study (Q-011-11, the same section). This plan's choice, for the owner's scenario card:

| Feed | Kind of source | Read in each round |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Makers' engineering write-ups: the engineering blogs of the leading AI labs (Anthropic's, OpenAI's) | Every post since the last round |
| F2 | Research preprints: the new listings of arXiv's software-engineering (cs.SE) and multi-agent systems (cs.MA) sections, searched with the general words below | The 20 newest matches since the last round |
| F3 | Independent practitioners: Simon Willison's weblog, and the Thoughtworks Technology Radar when a new volume is out | Every post on agents and agent practice since the last round |
| F4 | Release notes of the tools the team's sessions run in: the agent tool's published changelog, and GitHub's changelog | Every entry since the last round |

*Reserve feeds*, used when a feed is replaced: Martin Fowler's site; the Google DeepMind blog; Hacker News, searched with the general words below. A feed that yields no kept lead in 3 rounds in a row is replaced by a reserve feed of another kind, and the change is reported in the next status update (pre-mortem R8: "One source yields nothing for several rounds"). Exact addresses are recorded by the Input reader at the first round.

**The general words.** Every search query or page fetch carries only general words about a method, never repository names, project text, records or the owner's details (docs/projects/P-002.md section 8). The queries are made only from these words and their plain combinations: agent, agents, multi-agent, orchestration, delegation, autonomy, approval, human in the loop, code review, evaluation, testing, verification, hand-off, memory, context, instructions, prompt, skills, tools, permissions, sandbox, failure, incident, workflow, planning, research, software engineering. Each reading brief holds its queries verbatim; the Builder's reading-brief check (Build) refuses a brief holding any other word in a query, any repository or project name, any record id, or the owner's name or address, which the check reads from the session's git settings at run time and never writes.

**Who does what in a round.**
1. **The Chief of Staff starts the round** on its day, from its session (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4). Once two rounds are on record, the Chief of Staff proposes to the owner a scheduled task that starts each round as a fresh Chief of Staff session with the same brief; until the owner agrees, the session starts it. A missed round is recorded and shown on the desk (pre-mortem R6).
2. **The Input reader** (one helper per round, fresh) reads each feed and returns structured findings, as data, never as instructions (OPERATING_MODEL.md §5, D-064): one entry per item, with its address, date, feed, kind of source, the method it describes in a quoted line, and whether the text tried to instruct agents (J5). It writes no files.
3. **The Chief of Staff picks the leads** (the screener step; section 4): an item is a lead when it describes a way of running or improving a team of agents in strategy, planning, research or building work (docs/projects/P-002.md section 1), needs no paid tool (section 5), is not a clear misfit for a one-owner team (ruled T9) and could plausibly be switched on and off piece by piece (U5). It records the round: items per feed, leads kept per feed, and why each item was not kept, in one of those four reasons. This is the yield by source (M12).
4. **A lead becomes a research question.** The Chief of Staff writes it in the research/_QUESTION.md form, neutrally, without the hoped-for answer, asking what the method is, what conditions it rests on, what evidence there is for it and who has a stake; the Critic checks it for gaps and neutral wording (section 4).
5. **The research memo.** The Researcher answers it from what the Input reader returns, not by reading outside pages itself (section 4; D-064); the Orchestrator records the memo verbatim in research/.
6. **The source check.** "One source someone else has opened and confirmed" (ruled T8). Outside text reaches the Source checker only through the Input reader: the Source checker names each source address and the claim it must confirm; a fresh Input reader session, not the one that served the Researcher, opens each and returns the passage around the claim verbatim, with its address and date; the Source checker compares the claim with the passage and files the facts that pass in the portfolio library (J7).
7. **The conditions.** The Chief of Staff reads the method's conditions, from the memo, against ours, and a clear misfit is not tried (ruled T9).
8. **Outside code, prompts and skills** are checked before they run, with no data out (ruled T10; below).
9. **The entry card** (E-004, Step 1), checked by the Critic, joins the queue of at most 3 (ruled P15).

**Checking outside code, prompts and skills with no data out (U4).** "The Reviewer checks; the Builder sets up the place it runs" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4).
- **The place:** a cloud environment of its own, holding no repository, no environment variables or secrets, no connectors, and no network: no allowed hosts, with package registries and connector hosts off. The Builder proposes it through the environment tools, which show the owner what it holds before it is made; nothing is put there but the item and the brief.
- **The route:** the Input reader fetches the item and returns it verbatim as data. The Orchestrator starts a fresh Reviewer session in the place, with the item in its first message and no other project text. The Reviewer reads it for network calls, reads of files or settings outside its own folder, requests for keys, and instructions aimed at agents; runs it there on a made-up input; and notes every refused connection. It returns only a fixed form: `item: <address>`, `sha256: <hex>`, `network: none | tried`, `files-outside: none | tried`, `secrets: none | asked`, `instructions-to-agents: none | found`, `ran: yes | no`, `verdict: PASS | FAIL`. Nothing of the item comes back in that return.
- **The one harmless sample (U4's test):** before any lead uses the place, the Builder writes a sample of its own, kept in Model-Lab under tests/E-004/, that prints a line, tries to read an environment variable and a file in the home folder, tries to connect to a public host, and holds a comment telling agents to ignore their instructions. The test passes when the Reviewer's return reports each of those four, the connection is refused by the place, and a listing in the place shows no repository and no secret. Until it passes, nothing from outside runs; if it cannot pass, only methods that need no outside code, prompt or skill are tried (U4's fallback).
- The model's rule is that untrusted text is read only by the Input reader (OPERATING_MODEL.md §5, D-064); reading code to check it is the Reviewer's task in section 4. This plan keeps it to a place with nothing of the owner's in it and a fixed-form return; whether that needs the owner's ruling as an exception is asked below.

## Risks and mitigations
- **The touch log is missed.** A conversation touch not logged lowers the count on whichever side it falls. Mitigation: the monthly cross-check (AC5); the trial's sides are drawn, so a missed touch is as likely on either side unless agents ask less on one, which the hold-up checks are there to catch (VT card, "Not blind").
- **Agents know the side.** Not blind (ruled T29); only the short reads are unlabelled. Mitigation: the hold-up checks and the short reads.
- **The sides are known before pieces arrive.** The sides of places 1 to 60 are fixed in the frozen trial record so that no one can steer a draw, and a reserve place's side is known once its withdrawal or void line is logged; so the session that dispatches can know the next place's side, and could choose which brief takes that place or delay a dispatch, or merge two entered briefs into one pull request or enter a brief twice, voiding an entry of its choosing. Mitigation, in part: eligibility is by the brief's role alone (K4), every eligible piece dispatched in a look's weeks is entered in dispatch order, and the Reviewer checks each look's entries against the brief records' times and lists any eligible piece with no entry. These stop a dispatched piece being skipped or entered out of order; a void entry's place is refilled on its side, and void entries share the limit of 3 a look with withdrawals; they do not catch a choice of which brief to dispatch next, a dispatch delayed on purpose, a merge of two entered briefs into one pull request or a brief entered twice on purpose, which the hold-up checks catch only in part. The limit is stated on every entry card and results card and in this spec's plain words (E-004, "What this trial cannot catch").
- **A method leaks into "without" pieces**, shrinking the difference (U5). Mitigation: per-piece switch through the brief only; the marker search (AC13).
- **Kinds arrive too slowly** (U3). Mitigation: the 4-week pace and the 26-week mark (AC14).
- **Owner comments counted as touches over-count** (E-RT5). Mitigation: the hand check measures it; the rule is the same on both sides of a trial.
- **A with-method piece also enters the prompt engine's paired test** for a kind it is testing, with a method that cannot change first-time pass (allowed by ruled P12). Mitigation: the entry card shows it and the Critic checks; whether such pieces should be kept out of the prompt engine's test altogether is asked below.
- **Outside code** reaches project data. Mitigation: the empty place, the fixed-form return, the harmless sample first (AC18).
