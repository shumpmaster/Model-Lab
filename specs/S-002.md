## In plain words
This plan sets down three things before anything is counted or tried. First, the rules for counting: what a piece of work is, what counts as you stepping in on it, what counts as it being reopened for a fault, and which kind of work it is. The records cannot show what you said in conversation, so those touches are logged by hand as they happen. Before the rules are trusted, a sample of 60 past pieces, 20 from each repository, is coded by them by someone other than this plan's writer, and every rule's awkward cases are checked against the records by a third. Second, the trial: each method you let through is tried on 30 new pieces of one kind against 30 without, drawn by shuffle, judged 30 days after the last piece, with one planned second look of 30 more each way; the fixed plan is experiments/E-004.md. One choice in it was yours, on a card of its own before it freezes: the line at two looks. You chose 0.097 on 2026-11-11 (decisions/questions/P-002-two-look-line.md): the line as worked out so far, which lets a method that does nothing pass up to about 0.106 of the time at some starting shares, rather than one held at most 1 in 10 at every share (0.093), which passes a true halving up to about 1 in 100 less often and takes the second look, with its 60 more pieces, up to about 4 in 100 more often. Third, the sources: four kinds of feed read every two weeks, the leads the Chief of Staff picks from them, and the path from a lead to research, a source check and an entry card, with outside code checked in a place that holds none of your data. Nothing here is irreversible; no trial starts before this plan is frozen, the starting count is on record and the ceilings are set.

## The design

### M8 — the counting rules

M8 is done "when what counts as a piece, a touch and a reopening is written as rules, the rules have been applied by hand to a sample of past pieces from all three repositories with every case and its result listed, and what they cannot catch is on record" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 3). The terms are section 8's and section 6's: a **piece** "is a merged pull request, in any of the three repositories, that changes built work, a plan or research; one that only files or answers a question, or only adds records, is not a piece" (T1); a **touch** "is a time the owner stepped in on a piece before it merged: a question about it, a fix the owner made, or a send-back; merging is not a touch" (T2); a **reopening** is "a piece reopened for a fault within 30 days of merging", "marked by the reviewer of the later change, not the piece's maker" (section 8; T25); a **kind of work** is "built work, plans or research, the three kinds a piece may change" (section 8). The rules below write these out. Each rule has its awkward cases (E-codes), which the hand check tests.

**Path classes (rule K1).** Every changed file is put in one class by its real path, by research/Q-017-memo.md §1's twelve path rules, in that order, with three changes: (i) a question or decision file is one under a top-level `questions/` or `decisions/` folder only (the memo's labels went by folder name at any depth and so caught test and template copies, §3 and §5); (ii) in the Personal-Org-Operating-Model repository, the operating model's own text (its OPERATING_MODEL.md and playbook files) is built work, since it is that repository's product; (iii) nothing else changes. The classes are built, plan, research, records, owner question or answer, and other.

**What a piece is.**
- **P1.** A pull request merged into the default branch of Personal-Org-Operating-Model, Model-Lab or Service-Desk, as GitHub's record of the pull request says (so a squash or rebase merge counts, though the commit extract coded only two-parent merges, research/Q-017-extract/README.md).
- **P2.** It is a piece when at least one changed file is built, plan or research (K1). When every changed file is records, owner question or answer, or other, it is not a piece (T1).
- *E-P1:* work that lands by a local merge or a direct push, with no pull request (Service-Desk had 434 such plain commits and 73 such merges, research/Q-017-memo.md §1): not a piece; the count gives their number per month apart, as work the measure cannot see.
- *E-P2:* a pull request closed without merging: not a piece; an owner's close can be a send-back of the piece that later carries the work (T3).
- *E-P3:* rule and guidance prose outside the operating model repository (AGENTS.md, readmes, governance prose: K1 "other"): not a piece.
- *E-P4:* one pull request carrying the work of several briefs: one piece.
- *E-P5:* a pull request merged by a workflow bot that changes built, plan or research files: a piece; the bot's merge is not a touch.
- *E-P6:* a revert merged as a pull request: a piece of its own, and a reopening of the piece it reverts (R1).

**Which kind a piece is (U3: a piece that touches several kinds).**
- **K2.** A piece has one kind: the kind of the brief that dispatched its work, by the role dispatched: Builder, Check author, tools-builder or release → built work; Definer, or a Chief of Staff or Orchestrator brief for a plan, brief, spec, gate case or project document → plans; Researcher, Source checker or Input reader → research.
- **K3.** A piece with no brief, or whose briefs are of more than one kind, has the kind with the most changed lines among its built, plan and research files (K1); a tie goes to built work, then plans.
- **K4.** For a trial, the kind is fixed at the draw, from the brief (K2), and does not change with what the piece's files turn out to be; a piece whose merged files are mostly of another kind is listed.
- So a piece that touches several kinds counts once, under one kind, for the count by kind and for a trial; it is eligible only for a trial of that kind. V1's own figures count all kinds together (T32), so the kind rule changes no figure of V1.
- *E-K1:* a piece touching several kinds (Q-017 flags 175, 50 and 38 pull requests as touching more than one kind, by its own rule, which counted records and question files as kinds, §1, "By kind"). *E-K2:* a brief role outside the list in K2 (K3 applies, listed). *E-K3:* Service-Desk's scripted research, dispatched by control files, not briefs (research by K2, the Researcher's role).

**What a touch is.** A touch counts on a piece when it falls between the piece's dispatch (the time its brief is recorded; with no brief, its first commit's author date) and its merge.
- **T1, a question.** (a) A question put to the owner, as a question file or in conversation, whose record names the piece: its pull request number, a brief whose work it carries, its spec id or a file it changes; (b) any comment by the owner on the pull request, or a review the owner left as "commented", before merging: counted without reading it, since the count holds no free text; (c) a question in the touch log (U1's fallback, below) that names the piece.
- **T2, a fix.** A commit in the pull request under the owner's identity that changes a built, plan or research file, when it was committed through GitHub's web editor or carries no `Agent-Session:` trailer; or a fix in the touch log.
- **T3, a send-back.** A review by the owner asking for changes; a pull request the owner closed unmerged whose work later merges in another pull request (the same branch or the same brief), which counts on the later piece; or a send-back in the touch log.
- **T4, not touches.** Merging; an approving review; the ruling that started the piece, given before its dispatch; the owner reading merged work, short reads included; an agent's FAIL verdict; a question about another piece; a commit under the owner's identity that only records the owner's own answer (it counts under T1 if that question is about the piece, and not again as a fix).
- **T5.** A ruling the operating model requires of the owner (a phase gate, a value target) is a question about the piece it rules (T2's words), and counts as a touch on it. No method can remove such a touch; it falls on both sides of a trial alike.
- **T6.** A touch on work that two pieces carry counts on each.
- **T7.** An owner's instruction given unasked, in conversation, that changes a piece already under way is logged as a send-back. This is this plan's reading of "stepped in"; the owner's scenario card asks whether it holds (Open questions).
- *E-T1:* an owner-identity commit made by an agent session under the owner's settings (Q-017 §2: "An agent session may commit under the owner's settings"): counted as the agent's when it carries an `Agent-Session:` trailer. *E-T2:* a commit under a tool's default identity, which Q-017 could not tie to anyone (§2): not a touch; listed. *E-T3:* an owner's web-editor commit adding an answer file (Q-017 §2: all 147 owner-identity plain commits were single-file commits that GitHub committed): a touch only through T1. *E-T4:* a question whose record names only a milestone or a project, not the piece: not a touch unless the touch log names the piece. *E-T5:* an owner comment that is only thanks: counted by T1(b) all the same; the hand check measures how often that over-counts. *E-T6:* a question asked in conversation in the past, before the touch log: the records cannot show it (below).

**First independent check and reopening (the hold-up measures, T25, T32).**
- **C1.** A piece passed its first independent check when the first round of its independent check is PASS: the Critic's plan review for plans, the Reviewer's review for built work, the Source checker's check for research, as recorded under reviews/ or research/. A piece with no independent check is left out of this measure only, and listed.
- **R1.** A piece is reopened for a fault when, within 30 days of its merge, (a) it is reverted, in whole or in part; (b) the reviewer of a later change marks, in that change's review record, the line `Reopens: <repository>#<pull request number>` for a fault the later change fixes (a reviewer, never the piece's maker, T25); or (c) for a trial piece, the owner would not have accepted it on a short read (P14).
- **R2.** Not reopenings: later work that builds on the piece; a change made because the owner ruled something new; a records update.
- **R3.** A reopening found after 30 days does not count. A piece whose 30 days have not passed is pending, never counted as held up.
- *E-R1:* a revert not worded "Revert" (Q-017 §4: such reverts are not found; R1(a) needs the reviewer's mark or a revert record). *E-R2:* a later pull request that shares a file or names the number (Q-017 §4's coarse tests): not a reopening by itself; the hand check lists them as candidates. *E-R3:* a fix that names nothing it fixes: caught only by R1(b).

**What the records cannot show, and how each case is caught.** Research/Q-017-memo.md §5 lists what the records cannot show: "Decisions, approvals and reviews given in conversation, or in pull-request reviews and comments", "Pull-request titles, and pull requests closed without merging", "The owner's time", "Work dropped before any commit", "Fixes that do not name what they fix". Each case:

| Case | What catches it from the start of the count | In past records |
|---|---|---|
| Questions, fixes and send-backs given in conversation | The touch log, kept by hand as they happen (U1's fallback; T29) | Not shown |
| Owner reviews and comments on pull requests | GitHub's pull-request records, read by the count where the session can read them (T1(b), T3) | Not in Q-017's extract; the hand check's extract takes them where it can |
| Pull requests closed unmerged | GitHub's records (T3) | Not in Q-017's extract |
| Fixes that name nothing; reverts worded otherwise | The reviewer's `Reopens:` mark (R1(b)) | Not shown |
| Who acted through an identity | The `Agent-Session:` trailer rule (T2, E-T1); otherwise not shown | Not shown |
| Work dropped before any commit | Not caught; a drawn trial piece that is withdrawn is listed (E-004, Exclusions) | Not shown |
| The owner's time | Not counted; touches are the measure (docs/projects/P-002.md section 6) | Not shown |

**U1's fallback, applied from the first piece the count covers.** The owner and the Chief of Staff record each touch as it happens (docs/projects/P-002.md section 7, U1). This plan's design: the Chief of Staff logs every question, fix and send-back given in conversation as one line in the touch log (J9) when it happens; a touch the owner gives to another session is passed by that session to the Chief of Staff in its handback, and logged from there; the owner may add a line through the desk. The count reads the log beside the records. If a monthly cross-check finds touches in the records missing from the log, or the log cannot be kept, the target is put to the owner again (U1's fallback).

**The hand check (U1's test), by the route the owner ruled for the record count** (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4, the M8 to M10 row; decisions/questions/P-002-q017-stop.md, P-002-q017-decide.md and P-002-q017-next.md). It runs on the rules as the Critic's plan review passes them, before the owner's scenario card, so the card carries its result; a rule it shows wrong is corrected in this draft and the Critic re-checks that rule's cases only.
1. **The Orchestrator builds the extract.** A new research question's extract folder, with a README in the form of research/Q-017-extract/README.md, from full clones of the three repositories and, where the session can read them, GitHub's pull-request records; no free text.
2. **The sample.** *Window:* pull requests merged from 2026-10-01 to the extract's cut-off date (the day it is built, stated in its README). *Random part, 60:* 20 from each repository. In Service-Desk and Personal-Org-Operating-Model, 10 from those whose 30 days after merging have passed at the cut-off and 10 from the rest; in Model-Lab, whose first merged pull requests are in November (research/Q-017-memo.md §1), 20 from all. All merged pull requests are drawn from, pieces or not, so rule P2 is tested. *Drawn how:* each repository's list is put in the order of the SHA-256 of `<seed>:<repository>:<pull request number>`, lowest first, and the first are taken; the seed is the full id of the start commit of the Orchestrator's extract brief, recorded in the brief before the extract is built. *Edge-case part, up to 2 per case:* for each case E-P1 to E-R3 above, the first 2 pull requests in the window, in the same order, not already drawn, that match that case's field filter (each filter recorded in the README); a case with no match is listed as "not seen". *Why 60, 20 each:* 60 is the size of the starting count; with 20 from a repository, a rule that fails on 1 in 10 of its pieces is seen at least once with chance 1 − 0.9^20, about 0.88.
3. **The extract's fields** (per sampled pull request; one file per repository; no subject, title, message, comment or path text): repository; pull request number; created, merged and closed times; merged or not; the merging account's class (owner, agent, bot, other); the head branch's role prefix only (`build/<role>/`, `orch/`, other); per commit: hash, author class, committer class (GitHub web editor, agent, other), author and commit times, whether it carries an `Agent-Session:` trailer and its role and brief number, whether its subject starts "Revert", the pull request numbers its subject names; per changed file: its class by K1 (computed by the Orchestrator on the real path, which is not written), its status, its added and removed lines; per review: reviewer class and state (approved, changes requested, commented, dismissed) and time relative to merging; owner comment counts on the pull request and on its lines, each before or after merging; for each brief whose work the pull request carries (by trailer): its role and whether its record exists; the number of question and decision files the pull request first adds or deletes; the number of decision records (top-level decisions/) whose text names the pull request number or one of its briefs, found by the Orchestrator by pattern, with each one's answer time relative to the pull request's open and merge times; the number of intake items of owner instructions or answers dated while it was open; for the 30 days after merging: later merged pull requests sharing a file (Q-017 §4's left-out files left out), later commits naming its number, reverts, and review records with a `Reopens:` line naming it; whether its 30 days are complete at the cut-off; whether GitHub's records were read for its repository ("read" or "not read").
4. **The Researcher applies the rules as a memo.** The research question holds these rules verbatim and the extract, and no share hoped for (D-055). The memo lists every sampled pull request with its result under each rule (piece or not, kind, each touch found with its rule, first check, reopened or pending), every case it could not decide from the extract and why, and the cases of E-P1 to E-R3 seen and not seen.
5. **The Critic checks the coding of every rule's edge cases against the records**: for every pull request the memo lists under an edge case, it opens the records (the repositories and GitHub's records) and says whether the rule's result is right. It also reads every owner comment counted under T1(b) and says how many were not stepping in (E-T5).
6. **On record:** the memo and the Critic's check list what the rules cannot catch, which, with the table above, closes M8's last clause. A miscoded edge case that the rules cannot be fixed to catch moves that kind of touch to the touch log (U1's fallback).

The Definer does not build the extract or apply the rules.

### M9 — the trial design

The fixed plan is experiments/E-004.md; every trial copies it into its own trial record before its first piece is drawn. It carries every ruled term in its ruled words, with its number: the shuffled draws, 30 each way in the same weeks (T23); the second look of 30 more each way (T16, T15); the hold-up checks (T25); judging 30 days after the last piece (P1); the stops and voids (P6, P7, P8); one method per kind at a time (P11); 5 short reads per look (P14); the entry card with its fixed plan and the results card with the Critic's view, the owner's two approvals (T12, T13, T27, T28, T31); the queue of 3 and the two-week limit (P15, P16); the summary after five trials (P4); the stop at a low starting count (P5); and the guards beside the prompt engine (P12, P19, P20).

It also sets:
- **How a method is switched on and off piece by piece, and how the entry card shows it (U5):** only through what one piece's brief hands its agent; never through a role file, AGENTS.md, a template, a pack or a shared file; with a marker phrase the Reviewer searches for in every "without" piece at each look (E-004, Step 3).
- **Each kind's expected trial length (U3):** from the count's first 4 weeks, the weeks to each look at that pace, on each entry card and in each status update; a kind that would take more than 26 weeks to its first look comes to the owner under U3's fallback before any entry card for it (E-004, Step 2). The 26 is this plan's figure for the owner's scenario card.
- **How many of this project's agents run at once:** at most two (the session that starts a helper, and one helper); a trial adds no agent to any piece. So no tier question is needed; a change needing three or more goes to the owner as a tier question first (docs/projects/P-002.md section 5).

**The line at two looks (P18).** The owner left it open, to rule on a card of its own before this design freezes (decisions/questions/P-002-design-gate.md, Part 7 and "How it is carried out"). experiments/E-004/line.py computes both versions, and its output is recorded beside it (experiments/E-004/line-output.txt): (a) the line as worked out so far, 0.097 at each look (any line from 0.09616 up to, not including, 0.09725 decides the same); (b) the largest line at which a method that does nothing passes at most 1 time in 10 at every starting share, 0.093 (any line from 0.09227 up to, not including, 0.09359 decides the same). Their figures are in E-004, "Deciding threshold": how often each passes a method that does nothing and a true halving, the two bands of starting shares (about 0.31 to 0.43 and about 0.57 to 0.69) at which (a) passes a method that does nothing more than 1 in 10 of the time, each worst case at both of its starting shares, how often each takes the second look, and four of the script's eight assumptions (A1, A3, A5 and A6) in plain words. E-004 works with either, and its frozen line names the one the owner rules. Someone other than this plan's writer runs the script before the card goes to the owner.

### M10 — the sources and how they are read

**The feeds, read every 14 days** (the owner's design: "Feeds set up that target preminent sites/sources/content - those feeds are read by screeners", decisions/questions/P-002-groundwork-shortlist.md, answer 2). More than one kind of source, since two security feeds overlapped by "only 2.5% to 4.0%" and cheap alerts found less than a full search in one test (docs/projects/P-002.md section 2; pre-mortem R8); few, since more search went with less past a point in one study (Q-011-11, the same section). This plan's choice, for the owner's scenario card:

| Feed | Kind of source | Read in each round |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Makers' engineering write-ups: the engineering blogs of the leading AI labs (Anthropic's, OpenAI's) | Every post since the last round |
| F2 | Research preprints: the new listings of arXiv's software-engineering (cs.SE) and multi-agent systems (cs.MA) sections, searched with the general words below | The 20 newest matches since the last round |
| F3 | Independent practitioners: Simon Willison's weblog, and the Thoughtworks Technology Radar when a new volume is out | Every post on agents and agent practice since the last round |
| F4 | Release notes of the tools the team's sessions run in: the agent tool's published changelog, and GitHub's changelog | Every entry since the last round |

*Reserve feeds*, used when a feed is replaced: Martin Fowler's site; the Google DeepMind blog; Hacker News, searched with the general words below. A feed that yields no kept lead in 3 rounds in a row is replaced by a reserve feed of another kind, and the change is reported in the next status update (pre-mortem R8: "One source yields nothing for several rounds"). Exact addresses are recorded by the Input reader at the first round.

**The general words.** Every search query or page fetch carries only general words about a method, never repository names, project text, records or the owner's details (docs/projects/P-002.md section 8). The queries are made only from these words and their plain combinations: agent, agents, multi-agent, orchestration, delegation, autonomy, approval, human in the loop, code review, evaluation, testing, verification, hand-off, memory, context, instructions, prompt, skills, tools, permissions, sandbox, failure, incident, workflow, planning, research, software engineering. Each reading brief holds its queries verbatim; the Builder's reading-brief check (Build) refuses a brief holding any other word in a query, any repository or project name, any record id, or the owner's name or address, which the check reads from the session's git settings at run time and never writes.

**Who does what in a round.**
1. **The Chief of Staff starts the round** on its day, from its session (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4). Once two rounds are on record, the Chief of Staff proposes to the owner a scheduled task that starts each round as a fresh Chief of Staff session with the same brief; until the owner agrees, the session starts it. A missed round is recorded and shown on the desk (pre-mortem R6).
2. **The Input reader** (one helper per round, fresh) reads each feed and returns structured findings, as data, never as instructions (OPERATING_MODEL.md §5, D-064): one entry per item, with its address, date, feed, kind of source, the method it describes in a quoted line, and whether the text tried to instruct agents (J5). It writes no files.
3. **The Chief of Staff picks the leads** (the screener step; section 4): an item is a lead when it describes a way of running or improving a team of agents in strategy, planning, research or building work (docs/projects/P-002.md section 1), needs no paid tool (section 5), is not a clear misfit for a one-owner team (T9) and could plausibly be switched on and off piece by piece (U5). It records the round: items per feed, leads kept per feed, and why each item was not kept, in one of those four reasons. This is the yield by source (M12).
4. **A lead becomes a research question.** The Chief of Staff writes it in the research/_QUESTION.md form, neutrally, without the hoped-for answer, asking what the method is, what conditions it rests on, what evidence there is for it and who has a stake; the Critic checks it for gaps and neutral wording (section 4).
5. **The research memo.** The Researcher answers it from what the Input reader returns, not by reading outside pages itself (section 4; D-064); the Orchestrator records the memo verbatim in research/.
6. **The source check.** "One source someone else has opened and confirmed" (T8). Outside text reaches the Source checker only through the Input reader: the Source checker names each source address and the claim it must confirm; a fresh Input reader session, not the one that served the Researcher, opens each and returns the passage around the claim verbatim, with its address and date; the Source checker compares the claim with the passage and files the facts that pass in the portfolio library (J7).
7. **The conditions.** The Chief of Staff reads the method's conditions, from the memo, against ours, and a clear misfit is not tried (T9).
8. **Outside code, prompts and skills** are checked before they run, with no data out (T10; below).
9. **The entry card** (E-004, Step 1), checked by the Critic, joins the queue of at most 3 (P15).

**Checking outside code, prompts and skills with no data out (U4).** "The Reviewer checks; the Builder sets up the place it runs" (docs/projects/P-002.md section 4).
- **The place:** a cloud environment of its own, holding no repository, no environment variables or secrets, no connectors, and no network: no allowed hosts, with package registries and connector hosts off. The Builder proposes it through the environment tools, which show the owner what it holds before it is made; nothing is put there but the item and the brief.
- **The route:** the Input reader fetches the item and returns it verbatim as data. The Orchestrator starts a fresh Reviewer session in the place, with the item in its first message and no other project text. The Reviewer reads it for network calls, reads of files or settings outside its own folder, requests for keys, and instructions aimed at agents; runs it there on a made-up input; and notes every refused connection. It returns only a fixed form: `item: <address>`, `sha256: <hex>`, `network: none | tried`, `files-outside: none | tried`, `secrets: none | asked`, `instructions-to-agents: none | found`, `ran: yes | no`, `verdict: PASS | FAIL`. Nothing of the item comes back in that return.
- **The one harmless sample (U4's test):** before any lead uses the place, the Builder writes a sample of its own, kept in Model-Lab under tests/E-004/, that prints a line, tries to read an environment variable and a file in the home folder, tries to connect to a public host, and holds a comment telling agents to ignore their instructions. The test passes when the Reviewer's return reports each of those four, the connection is refused by the place, and a listing in the place shows no repository and no secret. Until it passes, nothing from outside runs; if it cannot pass, only methods that need no outside code, prompt or skill are tried (U4's fallback).
- The model's rule is that untrusted text is read only by the Input reader (OPERATING_MODEL.md §5, D-064); reading code to check it is the Reviewer's task in section 4. This plan keeps it to a place with nothing of the owner's in it and a fixed-form return; whether that needs the owner's ruling as an exception is asked below.

## Acceptance criteria (scenarios; owner-approved)
Which milestone each serves: AC1 to AC6 deliver M8; AC7 to AC15 deliver M9, with experiments/E-004.md; AC16 to AC19 deliver M10; AC20 and AC21 are how the screening runs, watches itself and is reviewed once it graduates, built in Build.

- AC1 [trace: V1 → AC1] — When a pull request merges into the default branch of any of the three repositories, then the count records it as a piece exactly when at least one changed file is built, plan or research by K1, and records one that changes only records, question or answer files or other prose as not a piece; work that lands with no pull request is counted apart, per month, and is never a piece.
- AC2 [trace: V1 → AC2] — When the owner asks, is asked, fixes or sends back between a piece's dispatch and its merge (rules T1 to T7), then the count records a touch on that piece with its rule and its source (records or touch log); and when the owner only merges, approves, reads after merging, or gave the ruling that started the piece, then no touch is recorded.
- AC3 [trace: V1 → AC3] — When a later change within 30 days of a piece's merge reverts it, or its reviewer marks `Reopens:` for that piece, or the owner would not have accepted it on a short read, then the piece is recorded as reopened; a piece whose 30 days have not passed is shown as pending; and each piece's first independent check is recorded as passed, failed or none.

## Risks and mitigations
- **The touch log is missed.** A conversation touch not logged lowers the count on whichever side it falls. Mitigation: the monthly cross-check (AC5); the trial's sides are drawn, so a missed touch is as likely on either side unless agents ask less on one, which the hold-up checks are there to catch (VT card, "Not blind").
- **Agents know the side.** Not blind (T29); only the short reads are unlabelled. Mitigation: the hold-up checks and the short reads.
- **A method leaks into "without" pieces**, shrinking the difference (U5). Mitigation: per-piece switch through the brief only; the marker search (AC13).
- **Kinds arrive too slowly** (U3). Mitigation: the 4-week pace and the 26-week mark (AC14).
- **Owner comments counted as touches over-count** (E-T5). Mitigation: the hand check measures it; the rule is the same on both sides of a trial.
- **A with-method piece also enters the prompt engine's paired test** for a kind it is testing, with a method that cannot change first-time pass (allowed by P12). Mitigation: the entry card shows it and the Critic checks; whether such pieces should be kept out of the prompt engine's test altogether is asked below.
- **Outside code** reaches project data. Mitigation: the empty place, the fixed-form return, the harmless sample first (AC18).
