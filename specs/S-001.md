# Spec S-001 — A fair test of the changes (M2)

status: draft   frozen_at: <commit>   workstream: P-001 the prompt engine
work-type: experiment   design_id: <id>   lineage: []
mechanism: Each new piece of work of the kind under test is done, or checked, twice from the same starting point at the same time, once as things are and once with one candidate change, and a held-back check on a different AI model judges both blind, confirmed by a small sample the owner looks at; the test is sized and written down before any run (experiments/E-003.md).
problem: Work is sent back by its check more often than it need be, and the records seldom show why, so a change must be shown to help before it enters the operating model (docs/PROJECT.md section 2).
milestones: M2

## In plain words
Past tasks cannot be re-run faithfully, so the test cannot use them, and the check of the judges was not run, by the owner's ruling. So the test uses new work as it arrives: each new piece is done twice in a side copy, once as things are and once with one change, and the two results are judged blind by a check the work never sees, on a different AI model. The owner looks at about eight of the judgements for each kind. Three changes are tested, one for each kind of work, one kind after another. Nothing live changes while it runs.

What the owner should know before setting the budget:
- **Size.** At 60 pieces a kind, the test sees a real 20-point gain about 4 times in 5 under the library's assumption about how often the two versions disagree, and about 1 time in 2 for research and built work if they disagree as often as chance allows. Those figures hold only if each kind reaches 60 pieces and the judge is right every time; a judge wrong 1 time in 10 lowers them to about 1 in 2 or less.
- **Smaller gains.** Two of the three changes aim at causes that, by the records, could give at most about 11 to 19 points. A real gain of that size will usually read "not shown": that would say the change did not meet the owner's target, not that it does not help.
- **Time.** New work arrives at its own pace. At the pace of the Service Desk, the one project still producing all three kinds, built work would take about 7 to 15 weeks, plans 21 to 42, research 32 to 64; all three one after another about 60 to 121 weeks (one to over two years), or 32 to 64 if collected together. It would be faster only if the other projects resume. Plans and built work cannot start at all until each new piece's brief, starting point and model are recorded when it is dispatched, which today happens only for the Service Desk's scripted research (Model-Lab's brief records carry the brief and the model, not the starting point; open question 5).
- **Cost.** About 730 runs and 130 million tokens for all three at 60 pieces; the owner sets the budget, the size and the order at the Build gate, and must also rule on lines of the project document that still say "the same past tasks".

## The design

### Why new work (U2's fallback) and a held-back check (U3's fallback)
- The replay check sent every kind to the fallback: no plan has a recorded brief, the rebuilt built-work replay failed, and research cannot be replayed without the web (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md, "Outcome per kind"). So the test compares new work with and without each change over the same period (decisions/questions/P-001-open-unknowns.md, U2).
- The judge-consistency check was not run (decisions/questions/P-001-e002-not-tested.md). So a held-back set of checks the work never sees, and a sample the owner looks at, decide; the record states how far the checks can be trusted (U3).

### The candidate changes (one per kind)
Chosen from the count's causes. Of 84 classified returns with findings, the records name the check 10 times, material handed over 8, instructions 1, split of work 1, source access 1, the worker's own slip 2, and nothing beyond the fault 61; 41 more have no record of findings (research/Q-008-memo.md, bottom lines 3–4, §5). The text of each change, its reasons and its removal test are in experiments/E-003/C1 to C3. These, and this spec, are withheld from the Check author (AC4).

| Change | Kind | What changes | Cause it aims at | Why this one | Most it could reach, by the records |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Plans | The writer reads the role file of the agent that will check its plan (35 words added to the brief) | Instructions: what the writer is given to read | Plans pass first time least often (about 5%); 16 of 18 plan returns with findings were faults internal to the work with the material available; the check's standard is in no writer's pack | Large: about 95% of plans fail first time; whether reading the standard prevents those faults is what the test asks |
| C2 | Built work | The builder is handed, by name, the spec section, rulings, paths and locked checks its task rests on (two sentences: 10 fixed words plus a list the harness fills, 11 counting the list as one word) | Material handed over | The largest named cause for built work (5 of 16 returns with findings); long inputs lower accuracy (Q-002 F-c) | About 11 to 19 points: 3 to 5 of 16 classified returns, with 60% failing first time; 2 of the 5 came from inputs that changed mid-round, which the test leaves out |
| C3 | Research | The source check returns a memo only for a fault it can show, not for a source it could not open (64 words added to the check's brief) | The check itself | The largest named cause for research (7 of 14); two of those, and the one source-access return, came from sources that could not be opened | About 11 to 16 points: 2 or 3 of 19 memos |

Why so few, and why not the others:
- **One change per kind** keeps each kind's test a single comparison at 1 in 40 (V1); a second change on the same kind would add 2 runs per piece and a second chance of keeping a change by luck.
- **Split of work** (1 return) and **the AI model** (named by no record; changing it is out of scope, docs/PROJECT.md section 1) are not tested.
- **A mechanised check** (pre-mortem R6) is not among the three, since each change here is text applied in a copy (docs/PROJECT.md section 4); it is a candidate for a later registration.
- **C1 and C2 each add one reading and nothing else** (round 1's C2 also added a rule for disagreeing inputs; it is dropped). **C3 changes one rule of the check**, what counts as a fault, which is the change itself. Each is under 70 words, can be removed cleanly, and carries its own removal test (pre-mortem R6).

### What the test can and cannot show
- **It is a test of the owner's target.** It is sized for a 20-point gain (V1). For a real gain of 10 points, 60 pairs show it about 1 time in 2 if the change only ever helps, and about 1 time in 3 to 1 time in 7 otherwise (experiments/E-003/sizing.py). C2 and C3 aim at causes that, by the records, can give at most about 11 to 19 and 11 to 16 points (table above). So "not shown" for C2 or C3 will be the usual result even if the change helps by as much as its cause allows. It means the change did not meet the owner's 20-point target; it does not mean the change does no good. The owner may prefer to test C1 and C2 only, or to accept this for C3 (open question 3).
- **The measure is a stand-in.** The deciding pass is the held-back check's, judged on a different model, not the first-time pass at the project's real check that V1 names. The real round-1 verdict is reported beside it; how closely the two agree is reported, not tested.
- **Judge noise is not in the sizing above.** Judge consistency was never measured. If the judge got one verdict in ten wrong at random, 60 pairs would see a real 20-point gain only about 1 time in 2 at best (0.55, 0.45 and 0.34 to 0.47 under the three assumptions; sizing.py). The owner's cards catch a judge that is often wrong, not one that is sometimes wrong.
- **Same provider, same standard.** See "What this cannot rule out" below.

### How each piece is compared
- **What counts as a new piece:** one piece of work put to its independent check (research/Q-008-memo.md §1.1), dispatched after its kind's comparison opens, in a participating project, not part of this test, and on record at dispatch: for plans and built work the worker's brief, start commit and AI model; for research the memo's submission and the **source check's** brief, start commit and model, since C3 changes the check's brief (E-003, Method step 2).
- **Which projects:** every project of the owner's whose work goes to an independent check while the test runs. Today that is the Service Desk, whose scripted research already records the source check's brief, model and start commit in its control files; Model-Lab, except this test's own pieces, whose brief records (docs/briefs/) carry the brief text and the model but no start commit (29 of 29 have a model line, none a start-commit line), so none of its pieces is eligible today either; and the operating model repository, which has had no commit since 24 October (experiments/E-003/output.txt, arrivals parts 3 and 4).
- **Recording is a precondition for plans and built work.** None of the Service Desk's 6 plans and 8 built series of the last two weeks has any record of its brief at dispatch, and Model-Lab's records lack the start commit (experiments/E-003/output.txt, arrivals part 4). Until the Chief of Staff records each one at dispatch, or the brief-and-model record (M3) does, no plan or built piece can enter (open question 5).
- **How a piece is assigned:** every eligible piece is done both ways. I weighed two ways:

  | | Each new piece done both ways, in side copies (chosen) | Each new piece assigned at random to one way |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pieces needed for a 20-point gain at 1 in 40, about 80% power | about 60 per kind (below) | about 94 plans, 174 research memos, 190 built pieces (two groups; normal approximation) |
  | Runs per piece | 4 (2 doers, 2 judges); research 3 | 2 if run as side copies (1 doer, 1 judge); 1 if the live work takes the change |
  | Fairness | Each piece is its own comparison: same task, start, inputs, model and hour | Balanced only on average; tasks differ between the groups |
  | Live work | Untouched | Half the live work would run on an untested change, which section 4 rules out until M5 |

  Done both ways needs fewer pieces, and pieces are what is scarce (below); it is the fairer comparison; and it leaves the real work alone. Its cost is the extra runs per piece.
- **Same period, model and inputs (pre-mortem R5):** both arms start from the piece's own start commit (for research, the check's), in fresh copies, with the same brief apart from the change text, on the same model, within the same hour and within 7 days of the real dispatch. A change of served model splits the analysis (E-003, Exclusions 7).
- **Inputs that change mid-round** (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md, finding 8): a piece whose named inputs change, or whose real work takes in later commits, between its start and its real round-1 submission is left out, decided mechanically from git before judging (E-003, Exclusions 1).
- **Research** is compared on the check, not the work (C3): the real memo is checked twice, with and without the change, so no research doer runs are needed.

### Size, runs and time
**Method.** For each kind, the one-sided exact test on the pairs where the two arms differ, at 1 in 40 (V1). How often the two arms differ is not known from any record, so power is given under three assumptions: one-way (the change only ever helps), the library's middle assumption (25% of pairs favour the change, 5% the other way; portfolio library Q-003-F-i), and independent arms from each kind's starting figure (plans 5%, research 26%, built work 40%; docs/PROJECT.md section 6). The working is in experiments/E-003/sizing.py; it gives the library's "10 discordant pairs need 9, 20 need 15", and 0.80 at 60 pairs under the middle assumption.

**What counts as one run:** one helper session started for the test, whatever it does; a session that errors counts. Per pair: plans and built work 4 runs, research 3; plus 1 run per kind for its held-back check, and a 10% reserve for reruns (E-003, Sample). **Price:** E-001 used 2,526,069 tokens over 14 runs, about 180,000 a run (src/E-001/RESULT.md, Step 2: cost). By kind of run, E-001's plan and built doers averaged about 239,000 and its judges about 160,000, so a plan or built pair may cost about 11% more than the flat price below; research runs were cut short in E-001, so their cost is the least known.

**How fast new work arrives.** `python3 experiments/E-003/arrivals.py` derives it from the records, and anyone can re-run it; its full output, and sizing.py's, as run for this draft, is experiments/E-003/output.txt.
- Q-008 counts pieces (plans 44, built work 80, research 19; docs/PROJECT.md:99) but states no dates. I took its window from the first and last check records the count rests on, which Q-008 names but does not call first and last: the operating model's S-002 build review, added on 24 September, and Model-Lab's corrections confirmation of Q-002 to Q-007, added on 1 November: 5.57 weeks. As a cross-check, the check records each repository added before Q-008 was committed run from 30 September to 1 November in the Service Desk (601 files), 24 September to 24 October in the operating model repository (204) and 1 November only in Model-Lab (27), so no counted record falls outside the window.
- **The Service Desk's pace is the expected case:** from 30 September it gave plans 3.8, built work 10.8 and research 2.5 a week. The operating model repository has had no commit since 24 October, and Model-Lab's research was this project's own Discovery, now ending. The three projects together gave plans 7.9, built work 14.4 and research 3.4 a week; that pace returns **only if the other projects resume**, and is shown in brackets below, never as the headline.
- In the last two weeks (21 October to 3 November) the Service Desk gave plans 3.0, built work 20 and research 3.0 a week, by its file names and dispatch log. Work comes in bursts (13 built stages filed on 25 October), so short windows mislead; the five-week pace is used.

So 3 in 8 to 3 in 4 of arriving pieces enter. Eligible pieces a week at the Service Desk's pace: plans 1.4 to 2.9, built work 4.1 to 8.1, research 0.9 to 1.9.

### How the results are judged (U3's fallback)
- **The held-back checks.** One per kind, written before its kind opens, from that kind's existing check standard, never seen by the work, and stored outside the repositories with only its fingerprint recorded (governance/HOLDOUTS.md).
- **Two Check author jobs, two separate dispatches.** The Check author normally writes a spec's locked checks from the spec at Build (stage 4; governance/PACKS.toml `[roles.check-author]`). Here it has a second job that must be done blind, so the two are never one session:

  | | (a) The held-back checks (blind) | (b) S-001's own locked checks (normal) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Writes | One held-back check per kind, stored outside every repository; returns set id and fingerprint, which the Orchestrator records in governance/HOLDOUTS.md | The acceptance tests and join-tests for AC1 to AC11 and J1 to J8, in tests/acceptance/** and checks/** |
  | Pack | A per-task pack, `check-author` for task `S-001-holdout`, holding only experiments/E-003/check-author-extract.md, the role files and playbooks it names, and governance/HOLDOUTS.md; no `{spec}`, no `{ruling}`, no may_read and no include_feedback; and listing under must_not_see specs/S-001.md, experiments/E-003.md, experiments/E-003/C*.md, tests/acceptance/**, checks/**, reviews/S-001/**, docs/briefs/**, docs/gates/**, decisions/** and src/** | The role's standing pack, unchanged, with the spec |
  | Never | Sees this spec, E-003, the change texts, the locked checks of (b), or any record of the plan review or the gate | Writes or sees a held-back check: their text exists only outside every repository and is in no pack |
  | Dispatched by | The Orchestrator, once per kind, after the Build gate and before that kind opens, and only after J8's test passes | The Orchestrator, at stage 4 after the freeze, by the usual route |
  | When | First for built work (the first kind), then each later kind before it opens | Before any harness code is built |

  The per-task pack is added to governance/PACKS.toml beside the standing check-author table, which it does not replace. That file is the Chief of Staff's lane (governance/SURFACES.md); the Chief of Staff proposes the change, the owner approves it as a rule change, and it must be merged before the Build gate, where the Orchestrator confirms it is in place (Needed outside). J8's test reads that pack entry: it fails while the entry is missing, so the blind dispatch cannot happen without it (J8).
- **The judges.** Fable 5.1, the only model available outside the doers' 5.5 line within the one provider the plan allows (docs/reports/2026-11-03-design-environment-check.md; docs/PROJECT.md section 8, spend ceiling in section 5). For plans and built work each arm's deliverable is judged in its own session; for research the one memo is judged in one session, and each check version's pass counts only where that judge passes the memo. Built work must also pass the stage's locked checks, run by script, the one judgement here that no model makes.
- **The owner's sample.** Each card is one output with the judge's verdict, without arm labels; the owner answers "should this have gone back? yes / no / can't tell" (yes is FAIL, no is PASS). 8 cards a kind: 4 from the pairs that decide the result, 2 from pairs both arms passed and 2 from pairs both failed, so a judge that wrongly passes, wrongly fails or wrongly splits can each be seen. The draw follows a seed written down before the kind opens. If fewer than 6 cards get a yes or no, more are drawn, up to 12. The owner's answer replaces the judge's verdict and each pair is scored again. The judge is confirmed when at least 6 cards are answered and the owner disagrees on at most 2; otherwise no change is kept for that kind from this run (E-003, Step 5 and Deciding threshold).
- **What this cannot rule out.** Every judge comes from the same provider as the work, so a bias they share, such as preferring text written the way that provider's models write, would not show; whether Fable 5.1 is a different model family is not established. Judge consistency was never measured, so the same output might get a different verdict if judged again, and the sizing above assumes none. The held-back checks are written by a Check author on the same model line as the work, from the same standard that C1 shows the writer, so a gain under C1 may partly be work shaped to the check rather than better work. The deciding pass is the held-back check's, not the real check's. Eight cards per kind can catch a judge that is often wrong, not one that is sometimes wrong. The owner's cards are the only judgement from outside the provider. The record of each kind states these limits.

### Lessons from the replay run
| Lesson (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md) | How this test handles it |
|---|---|
| A judge ended its turn waiting on background tests, with no verdict (finding 4) | The judge brief bars background tasks and asks for the verdict line last; one fixed follow-up message, then one new judge session; then the pair is left out and listed (E-003, Sample, reruns) |
| Every helper got context beyond its brief: Model-Lab's instructions file, the Builder's session id, an attribution line (finding 5) | Each run gets the copy's own instructions file; the harness records each run's starting context and compares the two arms' before judging; any difference beyond the change text is a set-up fault; what cannot be removed is the same in both arms and named in the result (E-003, Exclusions 3) |
| Inputs merged mid-round made a replay unfair (finding 8) | Pieces whose inputs change between start and round-1 submission are left out, decided from git before judging (E-003, Exclusions 1) |
| A helper's reads of its own saved output under /root failed the leak test by the letter (ruling 1) | Reading the file the harness saved of the run's own output, at the path it named, is allowed in advance; anything else outside the copy is a leak (E-003, Exclusions 2) |

## Acceptance criteria (scenarios; owner-approved)
- AC1 [trace: V1 → AC1] — When the owner opens experiments/E-003/ at the Build gate, they find each of C1, C2 and C3 as the exact text the harness will insert, with its kind, the cause it aims at, how it is applied and its removal test; and every brief and role file in every repository is as it was, since the changes exist only as these texts.
- AC2 [trace: V1 → AC2] — When a new piece of the kind under test is dispatched in a participating project with its record made at dispatch (for plans and built work the worker's brief, start commit and model; for research the memo's submission and the source check's brief, start commit and model), then the harness runs both arms from that start commit in fresh copies, with briefs that differ only by the change paragraph, on the same served model, starting within the same hour and within 7 days of the dispatch, and records the pair; and when a piece has no such record, it does not enter and is listed.
- AC3 [trace: V1 → AC3] — When a piece's named inputs change between its start commit and its real round-1 submission, or an arm leaks, or the arms' starting contexts differ beyond the change text, then the pair is not judged as it stands: it is left out or rerun as E-003 says, listed with its reason, and never run again for a better answer.
- AC4 [trace: V1 → AC4] — When a kind's comparison opens, its held-back check already exists, written in a Check author session dispatched on the per-task pack `S-001-holdout`, which held experiments/E-003/check-author-extract.md and the standards it names and not this spec, E-003, any experiments/E-003/C*.md file or S-001's locked checks; that session is not the one that wrote S-001's locked checks, and the session that wrote them never saw a held-back check; the check is stored outside the repositories with its fingerprint recorded, and no arm's copy or brief contains it.
- AC5 [trace: V1 → AC5] — When a plan or built arm finishes, its deliverable is judged in its own session, and when both research checks have returned, the memo is judged once in its own session; every judge runs on a model other than the doers', without the arm's label, hand-back or the check versions' verdicts; for built work the stage's locked checks also run by script; a judge with no verdict line gets the one fixed follow-up and at most one new session, and the pair is then left out and listed.
- AC6 [trace: V1 → AC6] — When every pair of a kind is judged, the owner receives 8 cards, each one output without arm labels, drawn by the pre-written seed from the three groups E-003 names, each answerable in about five minutes; more are drawn, up to 12, until 6 have a yes or no; the owner's answers replace the judge's verdicts, the pairs are scored again, and the cards answered and the disagreements are recorded.
- AC7 [trace: V1 → AC7] — When the Reviewer analyses a kind, a change is kept only if the judge is confirmed (6 or more cards answered, at most 2 disagreements) and the exact one-sided test on the differing pairs clears 1 in 40 (for C3, also the false-pass guard); otherwise the record says "not shown", with the gain and its 95% range, and "no change helps" stands as an accepted result.
- AC8 [trace: V1 → AC8] — When the test is running, the owner receives, at every 20 runs and 16 weeks after each kind opens, the runs and tokens used, the pairs collected and left out, the power at the pairs collected and the weeks to the target at the pace so far, and no outcome; the owner's "go on" or "stop" is recorded; no kind starts more runs than its planned runs plus reserve.
- AC9 [trace: V1 → AC9] — When the served model of the doers or the judge changes during a kind's comparison, pairs are grouped by model, the deciding test is run on the larger group only, and the result says so.
- AC10 [trace: V2 → AC10] — When a change is kept, its result record gives the V2 measure for its kind (first-time pass rate over the first 30 pieces checked under the changed operating model, against the starting figure, at 1 in 25), how those pieces are counted from the review records, and its removal test, so the operating model can carry, monitor and remove it after M5 without Model-Lab.
- AC11 [trace: V2 → AC11] — When a kept change is removed, by its removal test or at the operating model's scheduled review, the brief or role file returns byte for byte to its text without the change.

## Join sheets
- **J1 —** change text → arm copy: the change paragraph (experiments/E-003/C<n>-*.md, the fenced block), with its fill (`<path>` for C1; `<list>` for C2), inserted as its own paragraph after the task statement of the "change" arm's brief (for C3, the source check's brief). Awkward cases: the brief has no separate task statement (insert after its first paragraph and record it); C1's plan-check role file does not exist at the start commit (piece not eligible); C2's list holds only the spec (eligible); C2's task names no spec (the list is the paths the task names); C2's list is empty (not eligible). A test marked `join-test: S-001/J1` covers it.
- **J2 —** dispatch record → pair: the recorded brief text, the start commit (full id), the repository, the kind of work and the model, as one entry per piece; for research, those of the source check, plus the memo's submission commit. Awkward cases: a record written after the dispatch or after the round-1 verdict (not eligible); a research item whose researcher is on record but whose source check is not (not eligible); a Service Desk control file holding several roles (the entry whose `role` is the dispatched one); a brief that uses the real repository's absolute path (replaced by the copy's path, nothing else changed, as in E-001); a piece with two lanes (the dispatched worker's lane only, E-001 reading 4). A test marked `join-test: S-001/J2` covers it.

## Risks and mitigations
- **Too slow (new):** pieces arrive at the owner's projects' pace; at the Service Desk's pace plans could take 21 to 42 weeks and research 32 to 64 at 60 pairs, and plans and built work cannot start until recording at dispatch exists. Mitigation: built work first; review points with power stated; the owner may choose 40 pairs, option C, or collecting together (open questions 3–5).
- **Too small (R2):** under independent arms, 60 pairs see a real 20-point gain only about half the time for research and built work, less with judge noise, and a gain below 20 points usually not at all. Mitigation: the power under each assumption, with noise and for a 10-point gain, is stated before the budget is set; a positive gain that does not clear is reported as "not shown", with its range, and read as "did not meet the target", not "does not help".
- **Fooled or biased checks (R3):** see "What this cannot rule out". Mitigation: held-back checks written without sight of the changes, blind judging, a different model, built work's locked checks by script, the owner's cards from all three groups with a confirmation rule.
- **Work shaped to the check (C1):** C1's gain may be teaching to the check. Mitigation: the owner's cards ask whether the work should have gone back; V2 re-checks in real use.
- **Changes fitted to past tasks (R4):** the changes were chosen from past records and are tested only on new work, which none of them was written from.
- **Model or setting moves (R5):** both arms always share model and start; a model change splits the analysis; kept changes carry a re-test on a new model.
- **Rule growth (R6):** C1 and C2 each add one reading only; C3 changes one rule of the check; each is under 70 words and carries a removal test.
- **Owner's time:** 8 cards per kind, about 40 minutes, 12 at most; review-point rulings on counts only.
- **A side copy reaches real work:** no arm's output is merged, pushed or copied into any real repository (E-003, Method step 3); the harness has no push rights to them.
- **Harness complexity:** the Builder must start sessions in copies with their own instructions file, which E-001 did with a working-directory hook (src/E-001/RESULT.md:166-175); the arms' context check catches what it misses.
