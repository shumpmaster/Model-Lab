# Spec S-001 — A fair test of the changes (M2)

status: draft   frozen_at: <commit>   workstream: P-001 the prompt engine
work-type: experiment   design_id: <id>   lineage: []
mechanism: Each new piece of work of the kind under test is done, or checked, twice from the same starting point at the same time, once as things are and once with one candidate change, and a held-back check on a different AI model judges both blind, confirmed by a small sample the owner looks at; the test is sized and written down before any run (experiments/E-003.md).
problem: Work is sent back by its check more often than it need be, and the records seldom show why, so a change must be shown to help before it enters the operating model (docs/PROJECT.md section 2).
milestones: M2

## In plain words
Past tasks cannot be re-run faithfully, so the test cannot use them, and the check of the judges was not run, by the owner's ruling. So the test uses new work as it arrives: each new piece is done twice in a side copy, once as things are and once with one change, and the two results are judged blind by a check the work never sees, on a different AI model. The owner looks at up to eight of the judgements for each kind. Three changes are tested, one for each kind of work, one kind after another. At the recommended size, 60 pieces for each kind, the test sees a real 20-point gain about 4 times in 5 under the library's assumption about how often the two versions disagree, and about 1 time in 2 for research and built work if they disagree as often as chance allows. It would take about 730 runs, about 130 million tokens, and months rather than weeks, because new work arrives at its own pace. Nothing live changes while it runs. The owner sets the budget, the size and the order at the Build gate, and must also rule on two lines of the project document that still say "the same past tasks".

## The design

### Why new work (U2's fallback) and a held-back check (U3's fallback)
- The replay check sent every kind to the fallback: no plan has a recorded brief, the rebuilt built-work replay failed, and research cannot be replayed without the web (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md, "Outcome per kind"). So the test compares new work with and without each change over the same period (decisions/questions/P-001-open-unknowns.md, U2).
- The judge-consistency check was not run (decisions/questions/P-001-e002-not-tested.md). So a held-back set of checks the work never sees, and a sample the owner looks at, decide; the record states how far the checks can be trusted (U3).

### The candidate changes (one per kind)
Chosen from the count's causes. Of 84 classified returns with findings, the records name the check 10 times, material handed over 8, instructions 1, split of work 1, source access 1, the worker's own slip 2, and nothing beyond the fault 61; 41 more have no record of findings (research/Q-008-memo.md, bottom lines 3–4, §5). The text of each change, its reasons and its removal test are in experiments/E-003/.

| Change | Kind | What changes | Cause it aims at | Why this one |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Plans | The writer reads the role file of the agent that will check its plan (35 words added to the brief) | Instructions: what the writer is given to read | Plans pass first time least often (about 5%); 16 of 18 plan returns with findings were faults internal to the work with the material available; the check's standard is in no writer's pack |
| C2 | Built work | The builder is handed, by name, the spec section, rulings and locked checks its task rests on (35 words plus a list the harness fills) | Material handed over | The largest named cause for built work (5 of 16 returns with findings); long inputs lower accuracy (Q-002 F-c) |
| C3 | Research | The source check returns a memo only for a fault it can show, not for a source it could not open (64 words added to the check's brief) | The check itself | The largest named cause for research (7 of 14); two of those, and the one source-access return, came from sources that could not be opened |

Why so few, and why not the others:
- **One change per kind** keeps each kind's test a single comparison at 1 in 40 (V1); a second change on the same kind would add 2 runs per piece and a second chance of keeping a change by luck.
- **Split of work** (1 return) and **the AI model** (named by no record; changing it is out of scope, docs/PROJECT.md section 1) are not tested.
- **A mechanised check** (pre-mortem R6) is not among the three, since each change here is text applied in a copy (docs/PROJECT.md section 4); it is a candidate for a later registration.
- Every change is short and self-contained, so it can be removed cleanly, and each carries its own removal test (pre-mortem R6).

### How each piece is compared
- **What counts as a new piece:** one piece of work put to its independent check (research/Q-008-memo.md §1.1), dispatched after its kind's comparison opens, in a participating project, with its brief, start commit and AI model on record at dispatch, and not part of this test (E-003, Method step 2).
- **Which projects:** every project of the owner's whose work goes to an independent check while the test runs: today the Service Desk (its second project is in Discovery, with research arriving), the operating model repository, and Model-Lab, except this test's own pieces.
- **How a piece is assigned:** every eligible piece is done both ways. I weighed two ways:

  | | Each new piece done both ways, in side copies (chosen) | Each new piece assigned at random to one way |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pieces needed for a 20-point gain at 1 in 40, about 80% power | about 60 per kind (below) | about 94 plans, 174 research memos, 190 built pieces (two groups; normal approximation) |
  | Runs per piece | 4 (2 doers, 2 judges); research 3 | 2 if run as side copies (1 doer, 1 judge); 1 if the live work takes the change |
  | Fairness | Each piece is its own comparison: same task, start, inputs, model and hour | Balanced only on average; tasks differ between the groups |
  | Live work | Untouched | Half the live work would run on an untested change, which section 4 rules out until M5 |

  Done both ways needs fewer pieces, and pieces are what is scarce (below); it is the fairer comparison; and it leaves the real work alone. Its cost is the extra runs per piece.
- **Same period, model and inputs (pre-mortem R5):** both arms start from the piece's own start commit, in fresh copies, with the same brief apart from the change text, on the same model, within the same hour and within 7 days of the real dispatch. A change of served model splits the analysis (E-003, Exclusions 7).
- **Inputs that change mid-round** (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md, finding 8): a piece whose named inputs change, or whose real work takes in later commits, between its start and its real round-1 submission is left out, decided mechanically from git before judging (E-003, Exclusions 1).
- **Research** is compared on the check, not the work (C3): the real memo is checked twice, with and without the change, so no research doer runs are needed.

### Size, runs and time
**Method.** For each kind, the one-sided exact test on the pairs where the two arms differ, at 1 in 40 (V1). How often the two arms differ is not known from any record, so power is given under three assumptions: one-way (the change only ever helps), the library's middle assumption (25% of pairs favour the change, 5% the other way; portfolio library Q-003-F-i), and independent arms from each kind's starting figure (plans 5%, research 26%, built work 40%; docs/PROJECT.md section 6). The working is in experiments/E-003/sizing.py; it gives the library's 0.70 at 50 pairs under the middle assumption, and its "10 discordant pairs need 9, 20 need 15".

**What counts as one run:** one helper session started for the test, whatever it does; a session that errors counts. Per pair: plans and built work 4 runs, research 3; plus 1 run per kind for its held-back check, and a 10% reserve for reruns (E-003, Sample). **Price:** E-001 used 2,526,069 tokens over 14 runs, about 180,000 a run (src/E-001/RESULT.md, Step 2: cost). By kind of run, E-001's plan and built doers averaged about 239,000 and its judges about 160,000, so a plan or built pair may cost about 11% more than the flat price below; research runs were cut short in E-001, so their cost is the least known.

**How fast new work arrives** (pieces a week). Over the 5.4 weeks Q-008 counts (24 September to 1 November): plans 44, built work 80, research 19 (research/Q-008-memo.md §2), so about 8, 15 and 3.5 a week. In the last week (28 October to 3 November): plans 2 (Service Desk HD1, M3C), built 5 (VT2, VT3, HD1, LA1, LA2), research 12 (Service Desk Q-016, Q-018, Q-020, Q-021; Model-Lab Q-001 to Q-008) (first round-1 review files and memo commits in git, Service-Desk and Model-Lab). The operating model repository has had no commits since 24 October. E-001 found mid-round inputs in 4 of the 16 plan and built pieces it audited (src/E-001/RESULT.md, notes), so the time below assumes 3 in 4 new pieces stay in. The range runs from the faster pace to the slower; it depends on what the owner's projects do.

### How the results are judged (U3's fallback)
- **The held-back checks.** One per kind, written by the Check author from that kind's existing check standard, before any arm runs, never seen by the work, and stored outside the repositories with only its fingerprint recorded (governance/HOLDOUTS.md). The Check author does not see the change texts.
- **The judges.** Fable 5.1, the only model available outside the doers' 5.5 line within the one provider the plan allows (docs/reports/2026-11-03-design-environment-check.md; docs/PROJECT.md section 8, spend ceiling in section 5). Each output is judged in its own session, blind to which arm made it. Built work must also pass the stage's locked checks, run by script, the one judgement here that no model makes. For research, the held-back check opens the sources itself, and a check version's pass counts only where it agrees.
- **The owner's sample.** Up to 8 cards per kind, about five minutes each: up to 6 pairs on which the arms' verdicts differ (the pairs that decide the result) and 2 passed outputs, without arm labels. The owner's answer replaces the judge's. If the owner disagrees with the judge on 3 or more, the judge is not confirmed and no change is kept for that kind from this run.
- **What this cannot rule out.** Every judge comes from the same provider as the work, so a bias they share, such as preferring text written the way that provider's models write, would not show; whether Fable 5.1 is a different model family is not established. Judge consistency was never measured, so the same output might get a different verdict if judged again. C1 shows the writer the check's standard, from which the held-back check is also written, so a gain under C1 may partly be work shaped to the check rather than better work. Eight cards per kind can catch a judge that is often wrong, not one that is sometimes wrong. The owner's cards are the only judgement from outside the provider. The record of each kind states these limits.

### Lessons from the replay run
| Lesson (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md) | How this test handles it |
|---|---|
| A judge ended its turn waiting on background tests, with no verdict (finding 4) | The judge brief bars background tasks and asks for the verdict line last; one fixed follow-up message, then one new judge session; then the pair is left out and listed (E-003, Sample, reruns) |
| Every helper got context beyond its brief: Model-Lab's instructions file, the Builder's session id, an attribution line (finding 5) | Each run gets the copy's own instructions file; the harness records each run's starting context and compares the two arms' before judging; any difference beyond the change text is a set-up fault; what cannot be removed is the same in both arms and named in the result (E-003, Exclusions 3) |
| Inputs merged mid-round made a replay unfair (finding 8) | Pieces whose inputs change between start and round-1 submission are left out, decided from git before judging (E-003, Exclusions 1) |
| A helper's reads of its own saved output under /root failed the leak test by the letter (ruling 1) | Reading the file the harness saved of the run's own output, at the path it named, is allowed in advance; anything else outside the copy is a leak (E-003, Exclusions 2) |

## Acceptance criteria (scenarios; owner-approved)
- AC1 [trace: V1 → AC1] — When the test is frozen, each of C1, C2 and C3 exists as exact text under experiments/E-003/, with its kind, the cause it aims at, how it is applied and its removal test, and no live role file or brief in any repository has changed.
- AC2 [trace: V1 → AC2] — When a new piece of the kind under test is dispatched in a participating project, and its brief, start commit and model are on record, then the harness runs both arms from that start commit in fresh copies, with briefs that differ only by the change paragraph, on the same served model, starting within the same hour and within 7 days of the dispatch, and records the pair.
- AC3 [trace: V1 → AC3] — When a piece's named inputs change between its start commit and its real round-1 submission, or an arm leaks, or the arms' starting contexts differ beyond the change text, then the pair is not judged as it stands: it is left out or rerun as E-003 says, listed with its reason, and never run again for a better answer.
- AC4 [trace: V1 → AC4] — When a kind's comparison opens, its held-back check already exists, written by the Check author without sight of the change texts, stored outside the repositories with its fingerprint recorded, and no arm's copy or brief contains it.
- AC5 [trace: V1 → AC5] — When an arm finishes, its deliverable is judged in its own session on a model other than the doers', without the arm's label or hand-back; for built work the stage's locked checks also run by script; a judge with no verdict line gets the one fixed follow-up and at most one new session, and the pair is then left out and listed.
- AC6 [trace: V1 → AC6] — When every pair of a kind is judged, the owner receives at most 8 cards without arm labels, drawn by the pre-written seed, each answerable in about five minutes; the owner's answers replace the judge's verdicts, and the number of disagreements is recorded.
- AC7 [trace: V1 → AC7] — When the Reviewer analyses a kind, a change is kept only if the judge is confirmed and the exact one-sided test on the differing pairs clears 1 in 40 (for C3, also the false-pass guard); otherwise the record says "not shown", with the gain and its 95% range, and "no change helps" stands as an accepted result.
- AC8 [trace: V1 → AC8] — When the test is running, the owner's token checkpoint receives, at every 20 runs, the runs and tokens used, the pairs collected and left out, and the weeks elapsed against the time limit; no kind starts more runs than its planned runs plus reserve.
- AC9 [trace: V1 → AC9] — When the served model of the doers or the judge changes during a kind's comparison, pairs are grouped by model, the deciding test is run on the larger group only, and the result says so.
- AC10 [trace: V2 → AC10] — When a change is kept, its result record gives the V2 measure for its kind (first-time pass rate over the first 30 pieces checked under the changed operating model, against the starting figure, at 1 in 25), how those pieces are counted from the review records, and its removal test, so the operating model can carry, monitor and remove it after M5 without Model-Lab.
- AC11 [trace: V2 → AC11] — When a kept change is removed, by its removal test or at the operating model's scheduled review, the brief or role file returns byte for byte to its text without the change.

## Join sheets
- **J1 —** change text → arm copy: the change paragraph (experiments/E-003/C<n>-*.md, the fenced block), with its fill (`<path>` for C1; `<list>` for C2), inserted as its own paragraph after the task statement of the "change" arm's brief. Awkward cases: the brief has no separate task statement (insert after its first paragraph and record it); C1's plan-check role file does not exist at the start commit (piece not eligible); C2's list holds only the spec. A test marked `join-test: S-001/J1` covers it.
- **J2 —** dispatch record → pair: the recorded brief text, the start commit (full id), the repository, the kind of work and the model, as one entry per piece. Awkward cases: a brief recorded after the dispatch (not eligible); a brief that uses the real repository's absolute path (replaced by the copy's path, nothing else changed, as in E-001); a piece with two lanes (the dispatched worker's lane only, E-001 reading 4). A test marked `join-test: S-001/J2` covers it.
- **J3 —** arm → judge: the arm's deliverable as a commit range in a fresh copy of its final commit, with a neutral label; never the hand-back or commit messages. Awkward cases: no deliverable (that arm fails); a deliverable that names the change in its own text (judged as it is, and reported); a research memo, judged in a copy with the web allowed. A test marked `join-test: S-001/J3` covers it.
- **J4 —** held-back check → judge: the held-back check's full text, read by the harness from outside the repositories and checked against its fingerprint in governance/HOLDOUTS.md before it goes into the judge's brief. Awkward cases: a fingerprint mismatch (stop; no judging until the Orchestrator and the Check author resolve it); no held-back check for the kind (the kind does not open). A test marked `join-test: S-001/J4` covers it.
- **J5 —** judge → result record: the judge's first line `Verdict: PASS` or `Verdict: FAIL`, then numbered blocking findings citing file:line; for built work, the locked checks' exit status. Awkward cases: no verdict line (the reruns); two verdict lines (the first counts, and it is reported); background tasks still running at the end (no verdict). A test marked `join-test: S-001/J5` covers it.
- **J6 —** owner's card → result record: the card's item label and the owner's answer (yes, no, can't tell), in the owner's words, filed under decisions/questions/ as an owner's answer is. Awkward cases: the owner answers some cards only (the rest keep the judge's verdict, and the result says so); fewer than 6 differing pairs (all are shown). A test marked `join-test: S-001/J6` covers it.
- **J7 —** result record → the Reviewer's analysis and M4: per kind, every pair's two outcomes, exclusions with reasons, served models, runs and tokens, the owner's answers, the seed. Awkward cases: a kind stopped at its time limit (power at that size stated); fewer than 10 pairs ("too few pieces to test"). A test marked `join-test: S-001/J7` covers it.

## Risks and mitigations
- **Too slow (new):** pieces arrive at the owner's projects' pace; plans could take most of a year at 60 pairs. Mitigation: built work first; a time limit per kind with power stated at stop; the owner may choose 40 pairs or option C.
- **Too small (R2):** under independent arms, 60 pairs see a real gain only about half the time for research and built work. Mitigation: the power under each assumption is stated before the budget is set; a positive gain that does not clear is reported as "not shown", with its range.
- **Fooled or biased checks (R3):** see "What this cannot rule out". Mitigation: held-back checks, blind judging, a different model, built work's locked checks by script, the owner's cards with a confirmation rule.
- **Work shaped to the check (C1):** C1's gain may be teaching to the check. Mitigation: the owner's cards ask whether the fault is real; V2 re-checks in real use.
- **Changes fitted to past tasks (R4):** the changes were chosen from past records and are tested only on new work, which none of them was written from.
- **Model or setting moves (R5):** both arms always share model and start; a model change splits the analysis; kept changes carry a re-test on a new model.
- **Rule growth (R6):** each change is under 70 words and carries a removal test; none adds a rule to follow.
- **Owner's time:** at most 8 cards per kind, about 40 minutes per kind.
- **A side copy reaches real work:** no arm's output is merged, pushed or copied into any real repository (E-003, Method step 3); the harness has no push rights to them.
- **Harness complexity:** the Builder must start sessions in copies with their own instructions file, which E-001 did with a working-directory hook (src/E-001/RESULT.md:166-175); the arms' context check catches what it misses.
