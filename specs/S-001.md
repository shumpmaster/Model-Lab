mechanism: Each new piece of work of the kind under test is done, or checked, twice from the same starting point at the same time, once as things are and once with one candidate change, and a held-back check on a different AI model judges both blind, confirmed by a small sample the owner looks at; the test is sized and written down before any run (experiments/E-003.md).

## In plain words
Past tasks cannot be re-run faithfully, so the test cannot use them, and the check of the judges was not run, by the owner's ruling. So the test uses new work as it arrives: each new piece is done twice in a side copy, once as things are and once with one change, and the two results are judged blind by a check the work never sees, on a different AI model. That check is kept in a private repository that only the sessions writing and judging it are started with; the work runs in other sessions, which never hold it. The owner looks at about eight of the judgements for each kind. Three changes are tested, one for each kind of work, one kind after another. Nothing live changes while it runs.

What the owner was told before setting the budget at the Build gate, with what the owner has since ruled ("The owner's rulings on the open questions"):
- **Size.** At 60 pieces a kind, the test sees a real 20-point gain about 4 times in 5 under the library's assumption about how often the two versions disagree, and about 1 time in 2 for research and built work if they disagree as often as chance allows. Those figures hold only if each kind reaches 60 pieces and the judge is right every time; a judge wrong 1 time in 10 lowers them to about 1 in 2 or less.
- **Smaller gains.** Two of the three changes aim at causes that, by the records, could give at most about 11 to 19 points. A real gain of that size will usually read "not shown": that would say the change did not meet the owner's target, not that it does not help.
- **Time.** New work arrives at its own pace. At the pace of the Service Desk, the one project still producing all three kinds, built work would take about 7 to 15 weeks, plans 21 to 42, research 32 to 64; all three one after another, the order the owner ruled, about 60 to 121 weeks (one to over two years); 32 to 64 had all three collected from the start, which the owner did not rule (ruling 4). It would be faster only if the other projects resume, and since only public repositories' pieces enter (ruling 9), the figures given for that case overstate. Plans and built work cannot start at all until each new piece's brief, starting point and model are recorded when it is dispatched, which when counted happened only for the Service Desk's scripted research; the owner ruled that the Chief of Staff records them at hand-out, and Model-Lab's brief records gain a starting-point field, `start:` (ruling 5).
- **Cost.** About 730 runs and 130 million tokens for all three at 60 pieces. Those figures count runs only: the sessions' own turns (for option A about 540 to 1,090 wakes, and 60 to 121 weekly landing reviews) are outside them and outside the budget, and are not estimated. The owner set the budget, the size and the order at the Build gate: option A, 60 pairs of each kind, built work, then plans, then research, 732 runs and about 132 million tokens (decisions/questions/P-001-build-gate.md); and ruled that the project document's lines saying "the same past tasks" be reworded (rulings 1 and 2).
- **Your part in running it.** You create the private repository once, put in it one harmless file holding a word you choose and tell only the secret check's writer, and check the repository is not added to every new session. For each kind you start three sessions (the work's, the one that writes the secret check, the one that judges), and pass short fixed-form records from the secret side to the Chief of Staff: the sheet's, one at each of the judging side's checkpoints, and the closing one. The sessions are meant to wake themselves on a schedule, which is checked the first time; if they cannot, or if a session cannot be resumed after its machine is reclaimed, your part grows by one to four messages or new sessions a week for every week a kind runs: for option A, up to 240 to 484 wake messages in all if no session can wake itself, or 243 to 487 new sessions if none can be resumed ("The owner's actions per option"). Your token checkpoints are split between the work side and the judging side, at most 12 instead of 13 for plans or built work at 60 pieces, 9 instead of 10 for research. The runs and tokens do not change.
- **Ruled before the freeze, and one likely limit.** You ruled one judging session per kind (9 sessions for you to start under option A, against about 336 with one per judging) and that only public repositories' pieces enter, so no whole copy of a private repository's work is kept in public Model-Lab (rulings 8 and 9). If the first check shows that the Orchestrator's side can read the secret sessions' events, which is likely since the Chief of Staff's session holds a tool that reads other sessions' transcripts, then the secret check is kept from the side that writes your cards and the gate case by instruction alone, unless you change that access or stop the test (K1).

## The design

### Why new work (U2's fallback) and a held-back check (U3's fallback)
- The replay check sent every kind to the fallback: no plan has a recorded brief, the rebuilt built-work replay failed, and research cannot be replayed without the web (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md, "Outcome per kind"). So the test compares new work with and without each change over the same period (decisions/questions/P-001-open-unknowns.md, U2).
- The judge-consistency check was not run (decisions/questions/P-001-e002-not-tested.md). So a held-back set of checks the work never sees, and a sample the owner looks at, decide; the record states how far the checks can be trusted (U3).

### The candidate changes (one per kind)
Chosen from the count's causes. Of 84 classified returns with findings, the records name the check 10 times, material handed over 8, instructions 1, split of work 1, source access 1, the worker's own slip 2, and nothing beyond the fault 61; 41 more have no record of findings (research/Q-008-memo.md, bottom lines 3–4, §5). The text of each change, its reasons and its removal test are in experiments/E-003/C1 to C3. These, and this spec, are withheld from the Check author (AC4).

| Change | Kind | What changes | Cause it aims at | Why this one | Most it could reach, by the records |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Plans | The writer reads the role file of the agent that will check its plan (35 words added to the brief) | Instructions: what the writer is given to read | Plans pass first time least often (about 5%); 16 of 18 plan returns with findings were faults internal to the work with the material available; the check's standard is in no writer's pack | Large: about 95% of plans fail first time; whether reading the standard prevents those faults is what the test asks |
| C2 | Built work | The builder is handed, by name, the spec section, rulings, paths and locked checks its task rests on (two sentences: 10 fixed words plus a list the harness fills, 11 counting the list as one word) | Material handed over | The largest named cause for built work (5 of 16 returns with findings); long inputs lower accuracy (Q-002 F-c) | About 11 to 19 points: 3 to 5 of 16 classified returns, with 60% failing first time; 2 of the 5 came from inputs that changed mid-round, which the test leaves out |
| C3 | Research | The source check returns a memo only for a fault it can show, not for a source it could not open (64 words added to the check's brief) | The check itself | The largest named cause for research (7 of 14); two of those, and the one source-access return, came from sources that could not be opened | About 11 to 16 points: 2 or 3 of 19 memos |

Why so few, and why not the others:
- **One change per kind** keeps each kind's test a single comparison at 1 in 40 (V1); a second change on the same kind would add 2 runs per piece and a second chance of keeping a change by luck.
- **Split of work** (1 return) and **the AI model** (named by no record; changing it is out of scope, docs/PROJECT.md section 1) are not tested.
- **A mechanised check** (pre-mortem R6) is not among the three, since each change here is text applied in a copy (docs/PROJECT.md section 4); it is a candidate for a later registration.
- **C1 and C2 each add one reading and nothing else** (round 1's C2 also added a rule for disagreeing inputs; it is dropped). **C3 changes one rule of the check**, what counts as a fault, which is the change itself. Each is under 70 words, can be removed cleanly, and carries its own removal test (pre-mortem R6).

### What the test can and cannot show
- **It is a test of the owner's target.** It is sized for a 20-point gain (V1). For a real gain of 10 points, 60 pairs show it about 1 time in 2 if the change only ever helps, and about 1 time in 3 to 1 time in 7 otherwise (experiments/E-003/sizing.py). C2 and C3 aim at causes that, by the records, can give at most about 11 to 19 and 11 to 16 points (table above). So "not shown" for C2 or C3 will be the usual result even if the change helps by as much as its cause allows. It means the change did not meet the owner's 20-point target; it does not mean the change does no good. The owner ruled that research is tested, last, with this stated (ruling 3).
- **The measure is a stand-in.** The deciding pass is the held-back check's, judged on a different model, not the first-time pass at the project's real check that V1 names. The real round-1 verdict is reported beside it; how closely the two agree is reported, not tested.
- **Judge noise is not in the sizing above.** Judge consistency was never measured. If the judge got one verdict in ten wrong at random, 60 pairs would see a real 20-point gain only about 1 time in 2 at best (0.55, 0.45 and 0.34 to 0.47 under the three assumptions; sizing.py). The owner's cards catch a judge that is often wrong, not one that is sometimes wrong.
- **Same provider, same standard.** See "What this cannot rule out" below.

### How each piece is compared
- **What counts as a new piece:** one piece of work put to its independent check (research/Q-008-memo.md §1.1), dispatched after its kind's comparison opens, in a participating project whose repository is public (ruling 9), not part of this test, and on record at dispatch: for plans and built work the worker's brief, start commit and AI model; for research the memo's submission and the **source check's** brief, start commit and model, since C3 changes the check's brief (E-003, Method step 2).
- **Which projects:** every project of the owner's in a public repository whose work goes to an independent check while the test runs (ruling 9). Today that is the Service Desk, whose scripted research already records the source check's brief, model and start commit in its control files; and Model-Lab, except this test's own pieces, whose brief records (docs/briefs/) carried the brief text and the model but no start commit when counted (29 of 29 had a model line, none a start-commit line), so none of its pieces was eligible then, and which gain the starting-point field `start:` (ruling 5). The operating model repository, which has had no commit since 24 October (experiments/E-003/output.txt, arrivals parts 3 and 4), is private, so its pieces do not enter (ruling 9).
- **Recording is a precondition for plans and built work.** None of the Service Desk's 6 plans and 8 built series of the last two weeks has any record of its brief at dispatch, and Model-Lab's records lack the start commit (experiments/E-003/output.txt, arrivals part 4). The owner ruled that the Chief of Staff records each one at hand-out until the brief-and-model record (M3) does (ruling 5); a plan or built piece without that record cannot enter.
- **How a piece is assigned:** every eligible piece is done both ways. I weighed two ways:

  | | Each new piece done both ways, in side copies (chosen) | Each new piece assigned at random to one way |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pieces needed for a 20-point gain at 1 in 40, about 80% power | about 60 per kind (below) | about 94 plans, 174 research memos, 190 built pieces (two groups; normal approximation) |
  | Runs per piece | 4 (2 doers, 2 judges); research 3 | 2 if run as side copies (1 doer, 1 judge); 1 if the live work takes the change |
  | Fairness | Each piece is its own comparison: same task, start, inputs, model and hour | Balanced only on average; tasks differ between the groups |
  | Live work | Untouched | Half the live work would run on an untested change, which section 4 rules out until M5 |

  Done both ways needs fewer pieces, and pieces are what is scarce (below); it is the fairer comparison; and it leaves the real work alone. Its cost is the extra runs per piece.
- **Same period, model and inputs (pre-mortem R5):** both arms start from the piece's own start commit (for research, the check's), in fresh copies, with the same brief apart from the change text, on the same model, within the same hour and within 7 days of the real dispatch. A change of served model splits the analysis (E-003, Exclusions 7).
- **Inputs that change mid-round** (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md, finding 8): a piece whose named inputs change, or whose real work takes in later commits, between its start and its real round-1 submission is left out, decided mechanically from git before judging (E-003, Exclusions 1).
- **Research** is compared on the check, not the work (C3): the real memo is checked twice, with and without the change, so no research doer runs are needed.

### Size, runs and time
**Method.** For each kind, the one-sided exact test on the pairs where the two arms differ, at 1 in 40 (V1). How often the two arms differ is not known from any record, so power is given under three assumptions: one-way (the change only ever helps), the library's middle assumption (25% of pairs favour the change, 5% the other way; portfolio library Q-003-F-i), and independent arms from each kind's starting figure (plans 5%, research 26%, built work 40%; docs/PROJECT.md section 6). The working is in experiments/E-003/sizing.py; it gives the library's "10 discordant pairs need 9, 20 need 15", and 0.80 at 60 pairs under the middle assumption.

**What counts as one run:** one helper session started for the test, whatever it does; a session that errors counts. Per pair: plans and built work 4 runs, research 3; plus 1 run per kind for its held-back check, and a 10% reserve for reruns (E-003, Sample). **Price:** E-001 used 2,526,069 tokens over 14 runs, about 180,000 a run (src/E-001/RESULT.md, Step 2: cost). By kind of run, E-001's plan and built doers averaged about 239,000 and its judges about 160,000, so a plan or built pair may cost about 11% more than the flat price below; research runs were cut short in E-001, so their cost is the least known. The work, sheet and judging sessions themselves are not runs; the reserve is split between the work side and the judging side, with the same totals (E-003, Sample).

**How fast new work arrives.** `python3 experiments/E-003/arrivals.py` derives it from the records, and anyone can re-run it; its full output, and sizing.py's, as run for S-001's round-3 draft on 2026-11-04, is experiments/E-003/output.txt.

### The sessions, and what each holds
The owner ruled that the secret marking sheets (the held-back checks) are kept in a new private repository, **the sheets repository**, given only to the sessions that write and judge them, never to the sessions that do or start the work, and that the sheet writer and the judges run in their own sessions, started with that repository and never with the work's (decisions/questions/P-001-heldback-store.md; decisions/questions/P-001-plan-stop-2.md). This spec reads "each judging in their own sessions" as: each kind's judging in a session of its own, in which each judge is a helper session of its own, as each run already is (E-003, Sample); the owner ruled that reading at the Build gate (ruling 8). The design rests on these records only:
- a session's repositories are chosen when it starts (intake/P-001-environment-2026-11-04.md:21);
- the helpers a session starts share its machine, so they can read every repository it holds (intake, :22 and :26);
- a helper's transcript is stored in the starting session's folder as `subagents/agent-<id>.jsonl`, where that session can read it (src/E-001/RESULT.md:9, :299), and a top-level session can start helpers on a chosen model and set each one's working directory with a one-file hook (src/E-001/RESULT.md:158, :166-175);
- a session started with the remote `create_session` runs in its own container and can see neither the starting container's files nor its helpers' transcripts (src/E-001/RESULT.md:11);
- Model-Lab and Service-Desk are public (intake, :8-9), and the owner ruled that Model-Lab stays public with records from the owner's repositories in it (decisions/questions/P-001-model-lab-public.md:1, :10);
- a session's machine is reclaimed after a period of inactivity, and what it keeps only in its own storage is lost (intake, :28).

### Lessons from the replay run
| Lesson (reviews/E-001/reviewer.md) | How this test handles it |
|---|---|
| A judge ended its turn waiting on background tests, with no verdict (finding 4) | The judge brief bars background tasks and asks for the verdict line last; one fixed follow-up message, then one new judge session; then the pair is left out and listed (E-003, Sample, reruns) |

## Risks and mitigations
- **Too slow (new):** pieces arrive at the owner's projects' pace; at the Service Desk's pace plans could take 21 to 42 weeks and research 32 to 64 at 60 pairs, and plans and built work cannot start until recording at dispatch exists. Mitigation: built work first; review points with power stated, at which the owner may stop a kind on counts alone; the owner ruled 60 pairs, one kind after another, research last, and recording at dispatch by the Chief of Staff (rulings 3 to 5).
- **Too small (R2):** under independent arms, 60 pairs see a real 20-point gain only about half the time for research and built work, less with judge noise, and a gain below 20 points usually not at all. Mitigation: the power under each assumption, with noise and for a 10-point gain, is stated before the budget is set; a positive gain that does not clear is reported as "not shown", with its range, and read as "did not meet the target", not "does not help".
- **Fooled or biased checks (R3):** see "What this cannot rule out". Mitigation: held-back checks written without sight of the changes, blind judging, a different model, built work's locked checks by script, the owner's cards from all three groups with a confirmation rule.
- **Work shaped to the check (C1):** C1's gain may be teaching to the check. Mitigation: the owner's cards ask whether the work should have gone back; V2 re-checks in real use.
- **Changes fitted to past tasks (R4):** the changes were chosen from past records and are tested only on new work, which none of them was written from.
- **Model or setting moves (R5):** both arms always share model and start; a model change splits the analysis; kept changes carry a re-test on a new model.
- **Rule growth (R6):** C1 and C2 each add one reading only; C3 changes one rule of the check; each is under 70 words and carries a removal test.
- **Owner's time:** 8 cards per kind, about 40 minutes, 12 at most; review-point rulings on counts only.
- **A side copy reaches real work:** no arm's output is merged, pushed or copied into any work repository; it leaves the work session only as its neutral export, in Model-Lab (E-003, Method step 3); the harness has no push rights to the work repositories.
- **Harness complexity:** the Builder must start sessions in copies with their own instructions file, which E-001 did with a working-directory hook (src/E-001/RESULT.md:166-175); the arms' context check catches what it misses.
- **The secret side's set-up fails (new):** a first-use check fails (K1 to K7), or a session is lost to a reclaim, which with wakes days apart is the expected case. Mitigation: each check runs before the run it guards, and K1's probe before any sheet exists; each failure has a named fallback, and those that weaken a guarantee or multiply the owner's actions go to the owner as a question; no session keeps anything on its machine between turns, and the judging lead pushes each judgement before the next, so a lost judging session costs at most one judgement and a lost sheet session one sheet (1 run).
- **A sheet reaches the work (new):** through a session that holds the sheets repository against the design, an attached repository, or a record carrying its text. Mitigation: K1's listing and probe before the first sheet and at every work session's start and review point, and K6; only the fixed-form blocks cross, and J5's test refuses any other text; a found reach stops the kind for the owner's ruling.
